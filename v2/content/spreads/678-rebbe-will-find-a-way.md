Jewish children were waiting to come. But now, one neighbor’s objection could stop everything!

Tatty traveled to the Ohel and wrote a long letter to the Rebbe. Please give us a bracha to receive the permit easily, without any trouble, Tatty davened.

Arriving back home, Tatty waited anxiously to find out when the public hearing would take place. Every time he got an email, he hurried to check it. But no updates came.

A new law called SB-234 was just passed in California, he wrote. It changes the rules for home daycares. Under the new law, you will no longer need special zoning to make your daycare larger!

“Guess what?” he called to Mommy. “We won’t need a public hearing!”

Tatty submitted a new application, and everything went smoothly. There were no letters to the neighbors, no objections, and no hearing. Baruch Hashem, the permit was approved, and our preschool was able to welcome more children.

Tatty had asked the Rebbe for a bracha to receive the permit easily and without any trouble. The answer came in an incredible way: the law itself had changed!

Our preschool was ready to grow, but a public hearing could stop everything.

Inspectors swept through our house, opening doors and checking every room. Tatty followed close behind, waiting nervously for their decision.

“Congratulations,” he said. “Your home passed the inspection. You have permission to open your preschool! For the first year, you may have eight students.”

Tatty and Mommy were thrilled. Soon, our home was filled with the happy sounds of children playing, learning alef-beis, and singing the words of davening.

The next year, Tatty and Mommy wanted to accept more kids into the preschool. But

there was a challenge: They would need a new permit.

Tatty began filling out the forms, but it got complicated.

“Since you want more than eight children, your preschool will no longer be considered a small home daycare,” a worker explained. “To have a larger daycare, you will need to change the zoning of your house. That takes special permission.”

Then she added, “We’ll send letters to all of your neighbors telling them about your plans. If anyone objects and says they don’t want a large preschool on your block, there will be a public court hearing to decide if you should get the new permit or not.”

Tatty’s heart sank. At a public hearing, people could try convincing the city not to let our preschool grow.

Still, there was no other way to receive permission. Tatty gave in the forms and hoped for the best.
