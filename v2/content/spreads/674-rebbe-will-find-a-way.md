The chavrusa sat there, his heart pounding. What would they say?

The FBI investigation was turning the man’s life upside-down.

Our rav, Rabbi Baruch Epstein, was at a restaurant one day when a Litvisher Yid came over to him.

“Are you a Lubavitcher?” the man asked. “I must share a story with you about the Rebbe!”

He began telling Rabbi Epstein about a close friend who had been his chavrusa for many years.

It happened one day that this chavrusa discovered some alarming news: He found out that he was being investigated by the FBI. He became very worried and afraid. The FBI is a powerful government agency, and if they decided that he had done something wrong, he could face huge fines or even be sent to prison!

The worry followed him everywhere. At night, he lay awake, thinking about what might happen. And during the day, he couldn’t bring himself to relax. Would he lose all his money? Would he get into even bigger trouble?

The stress hurt his health, his parnassa, and his shalom bayis. He had so much heartache that he even stopped learning with his long-time chavrusa.

“It was so hard to see him suffer,” the man told Rabbi Epstein.

Soon after they had stopped learning together, he traveled to New York to meet with a lawyer. The day before the meeting, he met an old friend and told him about his crisis with the FBI.

“You’re in New York!” the friend said. “Everyone knows that when a Yid has a problem, he goes to the Rebbe’s Ohel to ask for a bracha. You should go, too.”

At first, the chavrusa refused. He was Litvish and didn’t feel comfortable going to the Ohel.
