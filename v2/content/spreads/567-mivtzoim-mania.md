All of a sudden, there was a knock on the classroom door. “That’s unusual,” I thought to myself. “Who could that be, in the middle of class?” I had my answer a moment later. It was Rabbi Wolf, the school administrator. All thoughts of my Purim costume vanished from my mind. I couldn’t wait to hear what he had to say. “Talmidim,” he began, “As you all know, Pesach is coming up in a few weeks. I’m sure your parents are already busy cleaning, buying groceries, and ordering matzah and wine. What you may not know is that sadly, there are some families in our community who aren’t able to buy the things they need for Pesach.

“That’s terrible,” I thought to myself. “I wish I could help! But how?”

As if he read my thoughts, Rabbi Wolf continued: “That’s where you come in, dear talmidim. We Yidden always help each other out—especially when it comes to Pesach. We raise money called Ma’os Chitim, so that every single Yid has what they need for Yom Tov. The Rebbe spoke about this important mitzvah every year, and strongly encouraged us to help however possible.”

“To that end,” Rabbi Wolf said, “We are giving each one of you a pushka. Right now, it’s empty. Your job is to fill it! Ask your family and friends, and anyone else you know, to give generously for Ma’os Chitim. When your pushka is full, count your total, and bring it back to school. We will be hanging a giant Pushka in the hallway. Empty your pushka into the big pushka, and watch as the entire school works together to fill it up!”

Full of excitement, I picked up my pushka from the front of the classroom. As soon as I got home, I came up with a plan. I won’t wait around for my family members to fill up my pushka. I’ll go up and down my block, and knock on every single door with a mezuzah. Surely, they’ll all be happy to take part in this incredible z’chus.

Door to door I went, and sure enough, everyone was thrilled to donate. It was actually lots of fun! I met a bunch of new people. One lady even asked for my home phone number, so she could call my parents and tell them how impressed she was. People were amazed that a young boy like me had the confidence to knock on a stranger’s door just to help others, and they appreciated the clear explanation I gave about this mitzvah. (Thanks, Rabbi Wolf!)

One day, I knocked on the door of a Russian family. Turns out they had just moved to town, and were growing in their Yiddishkeit, but weren’t yet frum. I launched into my fundraising pitch about money for Pesach when the mother interrupted me.

“I don’t know what we’re going to do about Pesach!” she sighed. “We’ve been learning all about it for the first time,

and we really want to try doing it right. But … to buy all new dishes? Even just the matzah and wine … it’s so expensive!”

I couldn’t believe the Hashgacha Pratis. This is exactly what I was raising money for! With a smile on my face, I said, “I know someone who can help.” I explained our project, and gave her Rabbi Wolf’s number. “Give him a call,” I told her. “He’ll take care of everything.”

Well, she called him, and Baruch Hashem, she received a check from the Ma’os Chitim fund. After Pesach, she called him back, and told him about the beautiful Seder they had made. “We even invited some of our non-religious friends, and we were all inspired and uplifted together. It’s really thanks to the incredible work of your students,” she said with tears in her eyes.

I am so proud of my school for having this wonderful project which helps so many Yidden. Last year, we raised ten thousand dollars! And as for me, I got the biggest gift in the world: the incredible feeling that I helped another Yid.

I’m sure there are families in your community who need help for Pesach, too. How can you raise Ma’os Chitim for them? Start collecting today!
