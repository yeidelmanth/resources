<p class="lead">A huge payment for camp was due right away. Where would the money come from?</p>

Before we moved on shlichus to Melbourne, Australia, Tatty gave shiurim at Chabad of the Upper East Side in Manhattan.

At Tatty’s final shiur before our move, he made a small farbrengen to say goodbye. Afterward, the shliach, Rabbi Krasnianski, pulled Tatty aside.

“One of my donors is inspired that you’re moving so far away on shlichus,” he said. “He decided to send your family a monthly donation for the next few years.”

Tatty felt uncomfortable. “But I don’t want to take donations away from your Chabad House,” he protested.

Rabbi Krasnianski smiled. “The more a Yid gives tzedaka, the better. Helping another shliach is a wonderful thing. You should accept it.”

Sure enough, after we moved to Melbourne, the donations began arriving each month. But after a few months, they suddenly stopped.

Tatty told Rabbi Krasnianski, who was surprised. “This donor always keeps his word,” he said. “It doesn’t make sense that he would stop the payments.”

Still, Tatty decided not to ask the donor about it.

Six years passed, and we were busy with our shlichus. Tatty worked with Rabbi Moshe Kahn at Chabad Youth, organizing programs for Jewish children, teenagers, and young adults.

One of the biggest programs that they run is Camp Gan Yisroel. Ever since the camp was founded more than fifty years earlier, it had never had its own campsite. Year after year, they had to keep renting different properties, which brought a lot of stress and challenges.

Then, after a huge fundraising campaign and many open miracles, they finally bought their very own campgrounds!

But the new property needed a lot of construction work before camp could start, and money was very tight.

One day, Tatty got a surprising phone call from Manhattan.

“Hello,” the man on the phone said. “I’m the donor’s accountant. We just discovered that for the past six years, your monthly donations were being set aside, but they never reached you. We’ll send you the full amount right away.”

Tatty couldn’t believe his ears. “How much is it?”

Tatty’s eyes widened. Right away, he remembered Rabbi Krasnianski’s words from six years earlier about helping other shluchim through tzedaka.

Without hesitation, he decided to donate the entire amount to Chabad Youth.

<figure class="handimg inline"><img src="https://mashpia.com/resources/assets/img/245270837fdbdc16.png" alt="handwritten note"></figure>

Tatty hurried to Rabbi Kahn’s office to tell him the exciting news.

As soon as Rabbi Kahn heard the amount, his eyes filled with tears.

“You have no idea what just happened,” he said emotionally. “This morning, I needed to pay $60,000 to continue fixing up the campgrounds, but I didn’t have the money. I felt so overwhelmed, and wrote a letter to the Rebbe asking for a bracha. And now, here you are giving me that exact amount!”

The Rebbe’s bracha had come in the quickest, most exact way possible. And it didn’t end there! Soon afterward, another donor offered to cover the remaining cost of the new campsite, and Chabad Youth’s financial situation got a lot better.

Seeing such an open miracle gave everyone tremendous chayus and bitachon. The “lost” money had arrived just when it was needed most. And now, every summer, the beautiful new campsite is filled with hundreds of children growing in Torah and mitzvos!
