The doctors had no hope of saving my eye. We needed a miracle.

I was just four years old when a metal arrow hit my eye on Lag Ba’Omer. Mommy quickly called for help, while Tatty immediately contacted someone at the Ohel to ask the Rebbe for a bracha.

At the hospital, the doctors were shocked at what they saw. “It’s a miracle that nothing worse happened,” they told us, “but the eye is badly damaged.”

I was rushed into surgery, and Tatty wrote a detailed letter to the Rebbe about what was going on. He also put tefillin on a paramedic whom he had found out was Jewish.

The surgeon tried her best, but did not think the eye could be saved. Over the next few days, we flew from one eye specialist to the next. They all told us the same thing: “The eye is terribly damaged, to the point that it can’t see light. We don’t think that there’s anything we can do to save it. We suggest that you have the eye removed.”

Tatty and Mommy were told that they must decide quickly whether or not they’ll follow the doctors’ suggestion.

Understanding how serious the situation was, Tatty and I traveled to the Ohel where we wrote to the Rebbe and asked for a miracle.

Meanwhile, at home, Mommy gazed at a picture of the Rebbe, asking for a bracha as well.

“We need clarity about whether we should remove Shmuly’s eye or not,” she said out loud.

A moment later, and at the same time that Tatty and I were in the Ohel, Mommy’s phone buzzed.

“I heard about what happened to your son,” the message read. “My name is Asher, and when I was four years old, I also had a serious eye injury. The Rebbe told us not to remove my eye and gave us a dollar for a refuah shleima. I will send you the dollar now. I hope it will bring you the Rebbe’s bracha.”

Mommy looked at the text message in amazement. The Rebbe had sent an answer— from someone they had never met before!

The next day, Tatty and I traveled to a top doctor in California, hoping that he would agree to operate on my eye. But the doctor refused, saying that if the eye wasn’t seeing any light, even surgery wouldn’t help.

Suddenly, while at the doctor’s office, a miracle happened: My injured eye began to see light! Now, the doctor agreed to operate.
