Devorah Leah settled into her aunt and uncle’s house fairly easily. She kept busy with her own sewing and learning, of course, as well as by helping Freida with her numerous projects. Freida was always busy with one form of tzedaka or another. In the mornings, it was cooking and delivering food baskets for poor families, in the afternoons, it was collecting clothes for poor children, and in the evenings, it was collecting money for poor kallos.

Baruch, on the other hand, just seemed to be getting more and more restless. He wandered the forests around Vitebsk during the day, and slept in the beis medrash most nights.

He rarely came back to Reb Kaddish and Frieda’s house at all. And even then, it was usually just

on Shabbos, to hear kiddush and have lechem mishna, after which he would immediately leave once again.

So it was a surprise to Reb Kaddish, Freida, and Devorah Leah when Baruch appeared in the kitchen one ordinary Monday morning.
