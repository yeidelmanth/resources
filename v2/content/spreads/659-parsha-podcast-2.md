chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

We have a few options of who to call this week. Hey, guess what? One of them is Mommy’s great-great-great- grandmother, Bubby Basya!

Mommy told me about Elter-Bubby Basya. She lived almost 170 years ago, and she was famous for her challah. The recipe has been passed down to all the mommies in our family, and Mommy still uses it today! I always wished I could have met her!

Yay, this is so exciting! Your wish will finally be granted!

Hi Elter-Elter-Elter-Elter Bubby! I heard that you made the best challah in the world!

Oh, I know! You’re talking about the mitzva of hafrashas challah!

Exactly, zeeskeit. The Torah tells us that “ֵרֵאִׁשׁית ֲעֲִרִֹסֵֹתֵֶכֶם ַח ַָלָה ָּתִּרִימּו ְתְרּוָמָה— The first portion of the dough is given as a present to Hashem.”

Why does Hashem want a piece of dough as a present?

Oh Chaim’ke, it’s much more than just this piece! The Torah uses the term, “ָּתִּרִימּו ”ְתְרּוָמָה, which really means “you shall lift it up as a gift.” With this little pitzele of dough, we elevate the entire batch of challah, making it heilig for Hashem!

Wow, I knew that taking challah is a mitzvah, but I never knew that it affected the whole batch of dough!

Of course, mein kind. And you know, there is a lesson here that applies to much more than just delicious challah dough!

Mommy always says that bubbies give the best advice. Tell us, what lesson can we learn from challah dough?

Well, bread represents all that we need in order to live. Oy, I remember that during the war, we barely even had a crust of black bread … but that’s for another conversation. Anyway, Hashem tells us to set aside the first of our dough for Him. This does not just mean challah dough; it means that the first part of everything we have is given to Hashem. And as a result, the rest of our possessions become heilig!

Just like by saying Modeh Ani, we give the first part of our day for Hashem…

Oh, and all of my books say, “לַה' הָאָרֶץ ּומְלֹואָּה—To Hashem belongs the earth and all that fills it.” So, everything I read is also all for Hashem!

Ooh, and when I give Hashem the first moments of my meal—by making a bracha—then the whole rest of the meal becomes heilig, too! I will make sure to say a bracha before eating my pizza and salad and muffins today!

That’s gevaldik, yes! By making a bracha, sitting and eating nicely, and then using the energy to learn more Torah.

Thanks, Bubby. This will definitely help me do the right thing. Next time my Yetzer Hara starts bugging me to want a milkshake after having chicken soup, I know just what to answer! “You, Yetzer Hara, are a silly fool, saying utter nonsense! I already gave the first part of my day to Hashem, so my entire day is holy! Stop trying to mess up my plans.”

Oy, kinderlach, I’m shepping such nachas! Kein ayin hara, you are all as sharp and zees!

And now, let’s ask all the other chayolim to think about it: How will you start your day so that you lift the rest of it up for Hashem?
