chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Okay, guys, Pesach is coming! We’d better record the episode before things get too busy with Yom Tov preparations.

What’s wrong, Chaim? You’ve been really quiet since yesterday.

I know; it’s your friend Levi, right? You went to visit him in the hospital yesterday. His sister Esti told me that he’s going to be there for the entire Yom Tov.

My class said Tehilim for him at our Yud-Alef Nissan farbrengen. How is he doing, Chaim?

Oy, this Golus is much too long. It was so hard to see him attached to so many machines. How will I concentrate on the Haggada this Pesach? How can I say Hallel happily when I know that there are so many sick people in the hospital?

I was thinking about that, too! I saw a paper in the kitchen earlier about how lots of families don’t have enough money to buy wine or matzah! That’s awful! How can I focus on Yom Tov with all my heart when so many people don’t have what they need?

Hello, chayolim! I was just learning with Rabi Eliezer, Rabi Yehoshua, Rabi Elazar ben Azarya and Rabi Tarfon.

Wow, the very chachamim who we read about in the Haggada— famous for staying up all night talking about the Pesach story.

We’re in the middle of discussing the most wondrous nissim ever. Join us!

Hold on; your expressions don’t reflect the simcha of this special Yom Tov. Tell me, why the gloomy faces?

Rabi Akiva, this Golus is just so long and so hard!

Ah, my children. When you’re at your Seder and you look outside, what do you see?

That’s right. We sit, every dark Pesach evening, telling the story of Yetzias Mitzrayim. I’m sure you kids know that it’s a mitzvah to discuss the story of our freedom all through the night, right?

Well, did you also know that nighttime is a symbol of golus? The Haggada is referring here to a ruchniyus’dike darkness.

Ruchniyus’dike darkness … is that talking about our lives now? When Chaim’s friend is in the hospital and some families can’t afford food for Pesach?

Exactly. This “night” mentioned in the Haggada is our current Golus which has lasted nearly two thousand years. Now, chayolim, have you ever learned the passuk, “ּכִימֵי צֵאתְָך מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם אַרְאֶנּו נִפְלָאֹות”?

Hey, yeah. I think that’s what’s written on the cover of my Haggada! The one I decorated with sequins and flowers.

You’re right, Mimi; it’s the same one. “Like the days that I took you out of Mitzrayim, I will show you wonders. It means that the final Geulah will be similar to Yetzias Mitzrayim.

That’s right, Chaim. And think about it: Yetzias Mitzrayim brought our nation one giant step closer to Moshiach.
