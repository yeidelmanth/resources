The manager at Barnes and Noble Booksellers led me to the nearest phone.

Senator want to talk to me? And why did he sound upset?

“Good luck, kid.” The manager pushed the flashing line 1 button, and handed me the phone.

“Sruli, I heard amazing things about the Jewish Book Day! It sounds like a smashing success. But I’m here with my wife, and she wants to get involved as well! Can you help me?”

My mind started racing. Senator Goldberg had a real predicament on his hands! How could we involve his wife? What campaign would she want to be a part of?

But then, I had a lightbulb moment. Or–to be more accurate–a candle moment. Neshek, of course!

“Mr. Senator, I have just the thing for your wife! The Rebbe wants every Jewish woman and girl to light Shabbos candles. Each Friday, girls take to the streets to hand out little boxes

“I think I have just the person for your wife to team up with!” I offered, and I gave him my home phone number so she could call Mussi. This way, I could still stay involved with my BRIGHT ideas.

I hung up and looked around. I was back in the manager’s office at Barnes and Nobles. And the manager was looking at me strangely. “What was that about?” he asked.

“Oh, just another campaign we’re starting. To add more light to the world.”

“Add more light to the world, you say? You see, here, with the invention of e-books and all that, Barnes and Nobles hasn’t been doing as well as it used to be. We thought of funding a charity as a part of our new marketing campaign ‘Barnes and Nobles Acts Noble.’ And I think that adding more light to the world is just the thing!”

And so, with a check for $40,000 in hand, I raced home to tell Mussi the news. I barreled through the front door and stopped short. Standing in front of me, holding the phone, was a grinning Mussi herself.

“I just got off the phone with the Senator's wife, Claudia,” she laughed. “At first, I was sure it was a prank call, but then I remembered that with a brother like you, anything is possible! She’s coming over in twenty minutes! Please help me clean up!”

Just as Mommy’s freshly baked cookies were coming out of the oven, we heard the doorbell ring. The table was set with our china teapot and teacups, and Mussi was wearing her favorite headband. It was almost like Shabbos was coming!

I took my place under the couch so I could listen in to the conversation. Obviously.

“We want every single Jewish woman and girl to light Shabbos candles,” Mussi explained. “That means that everyone needs to find out about it, and they all need to receive candles. We need to get the world glowing!”

The Senator’s wife and Mussi were deep in thought, trying to think of something, when the idea popped into my brain. How could I hint it to Mussi? I felt Leibel’s toy airplane with my foot, suddenly so grateful that I hadn’t had time to put it away earlier and had just stuffed it under the couch. I quietly and slowly slid it out to where Mussi could see it.

“Hmm…” Mussi said nonchalantly. “What do you think of advertising the campaign on an airplane banner? Like the airplanes that write messages in the sky? We’ll call it ‘Light Up the World With Shabbos Candles!’”

“Yes, yes!” exclaimed the Senator's wife, getting into it. “And everyone needs to send in a picture of themselves right before they light, dressed in their Shabbos finest! And we’ll raffle all the pictures and the

winner will get to go on a helicopter with me! I’ve always wanted an excuse for a tour of New York City,” she added with a wink.

“And we can drop neshek from the sky so everyone in the entire world will have access!” I exclaimed. Whoops. I blew my cover.

By the next day, the graphics were in for the new neshek boxes, volunteers all over the world were getting ready to pack away, and aerial advertising companies from every continent were already paid. A few days later and we had 47,000 pictures of people ready to light Shabbos candles in our inbox. What a smashing success!

Tuesday afternoon, I went with Mussi to the Senator’s private helipad to watch his wife and the winning Mrs. Lichtenberg from Denver take off into the sky. My yarmulka would have gone with them if not for my quick thinking!

“Y’know, Sruli,” sniffled the Senator. “That Mrs. Lichtenberg really reminds me of my grandmother, of blessed memory. She also wore a floral dress, just like that one. And always carried babka in her handbag.”
