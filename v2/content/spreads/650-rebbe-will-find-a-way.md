"Then … I woke up! And Baruch Hashem, I got better. Completely better."

He smiled at us, his eyes filling with tears. "That's why I’m coming to New York with you. To thank the Rebbe for saving my life."

The man went to the Ohel and thanked the Rebbe. Then, he asked for a bracha for his daughter, who had been trying to close a business deal for a long time.

Shortly after he left the Ohel, his phone rang. It was his daughter. “Guess what?” she exclaimed, “The deal just closed!”

We spent the whole week together in Crown Heights, and heard many more stories about the Rebbe. Meeting people who actually saw the Rebbe in person was very special for all of us kids. We haven’t had that z’chus yet, so hearing their stories made us feel more connected.

“Usually, when you give something, you get something back. But when the Rebbe gives a bracha, what does he get back?"

The answer was powerful: "Do a mitzvah. Learn more Torah. Help another Yid. That's what makes the Rebbe smile. Those are the best gifts that he can get back."

The trip is over now, but whenever we think about the Rebbe, we think about what we learned: How can we make the Rebbe smile today?

On our trip to the Ohel, we heard many incredible stories about the Rebbe that inspire us to do more mitzvos!

This past Gimmel Tammuz, our family traveled to New York with a group from our city to visit the Rebbe's Ohel.

The ride was long, but we didn't mind. Everyone was sharing stories about how they first heard about the Rebbe, the miracles they'd seen, and the reasons why they joined the trip.

Then, a man whom we didn't know started talking. He wasn't frum yet, but he had a story to tell:

"Back in 2020," he began, "Covid hit my whole family. Every single one of us tested

positive. I'm older, so I got really sick with a high fever. The hospital put me on a ventilator because I couldn’t breathe on my own. I stayed like that for fourteen days."

"Then, something terrible happened," he said quietly. "My heart stopped. For a minute and a half, I had a cardiac arrest."

"That's when everything changed," he said. "First, I heard a loud beep. Then, total silence. Suddenly, I was standing inside a huge cave. Am I going to pass away? I wondered.”

"At the end of the cave, there was a bright, powerful light. And then, suddenly, the Lubavitcher Rebbe was standing right in front of me! Rebbe, I cried, I want to live!”

The whole bus was silent. "And then?" someone asked.
