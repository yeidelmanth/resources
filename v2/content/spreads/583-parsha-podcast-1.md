Hi there! We’re the Levin Family: Basya, Mimi, Chaim and

Yossi—chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

I’m confused, Basya. I thought you, of all people, would like Yom Kippur.

Why would she like Yom Kippur? Nothing fun happens! At least not until the very end when everyone shouts together, and then we blow the shofar, and go home and eat yummy food.

Hmm, how can I explain this … Yossi, can we bring Aharon HaKohen onto the podcast? He’d be perfect for this. Also, if I remember correctly, Basya really admires Aharon HaKohen.

I do! He’s so peaceful, always bringing everyone together. I wish I could be like that.

Ah, the wonderful Parsha Podcast team, working together to bring Moshiach! How may I help you?

Yom Kippur! “אַחַת ּבַּשָׁנָה— the one day of the year!” Basya, aren’t you the one who always wants everyone to get along? This is the “one day” that unites all Yidden as one with Hashem!

Whoa, that was a lot of “ones”. What just happened?

Wait, so Aharon HaKohen, do you mean that on Yom Kippur everyone is united with Hashem? Is that because we all did teshuvah?

Yes! It all begins ten days earlier, on Rosh Hashana, at the start of the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah. All of the Yidden, including all boys and girls of Tzivos Hashem, are working really hard to do teshuvah.

Everyone is begging Hashem to be their King again. Then, at the end of these ten days—Basya,do you know what happens?

Hashem forgives us and agrees to be King again! Yay! It’s like we get a new contract with Hashem for another whole year.

Oh, oh! And then, at the end of Yom Kippur, everyone shouts all together as one!

Aha! Do you know what we all shout? Do you know what it means?

Everyone in shul shouts “Shema Yisroel” together, then “Baruch Shem.” It means that Hashem and everything is One, and Hashem is the King of the whole wide world!

Ooooh, I get it. Because all the Yidden are reunited with Hashem, and Hashem is re-crowned as King, so everyone and everything is united!

I like when everyone is united and getting along well. That makes me happy.

Me too. That’s why I love Yom Kippur. Chayolim, can you all please do a favor for me?

For sure! You want us to bring a karban for you? I could probably figure out how to shecht it!
