Time passed, and Shmuli prepared for another trip to America—this time, to attend yeshiva there. The night before the flight, he met a neighbor from his building. They always said hello to each other, but they never had a real conversation. They talked for a moment, and Shmuli mentioned that he was leaving for yeshiva.

The next day, after landing in New York, Shmuli went straight to the Ohel. He sat down and began writing his tzetel: Rebbe, I have a big challenge that I don’t know how to fix. The airline lost my credit, and now I owe a lot of money. Please give me a clear bracha for this to be solved.

Later that afternoon, when Shmuli called his mother to wish her a good Shabbos, she had surprising news.

“Remember the neighbor whom you spoke to last night?” she asked. “He came by today with a gift for you in honor of your trip to New York. He thought you were still in Brazil and wanted to catch you before you left.”

“Really?” Shmuli asked. “But we hardly even know each other.”

“That’s what makes it so special,” Mommy said. “He just wanted to wish you a safe trip. And you won’t believe what he left for you: An envelope with five hundred dollars!”

Shmuli was amazed. Only a few hours after he had asked the Rebbe for help, the exact amount he needed had arrived in the most unexpected way!

When I heard this story, it reminded me how much the Rebbe is with us. He hears our tefillos, cares about our worries, and finds a way to help—even kids like us!

A missing airline credit left Shmuli owing hundreds of dollars. How would he pay it back?

I once heard an amazing story about a bochur from my city of S. Paulo, Brazil, named Shmuli Labkowski.

Shmuli was learning in yeshiva and wanted very much to travel to the Rebbe for Yud Shvat. He worked hard to get everything ready, and even got an emergency passport at the last minute.

A friend helped him pay for the ticket by lending him the money. Another man offered

to cover the cost, but only if Shmuli went to the Ohel. Shmuli felt confident that with the Rebbe’s brachos, everything would work out.

But the day before his flight, something unexpected happened. He took the Covid test that everyone needed at the time, and it came back positive. That meant he couldn’t fly. He was disappointed, but he knew everything is Hashgacha Pratis.

“You’ll get full credit to use for a later trip,” the airline promised him. That made him feel a bit better, because at least the money wouldn’t be lost.

A few months later, Shmuli tried to use the credit for a trip on Yud-Alef Nissan. But when he called the airline, the agents couldn’t find the credit at all. Suddenly, Shmuli had lost almost five hundred dollars, which is a lot for a yeshiva bochur.
