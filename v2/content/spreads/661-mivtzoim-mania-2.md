For 23 years, our cousin Ari had not been able to say what he was thinking or feeling. He could read, daven, and follow along, but no one knew what was going on inside.

And now, all of a sudden, Hashem had opened up a door of communication, allowing him to finally express himself!

The Liberows were amazed, and Ari himself was just as excited. “Baruch Hashem, I can speak!” he typed, bringing tears of joy to everyone’s eyes.

Ari kept typing, sharing thoughts and ideas that he had never been able to before. His family was amazed to discover how much Torah he knew and how well he understood what he learned.

\*\*\* It was soon Rosh Hashana. Since the phone was muktzah, Ari sat quietly, watching everyone and following along.

Ari began to move his hands and shift in his seat, trying to say something. Everyone could see his frustration, but the words wouldn’t come.

“I have an idea!” said Ari’s older brother. He took out a siddur and opened it up to the Alef- Beis chart.

Ari walked quickly, as if he knew exactly where he was going. Soon, he stopped in front of a house and headed to the door.

He had heard about the woman who lived there, but he had never met her. How would she feel about a shliach knocking on her door? What if she didn’t want visitors?

Still, he took a deep breath and knocked. To his relief, the woman opened the door and welcomed them inside warmly. Rabbi Liberow blew shofar for her and her daughter, helping them fulfill the mitzvah.

Afterward, Rabbi Liberow told them how, after many years, Ari had just begun to communicate. “He asked to go on mivtzoim and help Yidden hear the shofar,” Rabbi Liberow explained. “That’s how we ended up here!”

Later that evening, in shul, Rabbi Liberow told everyone

how proud he was of Ari. And that was that. They all thought it was the end of the story.

A few weeks later, on Hoshana Rabba, a man came over to Rabbi Liberow. “I just heard an incredible mivtzoim story,” he said. “Did it really happen to you and Ari?”

The man looked surprised. “You didn’t hear?” he exclaimed. “Your mivtzoim visit had a huge impact! Apparently, the woman you visited always went to shul on Rosh Hashana. But this year, for the first time in her life, she couldn’t go. “As the afternoon passed, she and her daughter realized that they had no way of hearing the shofar,” the man continued. “‘Hashem,’ they cried tearfully, ‘please show us that You’re with us.’ “Just a few minutes later, there was a knock at the door. He had no idea how much his visit had meant to them!

Later, when more people asked about the story, Rabbi Liberow began to wonder: Was there something deeper about Ari’s request to go on mivtzoim? Had he known that these women needed help?

In that moment, Rabbi Liberow understood: Ari’s special neshama had sensed that this woman was crying to Hashem, asking to be able to fulfill the mitzvah of hearing shofar and Rosh Hashana. And he had made sure to go help her.

All along, he had thought that he was leading Ari on mivtzoim. But now he understood that Ari had been leading him. Chayolim, let’s be like Ari this Rosh Hashana. Take your shofar, go out on mivtzoim, and help another Yid!
