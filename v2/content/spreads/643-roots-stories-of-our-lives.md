And with the help of the Bashefer … he’s remained a good Jew.“When

I first met him, I remember, it was in shul on Shabbos. He was standing in the corner, his tallis down to here—” Eliezer Reuven tapped his eyebrow, “all by himself. And crying, crying,

because he couldn’t follow along, and no one was helping him. I’m sure some tried, but he doesn’t even speak Yiddish. I, on the other hand, because of my work, speak good Polish. So I helped him that day, and found him a teacher to teach him the alef-beis. He also started coming by my shop.

Eliezer Reuven’s face lit up with clear nachas, “He has a melamed teaching his three sons Torah! By now, they must know more than I do. And have you ever seen that big Sefer Torah in shul, the one with the double silver crown? That’s his donation; his and his wife’s.”

LAST TIME: Baruch travels from town to town learning Torah and working hard for his own money. He never accepts help from anyone. When he works for Eliezer Reuven, a blacksmith, Baruch learns to admire the sincerity of Eliezer Reuven and his friends, though they are unlearned. Seder in Dobromysl

Baruch didn’t say much in response, but his face showed it all: He was deeply impressed.

Observing all of this made Baruch willing to stay in Dobromysl longer than any other town so far. He appreciated everything that he learned during his work time. No, it wasn’t Mishna, Gemara, or halacha like he studied in the beis midrash, but there were other lessons—honesty, sincerity, and Ahavas Yisroel—that were no less

important. This, combined with the fact that the snow had made the roads nearly impassable, led Baruch to stay at his job in the smithy for a while.

It was a week before Pesach when Eliezer Reuven turned to Baruch with a question. “You’ll eat with us for the Seder, yes?”

Eliezer Reuven explained to his customers that he was sorry, but the shop was closed due to his holiday. Yes, his Passover only started on Saturday, but he needed all this time to prepare.

Eliezer Reuven spent those three days hard at work, but in a different way. He and his wife, Devorah, and their family worked together to make sure every nook and cranny in their house was scrubbed and cleaned. They whitewashed the walls, swept and mopped the floors, scrubbed the shelves and

closets, and changed the bricks in the oven. By the time the Seder night arrived, the whole house positively gleamed.
