This week’s parsha talks a lot about the kohanim and Kohen Gadol.

Yeah! We should bring Aharon the Kohen Gadol to the podcast again, and ask him more about his job!

Ahhh. I feel so comfortable here. This is a real Yiddishe home!

Well, it’s just our Parsha Podcast Headquarters, but we did make sure to keep a Chumash, siddur, and pushka here!

That must be why. I can see light shining all around here and spreading outwards. Just like the menorah that was lit in the Bais Hamikdash, and the inside-out windows.

The menorah? How are our sefarim and pushka like the menorah?

Menorah? I want to know about those inside-out windows!

You chayolim have the best questions. I’ll get to both of them, with Hashem’s help. Starting with the windows: You see, back in my day, there were no lightbulbs. So windows were shaped in a way that brought the outside light in. Normal windows were narrower on the outside, and wider on the inside.

So then “inside-out windows” would mean windows that are wider on the outside, and narrower on the inside. Hmm. That would bring the inside light to the outdoors.

Exactly. Because the Bais Hamikdash doesn’t need light from outside. It gets filled with its own light from the menorah. And the light of the menorah spreads out to the whole world.

That’s what the menorah was for! To spread the light of the Shechina to the world.

But we don’t have this menorah. So now how do we light up the world?

This room is already quite lit up! I’m sure your bedrooms are too. You made the room into a mini-mikdash when you put a Chumash, siddur, and pushka in it.

Yeah, I learned that a Chumash makes the home a house of Torah when we learn from it. A siddur makes it a house of tefillah when we daven. And a pushka makes it a house of kindness when we give tzedakah!

Yes. And that is why I feel at home here. It’s a place where the words of Torah can be heard, tefillos are felt, and tzedakah is given to the poor and to chassidishe mosdos. It’s a real mini-mikdash!

But we don’t have inside-out windows like the ones in the Bais Hamikdash.

True, not windows that you can see. But these rooms are full of ruchniyus’dik inside-out windows. If someone would step into your home, it would be obvious to them that it’s a true Yiddishe home where Hashem is comfortable to live. They’d feel the kedusha in the air. This feeling of kedusha is like a light that will spread out to your neighbors. Its brightness will continue shining along, farther and farther. In fact, with all of your Tzivos Hashem missions, you will light up the whole world.

Then we’ll bring Moshiach, when we’ll see you light the menorah in the greatest home of all—the Bais Hamikdash! And the whole world will be completely lit up!

So, chayolim, think about it: Where can you begin if you want to light up the whole entire world?
