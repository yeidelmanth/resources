A few days before her trip, Mommy wrote a pan to the Rebbe, pouring out her heart about the Chabad House. Then she made a copy of the Chabad House key and slipped it in her pocket.

When Mommy landed in New York, she went straight to the Ohel. It was late at night and very quiet. She stood there with her pan and the key, and told the Rebbe everything, asking for a clear bracha. Then, she left the key and the pan there, and continued to Crown Heights for the Kinus.

Mommy came home from the Kinus feeling a lot calmer. She knew that the Rebbe would help.

It looked like we would have to sell the Chabad House. Was there a way to save it?

We used to live in a very small house, where everyone shared the same tight spaces. As our family and shlichus grew, challenges got in the way, and we have a special brother who needed more space and support.

Then, we moved into a beautiful, large Chabad House that also served as our home.

For us kids, it felt big and exciting. For Mommy and Tatty, it was a dream come true.

One day, Tatty spoke with Mommy for a long time. “I think we’ll have to sell the Chabad House,” he said quietly. “I don’t know what else to do.”

We didn’t understand all the details, but we understood that something serious was happening.

Mommy didn’t give up. “This Chabad House came with so many brachos,” she told Tatty with bitachon. “I’m sure another bracha will come!”

But still, she also felt worn down. She wasn’t even planning on going to the Kinus Hashluchos that year because she felt too tired.
