<p class="lead">All of Yaakov Avinu’s sons were tzaddikim. They grew up in his home, learned Torah, and behaved as righteous Shevatim should. But if they were all</p>

The answer is that there are different levels in being a tzaddik. Doing the right thing is important— but we shouldn’t stop there. Yosef didn’t say, “I’m already good enough. I don’t need to work any harder.” He kept pushing himself to do better, and that’s why he earned the special title.

Think about your own day for a moment. You may have learned Torah, davened, and done wonderful mitzvos! But looking even more closely, you might notice that there were moments when you could have tried harder—maybe you could have learned with more chayus, focused better in davening, or helped someone with more Ahavas Yisroel. Even when you fill your day with goodness, there’s always a way to add even more.

Yosef Hatzaddik lived this way—especially when things were difficult. Most of his life was spent in Mitzrayim, surrounded by people who didn’t behave according to the Torah. Yosef was busy, important, and constantly around not- good influences. Still, he didn’t change who he was. Instead of being pulled down, he grew even stronger. Truly, Yosef Hatzaddik.

We learn this from Yosef … who is called Yosef Hatzaddik.

<span class="mc">Let’s learn from Yosef’s example. No matter where we are, and no matter how hard things feel, we can always serve Hashem better and better. We can be like Yosef Hatzaddik: always growing and remaining proud Yidden.</span>

Which choice is the way of Yosef Hatzaddik?

I already did well, so I can take it easy now.

I already did well, and I’ll look for more ways to keep growing.

Yosef was all alone; sold by his own brothers. How did he have the ko’ach to remain strong?

What makes it hard to keep growing after you’ve already done well?

What is one small way you can push yourself a little higher this week?

What can you learn from Yosef Hatzaddik? Find family members or friends to play this board game with you. Get pieces, roll a dice, and move around the board. Every time you pass a growing space, share one way that you can do even better.

The game is over, but the work has just begun. How can you be like Yosef Hatzaddik in real life?

Benny ran outside for recess, but stopped on the stairs. He had noticed the new boy, Aizik, standing alone.

He never joins in our games, Benny thought. Maybe it’s because he doesn’t know much English yet. He just arrived here after the war with other refugee boys. He must miss his family back in Europe so much.

“Hey, Aizik,” he called out. “Can you believe it’s almost Chanukah? I already went to the store to choose the new bike I’m getting with my Chanukah gelt! What are you doing with your gelt?”

“Oh, I wish I’d get some,” Aizik said sadly. “I just got a letter that my little sister Golda is very krank and needs penicillin\*

During afternoon classes, Benny just couldn’t concentrate. The minute the last bell rang, he gathered his friends and repeated the conversation he’d had with Aizik. “We have to do something!”

“You know how much I wanted that bike, but I think I’m going to give my gelt to Aizik, and if we all chip in…”

<span class="mc" style="color:#2873b6">Everyone readily agreed. That night, Benny wrote a letter.</span>

To Best Brooklyn Bicycles:

I put a blue bicycle on hold in your back room so that I could purchase it with my Chanukah allowance. However, my friends and I have decided to pool our money for a better purpose, so that our classmate Aizik can send medicine to an ill family member in Europe. Please sell the bike to another customer.

Chanukah came. All the boys gathered around Aizik during recess. “This is a present for Golda,” Benny said shyly, stepping forward and handing him a package. Aizik tore it open.

“Penicillin!” Aizik cried out, tears welling in his eyes. “How can I ever thank you?”

“It’s nothing,” the other boys mumbled. They, too, were crying.

That evening, after lighting the menorah, Benny’s father said, “A letter came for you in the mail today.”

Benny opened the letter from Best Brooklyn Bicycles nervously. Were they asking him why he didn't pick up his bike? Had the letter with his instructions about the bicycle gotten lost?

Thank you for your letter in which you told us why you wouldn’t be buying a bike from us. Benny, we think that you and your friends did a remarkable thing and we have decided to keep that bike in the back room—until you can come to the store and claim it as a gift.

P.S. Tell your friends to come with you. We have a present for each one of them. And one for Aizik, too.

“Oh!” Benny exclaimed. “This is the best Chanukah ever!”
