Source: Der Rebbe Redt tzu Kinder Vol. 3, pg. 104. Sicha of the Lag BaOmer Parade, 18 Iyar, 5740 (1980).

You know what is my biggest pet peeve, my least favorite thing in the world?

Especially being bored on a quiet, boring day with no one around and nothing to do right after two weeks of Pesach excitement.

At seven o’clock this morning, I stood outside in my slippers waving goodbye to my cousins, the Sharfmans from Arizona, who had spent the whole Pesach with us. I came back into the kitchen to see that all our Pesach boxes were packed up and the extra folding tables were already put back in the closet. Tatty and my big brothers waved goodbye as they left to shul. Yitzi and Menucha were playing with Magna-Tiles on the living room floor. I couldn't help but wonder how they never got bored of them.

It wasn’t so bad in the beginning. I had my first bowl of chametz’dik cereal, davened, and read some books. I colored some pictures. I stared out the window and watched the neighbors mow their lawn. I had another bowl of cereal. Then I started to really feel how quiet, empty, and boring the house had become.

“Bored?” she answered as she folded another shirt, “that’s great! Keep feeling bored, and you’ll accomplish great things!”

“Come on,” I said, “I thought you went to seminary to get smarter.”

“But it’s true,” she answered, “feeling bored is your neshama’s way of reminding you that there’s work to do.”

I walked down the hall and saw Mushkie organizing her desk. She just got a new set of Chumashim, and was making room for them on her shelf.

Back downstairs, Baila was practicing a niggun on the piano.

Mommy was already baking challah and calling guests to invite them for Shabbos.

Everyone was doing something important. (Well, except for Yitzi and Menucha—they were still (!) playing with Magna-Tiles.)

I lay down on my mission carpet and started to think.

Before you knew it, we had a folding table set up on the sidewalk, complete with freshly squeezed lemonade and chocolate chip cookies still warm from the oven.

Our lemonade stand was a smashing success, Baruch Hashem. We raised thirty-two dollars, and Mommy already sent it to Colel Chabad, the tzedakah fund in Eretz Yisroel started by the Alter Rebbe himself.

And that’s where the day went from boring and empty, to fun and full. I bet my neshama feels it, too.

You know, Aidel might be right. That itchy, annoying feeling of being bored could be a good thing; a reminder to find more mitzvos to do. It’s even helping me win over the Yetzer Hara and get closer to being a five-star general.
