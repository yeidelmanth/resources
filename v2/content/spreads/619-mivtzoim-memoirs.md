So, today I flew on a dreidel to Alaska for a giant menorah lighting!

But what I do have to tell you is way more exciting than that! Today, I went to a Chanukah rally! Wait, what am I saying? It was a military parade! (That’s more like it.) And our General, the Rebbe, gave us an important mission.

I don’t know how much you know about armies, dear diary, so I’ll tell you: In an army, if there’s ever a special goal that you need to accomplish, then you must recruit more soldiers. In Hashem’s army, the usual goal is to learn more Torah and do mitzvos extra carefully, but now, we have a special goal from our Commander-in-Chief, Hashem: To bring Moshiach now!!! (Hence, the new song that I can’t stop singing!)

Anyways, to help with this special mission, Hashem is sending reinforcements—more Yiddishe boys and girls—to join us in our fight with our super-strong weapons: the Torah and mitzvos!

It is not enough to simply fight in this war. I must mobilize more kids to volunteer and join the ranks of Tzivos Hashem. Boys drafting boys, girls drafting girls. There is no time to waste. And we need everyone to join in!

Together with the Rebbe leading the way, we will win the fight against the Yetzer Hara and bring Moshiach now!

P.S. Surprise! The Rebbe requested a totally unexpected rally on Chof-Ches Tishrei, and Rabbi JJ Hecht taught our army our anthem (every army has an official song called an “anthem”).

It goes: “Am Yisroel have no fear, Moshiach will be here this year! We want Moshiach now! We don’t want to wait!”

Of course I can’t stop singing it! The Rebbe likes it very much, and even explained today that it’s perfect for our mission!

But why does Shulamis keep telling me to be quiet? Doesn’t she love my singing?

Mivtzoim—they feel like they’ve been around forever! Can you even imagine life without them? Yet, unbelievably, before the Rebbe introduced each of the mitzvah campaigns that we know so well, they simply didn’t exist.

Join us as we explore imaginary old diaries that could have belonged to kids just like you— kids who might have lived during the time when the Rebbe first announced many of these mivtzoim.

It might have been written over 40 years ago, but after you dust off the cover of Zeidy Tzvi’s old diary and take a peek inside, you'll be transported back in time, living the exciting beginnings of Tzivos Hashem for yourself\_right here, right now.

Never before have I felt so proud to be a soldier in the strongest army in the world!

Standing at attention with my fellow soldiers while listening to the Rebbe, our General, speaking to us was unreal. We are unstoppable!

In fact, when a boy was blocking me from seeing the podium, I didn’t pour water down his back. No siree! I pleasantly said “excuse me”, and felt my Yetzer Hara shrink away.

The Rebbe spoke about having a “soldier specialty”, which means a specific mission that you’re really good at, more than anything else. Guess what our soldier specialty is? Nope, not being a pilot—I’m scared of heights. And also no, definitely not sea battles! I would totally throw up.

Learning Torah is our specialty! We need to learn Torah so much that it becomes a part of us! And then, what’s the point of the learning? To help us do mitzvos! (Yeah, far better than jumping out of planes in parachutes.)

And the Rebbe made it clear: We’re not just here for ourselves. We need to expand our ranks.

I’m fired up! I already know which Tanya and mishnayos I’ll learn, and I have a full list of kids to recruit. Victory is in sight!

P.S. I will never need encouragement to do my Gemara chazara again. After all, Torah is my specialty! (And I think my mother is sighing with relief.)

If you’re wondering why there are green smudges on this page, it’s because I had some green face paint on my face so I could really get into that soldier mode, and then there was a fly, and … okay, that’s enough information.

Now, there are three things that need to be completed in this world: 1) My homework, 2) My homework, and 3) My homework.

Okay, that’s it for the Adar jokes, ladies and gentlemen. For real, now.
