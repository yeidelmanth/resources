The words his father had written to the Rebbe so many years before were almost exactly the same as in the letter he had written just that morning!

And the Rebbe’s handwritten bracha was: “וְיְהֵא ּכָל הַנַ"ל ּבְׁשָעָה טֹובָה ּומּוצְלַחַת, אַזְּכִיר עַל הַצִיֹון—May it all take place at a good and successful time; I will mention it at the Ohel.”

The Rebbe is answering me! Zeidy realized. I will have hatzlacha to buy the building!

And so it was. Buying the building went smoothly, and soon, it was transformed into a beautiful Chabad House.

But that’s not all. Just a few weeks after buying it, Zeidy got a call from a lawyer who told him something amazing:

“Remember how the sellers wanted to sell the building quickly because they needed the money? Well, an elderly woman just passed away at 101 years old, and left five million dollars to the people who sold you the building. If she had died just a few weeks earlier, they wouldn’t have needed to sell the building so fast, and probably wouldn’t have sold it at all!”

The hashgacha pratis was clear: Hashem had wanted Zeidy to turn that building into a Chabad House.

And remember how Zeidy was worried that the building was too far away? Well, before long, many Yidden began moving closer to the area of the new Chabad House! It was in the perfect spot to help lots of people connect to Hashem.

Zeidy had turned to the Rebbe, and the Rebbe showed that he was with him. We, too, can turn to the Rebbe when we need help. The Rebbe will find a way to answer!

The tzetel from years before held the answer to Zeidy’s dilemma.

When Bubby and Zeidy first moved on shlichus to Memphis, Tennessee, they lived in a small apartment. Two years later, they bought a house with a big garage, which they turned into a shul and Chabad House.

As the community grew, they needed a bigger property—but twelve years later, the Chabad House was still in the garage! Over the years, they tried to buy eight different buildings, but none of them worked out.

Finally, a new idea came up: a huge property for sale with a big building and parking lot. The owners needed money quickly, so they were willing to sell it at a low price. There was just one problem: it was a bit far from the Jewish area.

During this time, he went to New York for the Kinus Hashluchim. On the flight, Zeidy wrote an emotional letter to the Rebbe.

He began with the words: “ּבְקָרֹוב יִתְמַלְאּו י"ג ׁשָנָה— Soon, it will have been thirteen years” since we came to Memphis, and we still don’t have a building for our Chabad House.

He wrote that a new property had come up, and asked for a sign if he should buy it. Zeidy finished his letter with the words: “ּבְאִם הַנַ"ל נָכֹון—Is it the right thing \[to do\]?”

When the plane landed, Zeidy went to daven at the Ohel. Afterwards, he went to visit his parents in Crown Heights.

That evening, his father, Rabbi Binyomin Klein, a”h, who was the Rebbe’s secretary, suddenly remembered something.

“I have a tzetel from the Rebbe with a handwritten answer about your Bar Mitzvah. I want to give it to you.”

Zeidy was very surprised. His father almost never gave him anything from the Rebbe, not even things that were about him!

Zeidy’s father got the tzetel and showed it to him. “You were born on Yud-Gimmel Tammuz,” he said, “the special day when the Frierdiker Rebbe came out of prison. The Rebbe would always make a farbrengen on that day, so we planned your Bar Mitzvah celebration for a few days later.
