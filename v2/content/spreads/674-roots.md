Heroes of Dobromysl

Next, Yitzchak Shaul pointed out Zevulun Binyamin the skinner and his son-in-law, Yirmiya. Zevulun Binyamin was one of the few people whom Baruch did actually get to know during his previous times in Dobromysl, as Zevulun Binyamin frequently visited the smithy. Baruch himself had noticed, even before Yitzchak Shaul had pointed it out, that Zevulun Binyamin had changed a lot.

In the past, Zevulun Binyamin would sit at the back of the shul, alone, the chairs and benches empty around him. He would sit with his eyes downcast and his lips barely moving. The only time he got an aliya was once a year on Simchas Torah, during kol hane’arim. The unlearned man would whisper the words after the children, his face dark with shame.

Baruch also heard the story of how Zevulun Binyamin saved the entire town of Dobromysl. It started with a terrible epidemic that raged through the small town, especially affecting young children. Zevulun Binyamin, with his knowledge of farm animals, was the only one who realized the source of the illness: poisoned milk, as a result of the cows and goats eating contaminated grasses. And he knew not just the source of the problem, but the exact herbs and plants that would cure the poison. Incredibly, within just a couple of days, all of the sick were healed, thanks to Zevulun naps during the day and slept for just a few hours each night—all on his bench.

After this, the rav of Dobromysl himself praised Zevulun Binyamin for his help during a public drasha in shul. Following that, everyone else’s attitude towards Zevulun Binyamin completely turned around. In fact, the rav himself made sure to greet Zevulun Binyamin with the honorary title of “Reb”, inspiring others to follow.
