chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Last week’s Parshafier ended too soon. I just feel like we just didn’t get enough time with Avraham Avinu. I wish we could talk to him again, just to know more about him.

Hmmm, we don’t usually have the same guest twice in a row, but I think I have an idea of someone else to call. Yossi, can you call Sara Imeinu?

Oooh! Yes. Good idea, Mimi. I’ll call her right away.

Hi Sara Imeinu! We’re so happy that we get to talk to you!

Actually, Basya has a question you might be able to answer. She’s just a bit shy.

Well then, until Basya is ready, why don’t I tell you a bit about what I do here? You see, whenever travellers are passing through our area, we always invite them in. The men go to Avraham’s tent, and the women come here, to my tent.

All about Hashem! This is our center for all types of gemilas chassadim, starting with hachnasas orchim. We invite everyone inside for a warm meal, and then we teach them about Hashem.

Baruch Hashem, yes. It’s how the entire Jewish nation began.

Well, sort of. The Yidden started with your great-great-great-zeidy’s love for hachnasas orchim. It was when he had mesiras nefesh to host and serve three of our guests that we found out we would have our precious son, Yitzchak!

Yes, that’s right. Tzedakah and gemilas chassadim are very important for us, so we were very careful to raise our son with those values, too. And we wish for all of our children and all of the Yidden to continue in this way! I am sure that you children are very good at it.

Yeah! I try to share my Legos with my friends who don’t have any. And Chaim is really kind to me—he helps me with my homework. Mimi always helps me find my missing socks, and Basya tries her hardest to help everyone get along.

Baruch Hashem! That’s wonderful to hear. You children are giving us much nachas by doing the first type of gemilas chassadim. And I’m sure you do the other kinds, as well.

Yes. With the first kind of gemilas chassadim, you are helping other people. With the second kind, you are helping your neshama. When you learn Torah or do any of the mitzvos, it makes your neshama extremely happy.

Chaim learns a lot of Torah! I’m sure his neshama is very happy. I mean, we all try to learn Torah and do mitzvos all day, because we really want to be nice to our neshamos!

Sara Imeinu, is there another kind of gemilas chassadim? Maybe where you help other people’s neshamos? I’m asking because you and Avraham Avinu wouldn’t just feed food to your guests; you also taught them about Hashem!

That, my dear, is the greatest form of gemilas chassadim!

Like when we use the Parsha Podcast to teach other children all about Hashem?

Exactly! You are making many neshamos happy—the best kind of gemilas chassadim! And now let’s ask all the other chayolim to think about it: How will you do all the types of gemilas chassadim?

...and that’s another fabulous episode of the Parsha Podcast. Thank you, Sara Imeinu, for joining us! We’ll be sure to think the question over. See you all chayolim next week!
