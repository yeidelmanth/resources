<p class="lead">Our aunt traveled all the way to Tzfas to find a hidden kever, but no one knew where it was.</p>

Growing up, our aunt Nechama often heard stories about her great-great-uncle, Ezra Chaim HaKohen. He was a well-known mekubal, and many miracles happened through him.

When she went to Eretz Yisroel a few years ago, she really wanted to daven by his kever. No one in the family knew where he was buried, so she called a historian in Tzfas named David.

“I can help you look for the kever,” he told her. “Let’s meet after Simchas Torah.”

But on that Simchas Torah—October 7th—there was a big attack in Eretz Yisroel. Our aunt had to go home without finding the kever.

Two years later, our aunt returned to Eretz Yisroel with her husband, our uncle. She contacted David again, who said he could meet her in Tzfas on Sunday.

“Call me on Friday to confirm,” he told her.

From Friday through Sunday morning, she tried calling again and again, more than thirty times! But there was no answer.

Still, they went to Tzfas.

The entire drive there felt uncertain. Everything depended on David, who wasn’t answering his phone.

When they arrived, they walked through the streets, asking if anyone knew David. Finally, a Chabad Chassid at a tefillin stand said he had seen David earlier that day. He tried calling him, but there was still no answer.

Someone nearby suggested calling a woman with the same last name as Ezra Chaim HaKohen. They called, but she didn’t know where he was buried, but she gave them the number of a man named Eli, who worked at the cemetery.

Our aunt called him. “I’ll see what I can do,” Eli said, and then he hung up.

Our aunt and uncle asked around if anyone else knew him or had seen him, and some people did! So wherever they were told he had been that day, our aunt and uncle went there to try and track him down! Hours passed, and it was getting

*Should we just give up?* She wondered. But then, she knew what to do. She took out her phone and began to write a letter to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha to find the kever, and emailed it into the ohel.

<figure class="handimg inline"><img src="https://mashpia.com/resources/assets/img/7a4e72f27f6a055d.png" alt="handwritten note"></figure>

Just a few minutes later, her phone rang.

It was Eli. “Meet me at the Arizal’s kever,” he said.

When they arrived, Eli led them to a hidden part of the cemetery. He explained that many talmidei chachamim were buried in caves, and over time the earth had shifted, hiding many kevarim.

“What hashgacha pratit,” he said. “I don’t usually answer my phone when I’m working, but I answered your call. Then, when you said the name, I knew right away which kever you meant, because I had just been there. And I’m the only person here who knows that he’s buried in this cave!”

He brought them inside, and there it was:

the kever of Ezra

Chaim HaKohen.

Our aunt said

Tehillim by the kever, her heart everything had felt hopeless, she turned to the Rebbe—and the Rebbe’s brachos helped her finally finish her long search.
