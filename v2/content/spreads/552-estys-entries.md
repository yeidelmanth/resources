General news for today: I just got promoted to two- star general in Tzivos Hashem! Generally-speaking , my class couldn’t believe it, but those who did were just two proud of me. (Get it? “...two proud of me?”)

Okay, I did actually get promoted. But enough with the jokes.

Mussia and I got together after school. We’re on a mission to become five-star generals before our Bas Mitzvahs, and we need all the brains we can get. So we sat on my faithful “Mission Carpet”—the fluffy pink rug in my bedroom where all the great ideas happen—and brainstormed there.

First things first: Get that pesky Yetzer Hara out of the way. For good!

Missions would be so much easier without Mr. YH around.

Like, you know when you come home from a late-night Melave Malka totally exhausted, and you just wanna plop into your bed with your dirty socks still on, curl up in your favorite fuzzy blanket, and forget about

being responsible? But then you remember that you’re a chayol and there’s no such thing. Missions are waiting. So you get up and find some nice, clean pajamas.

Not enough! Now you need to brush your teeth, prepare negel vasser, and say Shema from a siddur. That’s what being a soldier is about: doing what needs to get done even when you don’t feel like it.

But the Yetzer Hara wants us to forget that we are soldiers.

“Come on,” I hear the voice inside me say, “It’s okay to have a lazy night once in a while. Just hide under your blanket and forget about all those things that take so much time. Anyway, you need your sleep to

1\. Power in numbers: hire an army to fight the Yetzer Hara.

2\. Stick him in a bottle and glue-gun it closed.

3\. Ask Tatty to take care of him. (He does a good job at battling his own YH…)

or #2 yet, we settled on #3. Now I just need to wait for Tatty to come home from Mincha.

So…as it turns out, Tatty is not going to take care of our Yetzer Haras for us. He said that’s something we gotta do ourselves. Why? Because each person has their own Yetzer Hara. Your YH is inside of you. Blech.

No wonder it seems like he follows me around all the time. You know what it’s like trying to climb ranks with him around? It feels like two steps forward, one step backward, again and again.

Mussia and I didn’t get anywhere on the Mission Carpet today, and our Bas Mitzvahs are fast approaching. How are we ever gonna climb ranks fast enough?

Day 5 of sharing Sefer Hamitzvos at the dinner table. It took me only a couple minutes to review with Tatty what I learned in school, and then I shared it with the family. Mussia is doing the same in her own house.

I wish I could meet the Rambam myself, and tell him all about what we’re doing. I would travel to Cairo, Egypt, where he lived—riding on a camel, of course. I’d tell him how I, a fifth grader in Berryville, New Jersey, learn his Sefer Hamitzvos every day. And so does Mussia, so do my cousins in South Carolina, and also my friend Malky from sleepaway camp. How delighted he would be!

“Yes,” he would stroke his beard and beam with sparkling eyes. “That’s exactly what I wanted.”

“And a whole army of Yidden around the world are doing the same, learning all 613 mitzvos with Sefer Hamitzvos and Mishnah Torah.”

“Wow,” he would reply, “That is real achdus. This is going to bring the Geulah!”

And I know that it will, too. I already feel like a stronger chayol.
