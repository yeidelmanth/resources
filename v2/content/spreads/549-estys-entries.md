After lighting the menorah tonight, I sat on the couch to watch the candles as usual. Tatty sat on the couch with his Rambam, as usual. After a minute, I turned to Tatty and asked him if he had any ideas that could help save my special shoes.

Tatty took off his glasses and examined my feet. Then he put his glasses back on his nose and looked at the menorah. He said, “Esty, do you know why we lit three candles tonight?”

“Yeah,” I said. “Because it’s Chanukah n i g h t - n u m b e r- t h r e e ! ”

“That’s nice,” Tatty said. “But if one candle was good enough on the first night of Chanukah, why isn’t just one candle good enough every night after that?”

Then I remembered something from my d’var Torah on Shabbos. When it comes to mitzvos, the Rebbe says, we always need to add more and more. We can’t just do the same amount as yesterday. “So maybe,” I told Tatty, “that’s why we need to have more candles each night.”

Tatty said, “That’s right—the light of Torah and kedusha must grow brighter every day. And so must the light of your neshama.”

Tatty explained: “When you’re young, you grow taller and taller every day, and your feet get bigger and bigger. Your old clothes and shoes stop fitting you; they aren’t good enough anymore. And the way we did mitzvos yesterday isn’t good enough either; today we need to do the same again, plus more!”

“You mean I need to get even better super- sneakers?” I asked.

“Hmm,” said Tatty, “I guess you could look at it that way.”

Y’know that one of my favorite things to do is climb trees, right? Well, that might change real soon.

It’s because of what happened today when I tried going up the dogwood tree in front of our house. Sholom is home from yeshiva for Chanukah, and he said he’d help me. The first thing I did was get my purple super-sneakers on.

Bubby gave me those sneakers on my birthday. She says they are just nice, regular sneakers, but let me tell you, they’re not just good for sneaking; when I wear these shoes, I become a super- climber, super-cartwheeler, and super-runner. All of this is very important because I need to come in first place at the Chanukah carnival races this year. The grand

prize is a seven-speed mountain bike, which I’ve wanted for years.

So anyway, after I got my super-sneakers on, Sholom and I went outside. This time, I wanted to make it to the top of the tree. But I was only on the first branch when the big toe of my left foot popped out of my left purple sneaker. I kept going, but then my right ankle started to hurt. I looked down to see what was happening, and just then—I lost my balance. I nearly toppled off the tree and landed on Sholom, but I caught myself on a big branch right in time.

Sholom got worried about safety and stuff like that,

so he said we had to go inside. Also, it was getting dark out and he had to set up his menorah.

But now, it’s time for menorah lighting; I can hear Tatty calling me.

I started looking for a way I could grow in kedusha. I know it’s time to grow out of fighting with Menucha. She’s just four years old, so it’s normal for her to cry about little things, but I’m ten—I’m her big, mature, older siste r.

Still, I can’t just let her take my chocolate coins whenever she wants. I asked Mommy what to do. Mommy suggested that I make a compromise in a happy, calm way. I can share a few of my chocolate coins with Menucha and she’ll be happy. Then I can explain to her nicely that I want to keep the rest for myself.

I already feel the light of my neshama shining and making the world around me brighter. But I know that our Commander-in-Chief, Hashem, always wants us to grow more and do more.The best news is that Mommy said she’ll take me to buy a new, bigger pair of super-sneakers before the Chanukah carnival.

Maybe that’s Hashem’s gift to me. Mommy says that whenever we do more mitzvos, Hashem gives us more brachos—brachos that we can see as clearly as the Chanukah lights. I can’t wait
