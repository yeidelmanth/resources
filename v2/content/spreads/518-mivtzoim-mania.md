“You mean the soldiers in your organization who volunteered for the Jewish Book Day?”

“Exactly, Mr. Senator. The Rebbe taught us that every chayol should turn his or her room into a Mikdash Me’at.”

“I’m sorry Sruli, my Hebrew isn’t that good. What is a ‘Mikdash Me’at?’”

“It’s a mini Holy Temple,” I explained. “Every Jew is given the opportunity to make a home for Hashem where He can be comfortable, just like in the Holy Temple of Jerusalem.”

”The Beis Hamikdash contained many holy items. By having and using items in our rooms that represent what was in the real Beis Hamikdash, it’s like a mini Holy Temple do-it-yourself kit.

I couldn’t help but laugh. “No, Mr. Senator. But each mini Holy Temple will have a siddur, a prayer book, instead.”

“Right,” I answered. “Instead of the Aron Hakodesh, the Holy Ark which held the Ten Command- ments, we have a

Chumash with which to learn Hashem’s holy Torah. In place of the Shulchan, on which the showbread was placed, we put a charity box in the room.”

My mind was ticking. Next month, Tzivos Hashem will launch a campaign to make sure that every chayol’s room has a siddur, a Chumash and a tzedakah box. To start off, we’ll send Cheder Tzivos Hashem signs for members to put on their bedroom doors.

“Sruli,” the Senator interrupted my thoughts, “Maybe we can have each of the items custom made, and sent as a kit to each and every soldier in your organization. It would be my pleasure to sponsor this!”

“What do you think of naming the kit ‘Chabad House in a Box?’” he asked.

“I love it. It’s very clever.” I was smiling from ear to ear.

“By the way,” I hesitated, “the Rebbe also said that chayolim should have a Tanya in their rooms.”

“If your Rebbe wants a Tanya in each chayol’s room,” he said in a cheerful tone, “we’ll include a Tanya in the box too.”

The Senator's office arranged everything with Tzivos Hashem, and in no time, chayolim across the world received their very own kits of ‘Chabad House in a Box.’ We included copies of the Rebbe’s letter to children about a bakasha nafshis for children to set up their own Tzivos Hashem bases in their rooms.

We also requested that chayolim and their parents send us feedback letters, of which the top three would be featured in next month’s Hachayol Magazine. Baruch Hashem, the response was incredible! \[Look to

the right to read a snippet of one of the letters that we plan to feature.\]

At the end of one of those busy nights, just as my head hit my pillow, the doorbell rang. 10 seconds later, it rang again.

“Strange,” I thought to myself. “Usually, my parents answer the door by now.”

The house was silent. Where did everybody go? The bell kept ringing every few seconds. I made my way downstairs.

I opened the door for the familiar voice. My heartbeat must’ve broken the sound barrier; I was petrified.

No longer alive, yet standing at my front door, was Rafi Adler!
