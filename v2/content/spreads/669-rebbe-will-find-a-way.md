What brought Mordechai, a Gerrer Chossid, to the Kinus Hashluchim?

The banquet hall was packed with thousands of shluchim from around the world, all joining together b’achdus. Everyone sang and danced, so proud to be the Rebbe’s shluchim on the front lines to bring Moshiach.

Soon, it was time to go. Rabbi Sholom Leverton, my teacher’s father, left the hall on a high. He was filled with new chayus and energy to continue his shlichus with even more ko’ach.

As Rabbi Leverton walked to his car, a Gerrer Chossid hurried over. “Could you give me a ride to my car?” he asked. “It’s parked a few blocks away.”

“Of course,” Rabbi Leverton said. But he was curious. “What’s your name?” he asked as he pulled out of his parking spot. “And what brought you to the Kinus?”

“My name is Mordechai,” said the man, “and I come to the Kinus every year to thank the Rebbe.”

Rabbi Leverton looked at him in surprise. “To thank the Rebbe?”

“Yes,” Mordechai said. “Let me tell you my story. A few years ago, when my wife was expecting a baby, the doctors gave us very sad news. ‘'Your baby might not survive,’ they told us, ‘'and even if he does, he could be very sick.’”

Mordechai’s voice shook as he remembered that hard time. “We were heartbroken,” he continued. “My father took me to many tzaddikim for brachos, but as the pregnancy continued, the doctors became even more convinced that the baby wouldn’t survive.”

He wiped a tear from his eye. “One day, my grandmother told me something I had never known before. She told me, ‘'When I was expecting your father, the doctors were also very worried. I went to the Lubavitcher Rebbe for a bracha, and your father was born completely healthy.’”
