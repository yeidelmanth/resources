“I can’t thank you enough,” he whispered. “This letter means so much to me.”

Seeing how moved Levi was, Tatty realized that this was the perfect moment to ask Levi to do a mitzvah. “Would you like to put on tefillin?” he asked gently. Levi had not worn tefillin in many years.

“I’ll do it for the Rebbe,” Levi said with emotion. His neshama shone brightly as he wrapped the straps around his arm, connecting with Hashem in a very deep way.

This was the first of many more mitzvos that followed because of Levi’s newfound connection to the Rebbe. Just knowing how much the Rebbe cared about him helped him start growing in his Yiddishkeit once again.

No matter who we are, connecting with the Rebbe awakens our neshamos and helps us connect to Hashem more than ever before. Let us all strengthen our connection with the Rebbe, so we can have the ko’ach to do more mitzvos and bring Moshiach now!

Levi wasn’t frum for many years, but when he connected with the Rebbe, his neshama shone brightly.

Our family is on shlichus in New Jersey, about two hours away from Queens. Our favorite part of our shlichus is bringing Yidden to the Ohel, so they can connect to the Rebbe and grow in their Yiddishkeit.

One day, just a few years ago, Tatty got a phone call from his cousin Rabbi Levi Azimov, a shliach in Paris.

“I need your help,” Rabbi Azimov began. “I know a man in Eretz Yisrael who sells old sefarim and other valuable Jewish objects. He usually finds them in the libraries of people who have passed away. Whenever he finds letters and dollars from the Rebbe, I am always happy to buy them.

“He just sold me a letter that the Rebbe wrote in honor of the birth of a baby boy. In the letter, the Rebbe bentches the parents to raise the baby l’Torah, l’chuppah, ul’maasim tovim.”

“Okay,” Tatty said. “But what does this have to do with me?”

“Well, this letter was addressed to a family in New Jersey, where you’re on shlichus,” Rabbi Azimov explained. “Even though it was written more than thirty years ago, I was wondering if you might know who the baby is. His name is Levi W\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ .”

Azimov replied with a sigh. “But I’m grateful that you do know him. You know what? I’m planning to come to the Rebbe for Yud-Alef Nissan. I’ll bring the letter along so you can give it to Levi.”

A few weeks later, Tatty met Rabbi Azimov at the Ohel, got the letter, and drove to Levi’s house. When he explained why he had come, Levi’s eyes widened in surprise. “A letter from the Rebbe? For me?”

He took the letter and read it slowly, tears gathering in his eyes as he absorbed the Rebbe’s brachos.
