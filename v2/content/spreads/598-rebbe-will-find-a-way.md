Tatty felt that things couldn't get any worse. The officer could fine him for thousands of dollars. Excusing himself for a minute, Tatty ran inside and looked at the large Rebbe picture hanging in the living room.

He closed his eyes, imagined himself in front of the Rebbe, and spoke from his heart. "Rebbe, please help me!" he cried.

Feeling much calmer, Tatty went back outside to face the officer.

"Do you see this folder full of court papers?" the officer asked. "I'm throwing it all out. And you boys," he said, calling to Jacob and his friend, "Can you hurry up and finish the job?”

Seeing Tatty’s look of confused amazement, he explained. "You know, my wife grew up in this house. You bought it from my mother- in-law. I can see how well you've fixed it up, and how much care you put into it. That’s why I’m letting you go."

The officer glared at Tatty. What would happen next?

“See that boy?” the man standing next to Tatty whispered. "He's Jewish—17 years old, and he’s never put on tefillin before!”

Of course, Tatty immediately went over to the boy, whose name was Jacob, and asked if he'd like to put on tefillin. Jacob agreed.

Tatty helped him wrap the tefillin and say Shema. “Mazel tov!” he wished him when they were done. “This was your unofficial Bar Mitzvah! Putting on tefillin for the first time is a big deal. We’ll have to celebrate with a proper party!”

Jacob was excited. A few weeks later, Tatty organized a celebration for him in our shul. Jacob began spending a lot of time with our family, becoming like an uncle to us.

A few years later, our city redid the sewer systems. Because of this, everyone had to reconnect their pipes to the new sewer line.

This was very expensive, so Tatty decided to wait. Pretty soon, the city started sending him letters, warning him that he had to connect his sewer lines to the new system as soon as possible!

During this hectic time, Jacob came over and heard about the problem.

"Why didn't you tell me before, Rabbi?" he asked. "I can do this for you!"

"Sure! All I'll need is a specific pipe from the store. It should cost around $60."

A few days later, Jacob came with a friend and started digging in our backyard. While they were working, an officer from the city came with the final letter from the court that we had to listen to the law, or else we would be in big trouble. When he saw Jacob and his friend hard at work, his forehead creased and his face turned red.
