When the officer went inside for her lunch break, Tatty wrote to the Rebbe again and asked for a bracha.

Soon, a new officer came to the door. “Go home and email the office.” she ordered, sending Tatty away.

Tatty’s shoulders slumped in despair. What should he do now? He decided to go to the new office that that hadn’t opened yet, hoping that the visa would be there.

But the office was in the middle of being painted. There were no visas in sight.

On the way, our sister called, “Did you get the visa yet?”

“No,” Tatty said. “Say tehillim, and ask the Rebbe for help.”

Our sister hung up the phone and asked for a bracha.

Tatty ordered an Uber home, feeling very sad. He called Mommy to tell her what was going on, and suddenly, an email came through on his phone. The visa was ready!

Everyone was stunned. Just seconds after our sister had asked for a bracha, her tefillos were answered! With a huge sigh of relief, Tatty headed straight back to the office to get the visa.

While there, he learned that the first security guard had worked hard to get the visa, not giving up until it was ready. Tatty felt that the Rebbe had heard his tefillos too, sending this security guard who would be kind enough to help him.

That Sunday, we all boarded the plane with our visas in hand. It was time to go to the chasunah!

Tatty ran from one place to the next, desperately trying to get a visa for our sister. Was he too late?

We are shluchim of the Rebbe in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

When our older sister became a kallah, Tatty booked tickets to New York for the chasunah. Pretty soon, he realized there was a problem: one of our sister’s visas was expiring the day of our flight. Without it, she wouldn’t be allowed into America!

Tatty quickly applied for a new visa, which was supposed to be ready a week before our flight. But four days before the big trip, Tatty still hadn’t heard from the visa office. He went there to find out what was going on.

“We don’t have your daughter’s visa,” the man at the desk told him. “Email the main office, the U.S. consulate, and ask if it’s ready. Don’t even bother going; they won’t let you in without an appointment.”

Tatty was very worried that our sister might miss her flight for the chasunah. He went home and wrote to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha that the visa should be ready in time.

He then emailed the main visa office, but no one answered.

Soon it was Friday, the last chance to get the visa before our flight. Tatty went back to the local visa office, but it was

closed; they were moving to a new office in another part of town. “Where can I get my visa?” he cried out.

“Maybe it’s still at the main office,” someone nearby said. “Or maybe it’s at the new office that didn’t open yet.”

Hoping to get the visa, Tatty traveled all the way to the main office. The entrance was blocked by officers who were only letting people with appointments go inside.

“Please let me in,” Tatty begged one of the officers. “I need my daughter’s visa today!”
