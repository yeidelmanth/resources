Rochel was quiet for a minute. Her eyes grew wet, and a small smile played on her face. “Yes. Yes, I will teach you. But there’s one condition.” Rochel pressed an index finger to her lips. “It must remain our secret.”

Devorah Leah’s face lit up. “Oh Mamme, our learning will be just for us, like Zeide Baruch! He didn’t show off his learning, either.”

Rochel stood up and hugged her daughter tightly. “Yes, it will be for us. And for Hashem.”

Devorah Leah began a routine of learning together. They would start when Reb Shneur Zalman left to go with Baruch on a daily walk. Then they would pause for dinner, and resume again after Reb Shneur Zalman and Baruch left to shul, continuing for several more hours. Devorah Leah never spoke about these learning sessions with her father. And Reb Shneur Zalman never brought it up directly, either. But Devorah Leah could sense — it was something in her father’s eyes when he glanced in

her direction as he left for shul, or cleared sefarim off of the table — that her father was proud.

Devorah Leah and her mother started by studying a little Chumash, and then added in some Mishna. Later on in the night, when the candles would burn low and it was harder to read, Devorah Leah would start on the sewing basket, and Rochel would tell her stories about their past.

Many times, Rochel talked about Reb Shneur Zalman’s great- great-great grandfather, the holy Maharal, about the Maharal’s learned wife Perel, and about the beautiful artwork of the Maharal’s grandson Reb Shmuel, the rosh hakahal of Prague.
