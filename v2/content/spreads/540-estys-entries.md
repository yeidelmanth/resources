Source: Der Rebbe Redt Tzu Kinder, First edition, Vol. 1, pg. 133 and 141 Toras

I woke up this morning and realized that it’s already the last day of Chol Hamoed. The last day to bentch lulav and say leshev basukkah. Soon Yom Tov is gonna be over, Pesha and Freidel and all my cousins are gonna go home…and I’m gonna go back to boring regular life. I know I complained before about all my cousins taking over my house, but in the end, Pesha taught me how to Double Dutch jump rope, Freidel helped me climb to the top of the 40-foot tree in Blueberry Forest, and we all made a ridiculous play about Getzel, the neighbor’s pet goat.

What am I going to do all day long when Yom Tov is over and everybody leaves?

I know on Sukkos it’s a mitzvah to be happy, but I bet it’s not one anymore…maybe it’s even a mitzvah to be sad for the last two days. After all, the special mitzvos of Tishrei are going away, so what could we possibly be happy about?

Finally, one rainy day, I discovered a book on the dustiest basement bookshelf called Chess-Winning Strategies for Kids. After reading a few chapters, I finally figured out how to win the game. Boy, was Sholom surprised the next time we played!

“So the Torah is our Book of Orders,” I said, “and Hashem is telling us how to win the war. I guess the Torah really does make us happy.”

Just then, Freidel piped in. “Yeah,” she said, “but not only that—the instructions in the Torah come from Hashem, who loves us so much. And when we follow His precious Torah, we stay close and connected to Him.”

“And that’s why we celebrate on Simchas Torah,” said Pesha, “it’s the day we finish the whole Torah and start again.”

When we danced hakafos that night and the next, I had a lot to think about and be happy for. On Simchas Torah we followed along as the baal koreh finished the last parsha and started Bereishis. Of course, we filled our bags with more candy than we could carry home. But that was just for fun. I know what’s even sweeter than candy—the Torah and mitzvos.

Boy was I wrong, and I’m sure glad I found out in time. I can’t believe I didn’t realize this before.

It happened right after licht bentchen on Shmini Atzeres. Mommy reminded me to say the brachos of “Shel Yom Tov” and “Shehechiyanu”. Afterwards, I plopped down on the couch with a pillow on my face, making sure to be sad because Tishrei is almost over.

I wondered out loud, “Why do we say Shehechiyanu today? I thought we only say Shehechiyanu for good and happy things.”

“Esty,” Pesha answered even louder, “Tonight we’re going to dance with the Torah! It’s the most spectacular, fantabulous, happiest day of the year!”

“Well,” I said, “Not so happy. What is there to be happy about anyway, with Tishrei coming to a close?”

“Everything,” said Pesha in her matter-of- fact voice. “You know, the Torah is our Book of Orders. Imagine being a soldier trying to win a war for weeks and years. A soldier doesn’t know everything all by himself, he doesn’t know the best way to win the war. He needs instructions. So, imagine you’re a soldier and the battle is really hard to win, but then you get instructions from the Commander-in-Chief, the one who knows everything about the war and exactly the right strategy to win it.”

“Exactly!” Pesha exclaimed. “We’re all soldiers winning the war against the foolish Yetzer Hara, and it’s not easy. But Hashem gave us all the instructions—they’re right inside His Torah.”

Yom Tov is over. Tomorrow I’m going back to school. Freidel and Pesha are busy packing their suitcases, and my room is getting emptier and emptier by the second.

There are six and a half months till the next big Yom Tov—Pesach. How am I going to last until then?

I know exactly how—we talked about it at the Yom Tov table yesterday. The mitzvos that we do in Tishrei are like energy bars for our neshama that keep us going for months on end. All the davening, mivtzoim, and simcha of Yom Tov stay inside of us, keeping us strong.

My cousins’ suitcases are bursting, and you know, my suitcases are all packed up, too, with neshama energy to last a year.

Best of all, I learned that simcha is like a super- powered bombshell missile against the Yetzer Hara. Armed with a weapon like that, I’m ready for any challenge. Long, dark winter, you can’t scare me!
