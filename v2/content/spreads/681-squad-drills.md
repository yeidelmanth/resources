<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">In a war, the first step toward victory is to expose the enemy’s strategy. This way, we avoid falling into his trap. In Tzivos Hashem we face the trickiest enemy: the Yetzer Hara.</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">Here’s his strategy: First, the Yetzer Hara plants negative thoughts in our heads. Then, those thoughts grow into worse</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">feelings that can lead to even more dangerous actions.</span>

One of the ways the Yetzer Hara tricks us is by telling us to be apathetic. This means to be detached and uncaring about what’s happening around us (different from ‘'pathetic’, which means ‘in a pitiful or sorry state). The Yetzer Hara’s goal is for us to shut out other people’s feelings and be insensitive. He tries convincing us that if we think of others, there won’t be any space left to feel our own feelings or properly care for ourselves.

Look at the following scene and identify where the Yetzer Hara could influence you to be apathetic towards others, and trigger your thoughts, speech, and actions.
