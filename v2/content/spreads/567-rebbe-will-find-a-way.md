One year before Rosh Hashana, Tatty flew to the Ohel with four members of our community.

Tatty believes that the best way to light up a Yid’s neshama is to bring him to the Rebbe, and that’s exactly what he was doing. He farbenged with the group during the entire flight, and continued farbrenging as they drove their rental car to the Ohel.

“Remember,” he said as they neared the Ohel, “When we go to the Ohel, we must know one thing: that we are standing in front of the Rebbe.”

Tatty wanted his guests to be fully prepared for their first visit to the Ohel. He even invited our Zeidy, Rabbi Tuvia Teldon, to greet them at the Ohel with some inspiring words.

“A visit to the Rebbe is very special,” Zeidy told the men. “In fact, it can change your entire life!”

One of Tatty’s guests, a Yid named Mr. Rice, was listening carefully. Even though Mr. Rice wasn’t yet frum, he was a proud Jew.

As Mr. Rice entered the Ohel, he was very moved to see the thousands of letters from people all around the world turning to the Rebbe for brachos and inspiration.

He stood there in awe, knowing that the Rebbe was right there listening to his tefillos. Rebbe, he thought, sighing heavily, my son Nathan is planning to marry a non-Jewish girl. Please bless me to have Jewish grandchildren from him.

Mr. Rice finished davening and left the Ohel. Ten minutes later, he got a call from his wife. She was crying hysterically.

“What’s going on?” Mr. Rice asked, fearing the worst.

“It’s Nathan,” she cried. “He just called me. Cindy knocked on his front door ten minutes ago and said she doesn’t want to marry him anymore!”
