Baruch’s new employer, Reb Avraham, had a large vegetable farm and an orchard which he owned with his partner, Reb Azriel. Baruch agreed to take the job for three weeks, helping them prune trees and plant vegetables.

After the three weeks were up, Reb Avraham didn’t want to let Baruch go. And so he suggested that Baruch continue working for him as a watchman in the orchard.

Being a watchman would be a dream job for Baruch. He could sit in the watchman’s hut, enjoying the nature of the blooming orchard and the peace and quiet to learn for most of

the day, all while earning a steady income throughout the summer.

So he agreed. For the first few days, until after his first Shabbos at the orchard, Baruch was happy at his job.

But then, early on Sunday morning, Baruch approached Reb Avraham and told him he had to leave.
