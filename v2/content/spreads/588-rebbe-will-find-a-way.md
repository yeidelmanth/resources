He left the Ohel and began to drive to work. Less than an hour later, he received a call from Tatty. “Are you by any chance missing your tefillin?”

“Yes!” exclaimed Mr. Glick. “This is unbelievable. I just asked the Rebbe for a bracha, and here you are, calling me that you found my tefillin!”

He was even more amazed when he came to pick them up and discovered that somehow, the protective plastic and front page of his siddur were both missing.

“How were you able to find me without my phone number?” he asked, stunned.

Tatty told him about the incredible hashgacha pratis that had led to this moment: Eitan, who had found the tefillin, worked as a tow-truck driver. Right after giving Tatty the lost tallis bag, he went to his first job of the day at a certain warehouse.

hard to find the owner of the lost tallis bag, but with the Rebbe’s brachos, it was returned the very same day it was lost. Hashem had planned every detail perfectly, making sure it would all work out!

No one could figure out who the tallis bag belonged to. Through the Rebbe’s brachos, the challenge was solved with incredible hashgacha pratis!

One day, Tatty got a call from Eitan, a man who lives nearby. “Rabbi, I see a tallis bag on the road! What should I do?”

“Bring it to the Chabad House and I’ll try to find the owner,” said Tatty.

When Eitan arrived with the bag, Tatty saw that it contained a tallis and tefillin, but no phone number. However, embroidered on the bag was a name: Yoel Glick.

That didn’t help much—Yoel is an extremely popular name among the many Satmar chassidim living in our area. However, Tatty felt that since it was the special day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, he would put in every effort to fulfill the mitzvah of hashavas aveida, no matter how hard it would be.

Tatty called a few Satmar Yidden he knew, but they couldn’t help. “Do you know how many people have the name Yoel? You’ll never find him,” they said.

Meanwhile, on the other side of town, Mr. Glick had discovered that his tallis bag was missing. He went out to look for it, but he couldn’t find it.

Mr. Glick wasn’t worried. His phone number was written on the protective plastic bag, as well as on the cover of his siddur. He was sure that whoever would find the tallis bag would call him.

However, the clock was ticking. He needed tefillin right away so he could daven before going to work.

Where will I find a pair of tefillin? He wondered. I know! The Lubavitcher Rebbe’s Ohel is on my way to work. I’ll daven there and borrow someone’s tefillin.
