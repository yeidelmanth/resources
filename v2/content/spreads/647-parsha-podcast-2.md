chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Well, somebody—we’ll pretend his name was Shmerel—stole Zalman’s set of expensive fountain pens. When Zalman noticed that it was missing, he started to cry. So the teacher asked if anyone saw the pens, and everyone said no. Including Shmerel.

Because later, he finally did admit it. But that’s when it got terrible: The teacher told Shmerel to apologize, and he refused. He said that anyway, Hashem clearly planned for Zalman’s stuff to be stolen, so even if it made him cry or be embarrassed, it wasn’t Shmerel’s fault.

Yikes! Sounds like the Yetzer Hara took major control over there! He made him steal, and then gave him an excuse not to feel bad about it!

Interesting … Yossi, I think this story comes up in this week’s parsha. Are any of our guest options not so famous?

It’s … it’s m-my goat! Oy vey, my Zl-zlata has … gone m-missing! She must have been … stolen. Zlata would never leave me like that … She has always been a good, fai-faithful goat.

You don’t underst-stand. Zlata provided my family with m-money. Every day, she would give delicious milk! Some, my family would-would drink, and some I’d s-sell. Now, my family has no milk and no money … Oy, oy, oy … Hashem, what do I do?!

That is really so tough. But don’t worry, Reb Shalom. Hashem will certainly make sure that you get paid back for everything when the thief is found!

Oy … oy, I love Hashem, but … but, how do you know that it will happen that way?

Well, it’s in this week’s parsha. If someone steals something and then says they didn’t, and then they finally admit that they did, the Torah says they have to pay everything back, and more!

Well, like Shmerel in Yossi’s class, the thief might think that he’s not responsible for the pain he caused. But, that’s not true. Hashem says that the thief has to pay back what he stole, plus pay for all the sadness and loss that he caused the owner.

So, he’ll have to make up for all the money and milk that I didn’t get while Zlata was gone?

Yes! He has to think about all the pain he may have caused, and do what he can to fix it. So, he pays back what he stole, plus a fifth of what it’s worth. If he stole something worth $100, he ends up paying back $120.

That’s really neat. So, Hashem doesn’t just want you to get your goat back. Hashem also wants the thief to understand what he did to you, and make sure to do everything possible to fix it.

This is … tr-truly comforting. I’ll go tell the rav what happened, and ask him how we can track down this thief.

And uhh, guys, I think I owe a huge apology to Mommy for all the cookies that I’ve sneaked straight from the cookie sheet. Maybe I can bake her a big batch now to make up for it!

I also sneak cookies and I know how much time Mommy puts into them. Let’s make a big batch together.

I’ll help with the oven and clean up! So many cookies is sure to make a mess.

And now, let’s ask all the other chayolim to think about it: How can you be more careful not to cause any anguish to another person?
