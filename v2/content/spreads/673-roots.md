It was decided that the boy should stay home to calm down, while Baruch and Yitzchak Shaul went into town to see what they could find out about the rav. When they got there, they found the streets of town, which would normally be busy on a Friday afternoon, nearly empty. The shul was packed with people— men, women, and children— saying Tehillim. Baruch and Yitzchak Shaul understood, from the sighs and whispers around them, that the rav was unconscious.

A chazan led the Tehillim from the front of the room. He chanted it out loud, one passuk at a time, and then the minyan repeated after him. This particular chazan clearly took his job very seriously. Many “oy vey”s and choke-sobs embellished each and every line.
