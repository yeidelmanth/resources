Tatty’s shlichus felt unimportant. Was it worth the effort?

Tatty and Mommy always dreamed of being shluchim of the Rebbe. But when they couldn’t find a place to go on shlichus, they decided to move to Postville, Iowa, where Tatty became a shochet.

Never giving up on his dream to be a shliach, Tatty worked hard to help the Yidden in Postville connect to Hashem. He taught Chassidus to the frum families and workers in town. He traveled to faraway places to meet Jewish people who weren’t frum and teach them about Yiddishkeit. Tatty also sent bochurim on mivtzoyim to find more Yidden and help them do mitzvos.

Over time, Tatty officially became the shliach of Northeast Iowa, and we opened a Chabad House with a library and visitor center.

But sometimes, he felt unsure. While other shluchim were making big events for hundreds of people, Tatty and Mommy were only helping one or two Yidden at a time.

Does our shlichus really matter? He wondered. Maybe we should move somewhere else, where we’ll be able to have a bigger impact.

One year on Gimmel Tammuz, this question was bothering Tatty a lot. He went to the Ohel

and poured out his heart to the Rebbe. Please, Rebbe, show me if our shlichus in Postville is making a difference.

After leaving the Ohel, Tatty checked his phone and noticed an email from someone named Rabbi Martin Rosenfeld. He didn’t recognize the name at first, but when he read the email, his eyes widened in surprise.

Rabbi Rosenfeld was writing to let Tatty know that he was printing an article in The Jewish Link, a publication sent to families across the Tri-State area of New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut. Titled, “Making a Difference in the World of Kiruv,” the article was all about how Tatty’s shlichus had inspired him!

In the article, Rabbi Rosenfeld described his visit to Postville years before:

I was once in Postville and met a Chabad shliach named Rabbi Schimmel. I asked him, “How do you spend your day as a shliach if everyone in your community is already frum?”

Rabbi Schimmel told me what he does for the local Yidden. Then, he described how he travels to nearby towns to find Yidden and share Torah and mitzvos with them—one Yid at a time.

Submitted by Yosef Yitzchok, 12 Shneior Zalman, 9 Meshulam Zushe Schimmel, 7

Rabbi Schimmel’s shlichus taught me that you can’t wait for Jewish people to come to you; you need to go out and find them. He inspired me to do this in my own life and look for Yidden whom I can help.

Rabbi Rosenfeld ended the article by encouraging others to do the same: Anyone can make a difference and help another Yid. You just have to go out and look for them.

Tatty stared at his phone screen in shock. Just minutes after asking the Rebbe to show him if his shlichus was making a difference, this email had arrived at his inbox!

Not only was a Yid (whom Tatty barely remembered!) deeply inspired by our shlichus, but he was encouraging thousands of others to learn from Tatty and do the same!

All of Tatty’s doubts vanished in an instant. He began working with much more chayus, knowing that his shlichus was making a big difference.

This year, Tatty felt doubts creep up again. Are we really needed here? he wondered. Maybe we should move somewhere else, where we could have a bigger impact.

At that very moment, Tatty received a message from Hachayol, asking if they could publish this story in an upcoming magazine.

This is amazing! Tatty thought. Once again, the Rebbe heard my difficulties. And once again, he found a way to tell me that my shlichus is making a difference, however hard it may be.

And of course, once again, Tatty resolved to continue our important shlichus in the way that the Rebbe wants—one Yid at a time.
