Tanchum felt tears in his eyes. “Well, it looks like the Rebbe's brachos already came,” he said. “Just before you called, my white blood cell count suddenly rose back to normal!”

Baruch Hashem, Tanchum was able to spend Shabbos with his family. And from that day on, he started getting better and better, until he had a complete refuah shleima! Today, Tanchum lives a happy and healthy life, surrounded by his children and grandchildren.

We will always remember how the Rebbe found a way to uplift Great-Uncle Tanchum when he was feeling so sad and alone. As the Nasi of our generation, the Rebbe is deeply connected to each one of us. He knows what we are going through and gives us the ko’ach we need to get through our challenges!

Tanchum’s test results were suddenly a lot better. The doctors couldn’t explain it!

My great-uncle, Tanchum Pinson, lives in Kfar Chabad with his wife, Esty, and their children.

One day, Tanchum started to feel unwell. At first, he thought it would go away, but he kept on feeling worse and worse. Soon, he was in so much pain that he had to go to the hospital.

The doctors took a lot of tests to see what was wrong. They came back with the results: “You are sick with a disease that lowers the amount of white blood cells in your body. White blood cells are very important because they help fight off germs. You will need a lot of treatments to help you get better.”

For the next while, Tanchum was in and out of the hospital for tests and treatments.

One Friday, Tanchum lay in his hospital bed feeling very weak. “Your white blood cell count is dangerously low,” the nurses told him. “You will have to stay in the hospital until they get higher.”

Tanchum felt tears coming to his eyes. He wanted so badly to spend Shabbos at home with his family, and now, he would have to be in the hospital. He felt very much alone.

A few hours later, the doctors came in to do some tests. Imagine their surprise

when, out of nowhere, the numbers looked much better!

“We don’t understand what happened,” they said. “This morning, it was dangerous for you to leave the hospital. And now, your white blood cell count looks fine. You can go home for Shabbos!”

Tanchum was so happy! A few minutes later, his phone rang. It was his wife, Esty.
