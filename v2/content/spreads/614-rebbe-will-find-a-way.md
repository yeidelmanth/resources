Rabbi Hershkop immediately agreed to lend him the precious dollar and letter, understanding that if Tatty was asking, things were serious.

After Shabbos, Tatty went to pick them up, and showed them to Mommy. They read the letter together, which contained a beautiful bracha from the Rebbe.

Then, looking at the dollar, they were overcome with emotion. The date written on the dollar was Chof-Hey Tishrei, Mommy’s due date!

Tatty and Mommy felt that they had received a direct and clear bracha from the Rebbe.

They finally felt calm and confident, trusting that everything would go well.

Sure enough, only after Shabbos was over, did Mommy have the baby. A healthy baby girl, born on Chof-Hey Tishrei—the exact date that was written on the Rebbe dollar!

Even though the dollar was given more than thirty years before, the Rebbe’s bracha was as powerful as ever. We are so lucky to have a Rebbe who continues to be with us and help us!

With such a clear bracha from the Rebbe, Tatty and Mommy could finally feel calm.

After weeks of preparations, it was finally the Seder night! Mommy lit the candles, covering her face as she whispered the bracha and davened for our family.

I stood beside her, admiring the gleaming candlesticks and beautifully set table. I couldn’t wait to say the Ma Nishtana and share the divrei Torah I had learned in school. But little did I know that the Seder night wouldn’t go as planned…

Tatty left for shul, and Mommy began putting the finishing touches on the table.

That’s when things started to go wrong. Mommy, who was pregnant at the time, began to feel a lot of pain. Tatty was quickly called back home, and they rushed to the hospital.

The doctors did a lot of tests to understand what was going on. “The baby’s life might be at risk,” they said. “Mrs. Gniwisch, you will need to be on bed rest. No leaving the house. No lifting heavy things. You must sit and rest as much as possible to protect the baby.”

Tatty and Mommy came back from the hospital feeling worried. Would the pregnancy go well? Would the baby be healthy?

On the Shabbos after Pesach, Rabbi Avremi Hershkop came over to farbreng after shul. During the farbrengen, Rabbi Hershkop mentioned, “Many years ago, my wife had complications during a pregnancy. But soon, we received a dollar and letter from the Rebbe for an easy pregnancy and healthy baby. And baruch Hashem, everything went smoothly!”

Tatty was stunned. He hadn’t said a word to Rabbi Hershkop about what had happened to Mommy or about the concern for the baby. What hashgacha pratis!

“Would you mind if I borrow that dollar for a few months?” he asked.
