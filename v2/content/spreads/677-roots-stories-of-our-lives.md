After a few minutes, Nikolayev, red-faced and panting, managed to get close enough to grab at the boy’s coat. But Chayim Shimon swiveled around and pushed both of his palms, hard, into Nikolayev’s chest. The man’s eyes went wide as he crashed, arms flailing, down onto the floor.

“Ahhh!” Nikolayev howled, rolling and clutching his head.

Kuzitzky hesitated for just a moment before charging at Chayim Shimon. Chayim Shimon jumped onto Kuzitzky’s back, climbed it as though it were a ladder, and pulled himself up so that his knees were on Kuzitzky’s shoulders. Then, he wrapped his left arm around the man’s thick neck, using his right to punch him in the jaw again and again.

Kuzitzky’s legs wobbled and then buckled. Kuzitzky and Nikolayev were now both on the ground, moaning in pain, when the governor and the priest returned to the room.

Kuzitzky eased himself into a sitting position, leaning heavily on his left arm. “Thank goodness you’ve returned! This savage Jew-boy attacked

us the moment you left! Look what he’s done to us!” He gestured wildly at his jaw.

Nikolayev nodded vigorously, wincing as he touched the

newly-bruised bump on his forehead. “Completely unprovoked! We were just trying to get some water, and he launched himself at us like a wild animal!”

Chayim Shimon stepped toward the governor, a confident smile on his face. “Honorable Sir,” he

said, “I respectfully suggest that you question the guard who remained in the waiting room throughout this incident. He witnessed everything.”
