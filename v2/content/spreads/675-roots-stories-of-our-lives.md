Chayim Shimon squeezed through the narrow gap and found his way to a large brick oven which shared a wall with the meeting room. Behind it, where the builders had left a narrow space to access the chimney, lay a small space—just wide enough for a slight eleven-year-old boy.

Chayim Shimon folded himself into the cramped spot, the rough brick pressing against his knees and his chest. Dust tickled his nose but he fought back a sneeze, careful not to be heard by any of the men trickling into the next room.

If Chayim Shimon pressed his ear against the wall, he could just about hear their voices. The young boy’s knees were already beginning to cramp, and a protruding brick dug into his shoulder blade, but his curiosity far outweighed his discomfort.

Finally, Reb Yehoshua pounded on the table and cleared his raspy throat. “I suggest we start with a first step that we can all agree upon: to declare a communal fast, beginning tomorrow. We must pour out our hearts to the Ribono shel Olam and beg for His mercy. After that, we will try to meet with city officials, and plead our case.”

The discussion continued for another hour or so. They discussed the details of the fast, suggestions for how to make secret plans with government officials, and even rough plans for preparing the community to leave Liozna altogether, if, chas v’shalom, it would be necessary. Finally, the meeting was over.

Chayim Shimon pressed himself further into his hiding place as the men filed out.

When the last footstep faded and the door creaked shut, Chayim Shimon emerged from behind the oven. His legs were cramped and his back ached, but his goal was crystal clear. As he slipped into the frozen night, Chayim Shimon’s young mind was already formulating a plan.

Shabbos evening arrived with yet another howling blizzard. The snow fell in thick sheets, making it impossible to see more than a few feet in any direction.
