bumped into me outside my house a few minutes ago, and handed me a letter with this phone number written on—” “Is this Sruli?” the voice asked nervously. “Yes,” I answered. “Please don’t tell my parents that I gave you that letter,” he begged. “They’d be angry if they knew I found out.” “What are you talking about?” This was all very confusing. “What’d you find out?” “That we can’t afford to make a Pesach Seder this year.” I could hear in his shaky voice that he was struggling not to cry. “Ever since my father lost his job a few months ago,” he continued, “life has been really hard. I even heard my parents say…that…that we may get kicked out of our house… because…because rent…is too expensive!” “Oh no!” I heaved, “What’s your name?” “Avremi. I’m a chayol from Cleveland, Ohio. I didn’t know who to call for help. The chayolim in Cleveland look up to you. You’ve shown us time after time with your mivtzoyim projects that even a kid our age can make a big difference. You’re our hero. So…when I came to Crown Heights for Purim, I knew I had to figure out a way to reach you. Sorry again for knocking you over like that.” I felt terrible. I wished I could say, ‘Avremi, don’t worry. I’ll ask

my father to write you a check from Rafi’s money…or from Senator Goldberg’s funds…or from the recent anonymous check…’ But I couldn’t. There was nothing left. “Sruli?” He asked. “Are you still there? Is…is there anything you can do?” “Yes, Avremi, I’m here.” I said, hoping to sound supportive. Such a huge burden for a boy my age to carry! After a pause, I added, “What a tough situation you’re in. I’m sure there is a way I can help you and your family. Just a moment while I look into something.” I needed to think. I reviewed the information and repeated the details to myself. Lost job, rent money, Pesach Seder, thrown out of home, huge expenses, no job, Pesach seder, home. “I GOT IT!!” I burst out with excitement. “You do?” he asked, with new hope in his voice. “Yes. Tanya ba’al peh.” “Tanya ba’al peh?” Avremi asked. “I don’t underst—” “Trust me. It’ll help. I’ll call you back tomorrow at this time, after making some arrangements. Okay?” “Okay, Sruli. I’ll be waiting by the phone. Thank you so much.” The next day after school with my parents’ permission, I went to Tzivos Hashem Headquarters. Rabbi Benjaminson’s office. “A Learn-a-Thon in Cleveland, Ohio?” Rabbi Benjaminson repeated. “Yes!” I answered. “It’s a great way for the chayolim to raise funds for Ma’os Chitim by doing what they do best—learning Torah.” “So the chayolim need to find sponsors for what—every page of Tanya they learn ba’al peh?”

Rabbi Benjaminson verified as he jotted down the idea. “Actually, what if they find sponsors for every line learned?” I suggested. He stopped writing and looked up. “How much will every line cost the sponsor?” “I guess that decision is up to the person who is sponsoring the line. The higher the amount, the better. Right?” For the next minute or two, he wrote some more details in silence. Finally, he looked up to me and said, “Sruli, this idea is one of your best yet.” “It is?” I had not expected to get a green light so quickly. “I just tried to think of what would be the fastest way to help this family. But, of course, not to embarrass anyone, the chayolim can’t know which family the Learn-a-Thon is going to support.” “Or families,” Rabbi Benjaminson added with a knowing look. “What do you mean?” I asked. “Sruli,” He replied, “I think you’re onto something bigger than you imagined. Unfortunately, Avremi’s parents aren’t the only ones struggling to make ends meet. That’s why this Learn- a-Thon idea is so amazing. Every chayol across the globe can raise Ma’os Chitim funds to support families in his own area.” And that’s exactly what happened. When details of the Learn-a- Thon reached Tzivos Hashem bases everywhere, parentswere approached, neighbors were asked, and relatives were eagerly called on as well. Sponsors all around happily donated for this vital cause. Adding even more excitement to the Learn-a-Thon, Rabbi Benjaminson offered two grand prizes: one for the Tzivos Hashem base that raised the

most money, and one for the base that learned the most lines ba’al peh. On the final day of submissions, Tzivos Hashem hosted a livestream with a constantly-updating grand total, as well as a log of each base’s progress. 200 lines here, $3,000 there. I even managed to learn 20 lines baal peh, raising $400 from my aunts and uncles. It was a personal record breaker for me. Believe it or not, the chayolim from Cleveland, Ohio won both prizes. Baruch Hashem, Avremi’s family received the much-needed funds for their seder, and then some! Rabbi Benjaminson also contacted Cleveland’s chessed organizations, who anonymously reached out to Avremi’s parents for additional financial support. A few days later, the phone rang in my home. My little brother called out to me from downstairs. “Sruuuuuuuli. Rabbi Benjaminson from Tzivos Hashem is on the phone for youuuuuuuuuu.” I ran downstairs, thanking my brother for the message. Rabbi Benjaminson asked if I could come to his office for an emergency meeting. I got my parents’ permission, and off I went. “Sruli,” Rabbi Benjaminson began, “Let’s be honest here. The Adler and Goldberg accounts are both bone- dry.” With my head held up high, and with a voice only half-full of confidence, I said, “I guess this means you don’t need my help with Tzivos Hashem any more. Is that why you called

me in?” “Oh no,” Rabbi Benjaminson answered. “We actually need your help now more than ever.” “You do?!” Joy took over once more. “But how can I help? I mean, didn’t you just say that the bank accounts are empty?” “Exactly. We want to show chayolim that the possibilities of shlichus are endless. Anyone can make a real difference anywhere—not just chayolim with ties to large bank accounts. With that in mind,” he continued, “we want you to broadcast a special segment at the next Tzivos Hashem rally.” Wow!! Let me tell you, I may have been interviewed on national television, planned far-reaching mivtzoyim campaigns, and dealt with a massive budget—but appearing at a Tzivos Hashem rally has been I’ve been wanting to do for a very long time now. I couldn't wait!
