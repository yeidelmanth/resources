“Berel!” his friend Shea greeted him. “Someone came in here an hour ago and asked me to give this to you.” With that, Shea handed Berel a fat envelope.

Berel walked to a private corner of the room and peeked into the envelope. It was full of money! Counting the bills, Berel realized that there were three thousand dollars in the envelope—enough to pay up most of his debt!

“Who gave this to you?” he asked Shea, his voice trembling.

Minutes after davening at the Ohel, Berel’s tefillos were answered in a miraculous way.

Our Zeidy, Rabbi Mechel Karczag, helps run a shul in Boro Park called Beis Menachem, where he teaches Chassidus to all types of Yidden.

One of the people who comes to Beis Menachem is a man named Berel. Berel isn’t a Lubavitcher, but he loves learning Chassidus and the Rebbe’s teachings.

Unfortunately, Berel ran into hard times. He had lost his job, and wasn’t able to pay his rent for months. He owed his landlord four thousand dollars, but he didn’t have enough money to pay even some of that amount.

“Unless you pay up soon, I’ll have no other choice but to send you and your family out of the house,” his landlord told him.

Berel felt sick with worry. Where would his family live if they were thrown out of their home? He tried staying calm on the outside so that no one would know

the hardship he was going through, but he couldn’t hide his feelings from Zeidy.

“What’s wrong?” Zeidy asked him. Berel, feeling broken and afraid, told Zeidy the whole sad story.

“Let’s go daven at the Ohel,” Zeidy suggested. “The Rebbe cares for every Yid. His brachos can surely help!”

Late that Thursday night, Zeidy and Berel traveled together to the Ohel.
