Tatty couldn’t believe it. He had just written to the Rebbe, and now he was reading about the same question with the same number! It felt like a clear answer from the Rebbe to buy the house and spread Yiddishkeit further.

But then, the phone rang. It was the real estate agent. “Sorry,” he said. “Someone else offered more money. The house will go to them.”

Tatty’s heart sank. But soon enough, on Chol Hamoed Sukkos, Tatty found out that the higher offer didn’t work out. The house was still for sale! Tatty put in his offer again and hoped for the best.

After Yom Tov, Tatty found out that his offer was accepted. The house was ours!

Tatty raised enough money, and construction began. Whenever there were difficulties with the construction, Tatty wrote to the Rebbe. And each time, the problem was solved.

One day, a big challenge came up. Tatty wanted to tell the Rebbe right away, but he hadn’t yet gone to the mikvah. So, he asked a friend, “Can you please write to the Rebbe for me?” And guess what? Before Tatty's friend even started to write, the problem was already solved. Just thinking about writing to the Rebbe had brought brachos!

Today, our bigger Chabad House helps even more Yidden connect to Hashem. We do our shlichus with pride, knowing that the Rebbe is with us!

The Rebbe’s clear answer gave Tatty the courage to expand our Chabad House.

We are shluchim in Munster, Indiana, a small town with a close-knit Jewish community. Many Chabad Houses have their own separate buildings, but ours was in our house! We had davening in our living room, Yom Tov meals in our dining room, and Yidden were always coming over for classes and programs.

Right next door to us was an empty house that had nobody living in it for six years. Tatty and Mommy always said, “If only we could buy that house, we could make our Chabad House bigger!”

But the house was never for sale. Then, one day, it finally went up for auction! Tatty was so excited—until he saw the price. It was just too high.

A little while later, he heard that the house still hadn’t sold. So, Tatty made an offer for $70,770 and waited to hear back.

That Shabbos, Yud-Daled Elul, was going to be Tatty’s birthday. On Thursday morning, he drove us to school in Chicago like he always does, went to the mikvah, and then came home to write a letter to the Rebbe.

In his letter, Tatty told the Rebbe: Our community is very small, and I don’t want to

borrow money and go into debt. Should we really make our Chabad House bigger?

Tatty also works for A Chassidisher Derher, a magazine that helps Chassidim strengthen their hiskashrus with the Rebbe. That day, he sat down to edit an article called “Hora’os V’hadrachos—Guidance from the Rebbe.”

And guess what the topic was? Money challenges on shlichus!

Tatty read how the Rebbe told people to spend money for shlichus, but not too much. Then, he saw a story about how the Frierdiker Rebbe never had money in the bank, but also never went bankrupt!

After that, Tatty read something amazing. A chossid once told the Rebbe that he was $70,000 in debt.

Tatty froze. That was almost the exact amount he had offered for the house!
