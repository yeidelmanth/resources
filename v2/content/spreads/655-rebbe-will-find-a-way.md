Mommy asked me questions fast. “What color is this? How many fingers? Can you see this?”

On Monday, the doctors checked me again. There was no sign of a head injury, and the specialist said it looked like nothing had ever happened!

Baruch Hashem, my eye healed in a miraculous way, but Mommy says that the biggest neis was how our family felt through it all: watched over, cared for, and never alone. Knowing that the Rebbe is with us gives us strength in every challenge.

When the doctors didn’t know if I would get better, Tatty and Mommy trusted in Hashem.

It all started on Erev Shavuos, when I was almost three years old. I was going to light Shabbos candles for the very first time! Mommy gave me a sparkly-pink crystal candleholder, and I lit the candle and said the bracha all by myself.

Yom Tov was so much fun. My brothers and I made a Shavuos play for the guests, and everyone loved it. On Isru Chag, we went to play at the park.

While we were playing, two big kids zoomed into the playground on huge bikes. They were going way too fast. One bike flew into the air and landed right on me, hitting my eye.

Hatzalah came right away. My eye was badly hurt, and later, I started feeling very sick. Mommy rushed me to the doctor, and soon I was in an ambulance going to the children’s hospital for head injuries.

The doctors did a lot of tests, but I still couldn’t open my eye. My head hurt, and I didn’t feel good.

When we went home, I felt worse, so Mommy took me back to the hospital. On the way, she sent an email to the Ohel, telling the Rebbe that she knew he would find a way to help me.

Mommy and Tatty were strong in their bitachon. They were so sure about the Rebbe’s bracha that they even sponsored a seudas hoda’ah in our shul for that Shabbos!

I came home from my second hospital stay right before Shabbos. My eye was still closed, and the doctors didn’t know if it would get better.

On Shabbos day, Tatty went to shul for the seudas hoda’ah, and Mommy stayed home with me. While we were eating the meal, I suddenly felt something change.
