<p class="lead">On the day a king is crowned, trumpets are blown and everyone celebrates. The loud sounds announce that this king is the ruler. On Rosh Hashana, when we blow the shofar, we’re doing exactly that: We are crowning Hashem as King—over ourselves, and over the entire world!</p>

That’s why we also blow the shofar every day of Elul. Each blast helps us prepare ourselves to accept Hashem’s rulership; not just for the one day of Rosh Hashana, but for every day of the year.

But here’s an important question: When you crown a king, you usually bring him gifts. So what kind of gifts can we possibly bring to Hashem?

Hashem doesn’t need money, food, or fancy objects. What brings Him pleasure are the Torah and mitzvos that we do.

What is the best present to give Hashem on Rosh Hashana?

Something expensive or impressive.

Torah and mitzvos done with excitement and simcha.

With trumpets and the shofar’s sound, raise your voices before the King, Hashem.

Fill your day with learning Torah, saying pesukim, and doing mitzvos with chayus and joy. Every time you do even one of those things, you are giving Hashem the most wonderful gift. These gifts give Him tremendous nachas and pleasure, and He gives even greater gifts back to you and all of Klal Yisroel.

<span class="mc">The most important gift of all is preparing yourself to live the year in the right way, with Hashem as the focus of your life; your King.</span>

And when Yidden everywhere crown Hashem together, that brings the greatest bracha of all— the coming of Moshiach!

Why do we blow the shofar on Rosh Hashana if we’ve already done it throughout the whole month of Elul?

The Rebbe says that the best gifts for Hashem are Torah and mitzvos done with chayus and joy. Why does it matter how we do our avodah? Isn’t just doing it good enough?

What is one way you can show Hashem that you accept Him as your King this year?

One person starts by saying a small gift that they can give to Hashem, choosing something realistic and doable. Then they say “Our gift to Hashem is making sure to kiss the mezuzah.” The next person says the first gift plus adds on their own. “Our gift to Hashem is making sure to kiss the mezuzah AND bentching with extra kavana.” The next person continues: “Our gift to Hashem is making sure to kiss the mezuzah, bentching with extra kavana AND helping Mommy clear the table.” Keep playing until only one person can remember all of the gifts. Then when the game ends, make sure to keep all of your mini-hachlatos!

Ever got a grumpy gift? It doesn’t feel good to receive something that someone isn’t happy to give! To get through this maze, only go through a path of simcha and chayus—the way Hashem wants to receive our Rosh Hashanah gifts!
