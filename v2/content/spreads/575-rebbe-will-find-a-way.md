Susan went home and returned a little while later with a sealed envelope. “Here’s my letter,” she said, handing the envelope to Mommy. “Can you please send it to the Rebbe?”

B’hashgacha pratis, our uncle from New York was visiting us at the time. The next day, when he traveled home, he made a special trip to the Ohel to deliver Susan’s letter.

Some time went by, and soon Chanukah arrived. Susan came to help Mommy set up our Chanukah program. As they set the tables, Susan could hardly contain her excitement. She turned to Mommy with a big smile and said, “Rachel is five months pregnant! She was blessed very soon after I wrote to the Rebbe!”

While baking challah one week, Mommy noticed that something was bothering Susan. Was there a way she could help?

One of our favorite ways to help with our shlichus is through our Friday afternoon challah deliveries. The Yidden in our community love getting Mommy’s fresh, delicious challah, and we feel special sharing it with them.

Very often, a kind woman named Susan volunteers to help Mommy make the challah. As they measure, knead, and braid the dough, Mommy and Susan talk about all kinds of things. Over the years, they’ve become very close friends.

One spring morning, Mommy noticed that Susan wasn’t her usual happy self.

“Is everything all right?” Mommy gently asked. “I’m here for you if you want to discuss anything.”

Susan burst into tears. “It’s about my daughter Rachel. As you know, she wasn’t young when she got married, and I was thrilled that she would finally be able to start a family of her own.

“But time is passing, and she hasn’t been blessed with children,” Susan sobbed. “That’s why I’m so sad. I wish that she could have a child!”

Mommy gave Susan a hug. “With Hashem’s help, she certainly will have a child.”

She then continued, “Susan, write a letter to the Rebbe. The Rebbe is the head of the Jewish people. When we connect with the Rebbe, it can bring tremendous brachos into our lives.”
