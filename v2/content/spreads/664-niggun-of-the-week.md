<h3>Makes-you-dance Niggun</h3>

"The notes of a niggun are like words for the neshama," Tatty Shir explains to Esther Miriam, who sits glumly near her harp. "Some niggunim help us express how much we love and long for Hashem. But this niggun does something different." He reaches for his flute.

Reb Leivik’s Hakafos Niggun

Tzvi, who were up in the attic looking through some old Simchas Torah flags, quickly come down to join the dancing.

From the first three notes, the air becomes charged. It’s Reb Leivik’s niggun!

Six-year-old Yossi jumps up from building his Lego sukkah. His twin sister, Yochi, who was in the middle of gluing sequins onto her Sefer Torah craft, decides to just take it with her. Shneur, three years old, also brings his Sefer Torah, which is made out of soda bottles. And Freidel and

"Wow," says Esther Miriam, when the niggun ends. "I wasn’t in the greatest mood before, but now I’m feeling much better!"

"Yes!" says Tatty. "Some niggunim

just fill us with light

and happiness and energy, and we have to dance. Especially now, when we will soon be dancing with Melech Hamoshiach!"
