So I re-read the last ten issues of Hachayol. Then I went to the kitchen and had five crackers with cream cheese. Then I brushed my teeth again. I went back to bed, and still couldn’t fall asleep. So I stood on my head for sixteen seconds, till I tumbled onto the floor with a loud crash.

My big sister Mushkie came into the room. She always wants to make sure I’m okay. She looked at me and asked, “What happened, Esty?”

“Nothing much,” I said. “Just trying to fall asleep.”

“Hmm…” said Mushkie, “I have a bedtime story that can help you with that.”

Now, usually, a good bedtime story gives me a good night’s sleep. But this one did not. It was about a little girl who wanted to steal her friend’s sparkly purple pencil and lie that it

was hers. Of course, she knew that it was an aveirah, but the big, mean, Yetzer Hara was too strong to fight!

Before you knew it, the Yetzer Hara started chasing me with all sorts of aveiros. First, he wanted me to eat the hotdog sitting in the fridge, even though I was still milchig from my snack. Then, he wanted me to jump on Mommy’s bed when she was sleeping. And finally, he wanted me to tell my teacher that I’m a five-star general in Tzivos Hashem—which is not true (yet!).

I woke up shaking and sweating. The Yetzer Hara had been just inches away from catching me… Thankfully, it was all just a nightmare.

If only I could abolish that terrifying YH once and for all! Sometimes that goal seems so far away, and I’m afraid that I’ll never win.

Boy, am I tired from such a busy Purim! I thought I would get to sleep in this morning, but Tatty woke us up even earlier than on a school day! Of course, when I heard Tatty say he was packing up the van for the nursing home, I jumped out of bed and into my costume.

As I flew down the stairs toward the front door, I saw Mushkie sitting on the couch. “What?” I said. “Aren’t you coming?”

“Where, to the nursing home? Nah, everyone’s going to think we’re so weird, dressed up funny and handing out shalach manos. I’m staying home this year.”

“Wait a minute,” I said to Mushkie. “You mean you’re embarrassed?”

I knew I had a mission to do. I had to convince Mushkie to come with us. I thought and thought, and then I said, “Mushkie, you

know the story of Mordechai and the three cheder boys? They knew that Haman was watching them, but they were not afraid to act like Yidden and speak like Yidden, even in front of their biggest enemy. When we do the right thing, we don’t need to be afraid of what other people think!”

Right then, Mommy passed by. She heard what I said and added, “That’s right Esty. It’s the job of the Yidden to be a shining light for all to see. We can be a light for everyone just by practicing Torah and mitzvos wherever we go.”

Mushkie agreed to go if Mommy would come too. So the whole family went together to the nursing home this year. Tatty read the megillah, and we handed out shalach manos. Then we girls went to a room where there were only ladies, and we sang and danced with them. You should have seen the smiles on their faces! Everyone was so happy. Even the non-Jewish workers joined in our dancing.

The best part is that I forgot about my fears of the Yetzer Hara, and

Mushkie got over her fears of looking weird. And in the end, we both saw there was nothing to be afraid of.

Instead, we made our little part of the world brighter, happier, and closer to Geulah. A happy Purim was had by all.

Today, my little brother Yitzy was practicing his Purim skit with his friends. He asked me to come to the playroom and watch.

Yitzy was wrapped in a purple sheet twenty times his size. He was Mordechai, and his friends

The second boy said, “They make an evil plan, but it will be defeated; they make a plot, but it will not succeed, for Hashem is with us.”

The third boy said, “Until you are old, Hashem will be with you.”
