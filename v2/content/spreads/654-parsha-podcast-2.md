chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

I have a bunch of options for who to call this week, but … hmm … we’ve never called this person: Rabbi Binyamin Mendelson. I’m gonna call him.

Baruch Hashem! Better than ever. I was just checking on my miracle orange orchard.

I love miracles! Tell us more, tell us more! Please!

Well, years ago, we had just set up this new moshav called Moshav Komemiyus in Eretz Yisroel, and a wealthy Yid from America offered to plant an orchard for us. We were so grateful, since this would help us all with parnassa, but we had one condition which the Yid didn't like: we would only take care of the orchard if we could keep the halachos of shmita.

But he’s a Yid! Why was he upset about keeping shmita? The Torah says, “ּוַבַָׁשָׁנָה ַהְַׁשִׁבִיִעִת ַׁשַּׁבּת ַׁשָּׁבּתֹון ִי ְִהְֶיֶה ָלָָאֶָרֶץ— and in the seventh year, there will be a Shabbos for the land.”

Was it because he was afraid you'd lose the orchard? He must’ve thought leaving the farm work alone every seventh year and learning Torah instead would hurt the land.

Precisely. He was willing to pay a lot of money to plant the orchard. He was very afraid of losing it all. Especially since shmita was just one year away.

But wait, did the Yid know that shmita year is a taste of what life will be when Moshiach comes? It's a year-long Shabbos when we don't have to work, and are free to learn Torah with all our chayus!

Just like when Moshiach comes! Hashem will take care of everything for us.

Ah, yes—a taste of Moshiach! I finally explained this to the man, and when he understood, he was very excited about it. If shmita was a taste of Moshiach, then he wanted to be a part of it. So in the end, we got the orchard!

You planted it and took care of it for only one year, and then left it alone? Did all the trees die?

Actually, our trees grew more beautifully and healthily than anyone could’ve imagined! It was a huge kiddush hashem! And six years later, when we were about to keep shmita again, do you know what happened?

Well, Hashem promises that if we keep shmita, we will grow enough in the sixth year to last for three whole years. So, did you get extra fruit?

Yes! We usually grow 700 baskets of fruit. But in תשפ”א (2021), the year before shmita, we grew over 2,000 baskets— a miraculous number! The American Yid couldn’t believe it; he kept checking again and again. But we know that it's just Hashem keeping His promise.

I wish I could keep shmita. But I don't live in Eretz Yisroel…

Oh, but you still can keep this mitzvah! Every Shabbos, we all keep shmita when we put our work away and focus on limmud Torah. Shmita teaches us that every spare minute we have can be used for Torah!

Well, as kids, we don't have to work at all. Everything is already taken care of for us. Does that mean that we have shmita every day?

Yes. This way, you can put all your energy into learning Torah with simcha, all the time! And when you encourage your friends to do so, you’re doing the mitzva of Ahavas Yisroel, as well!

And then we'll bring Moshiach, when it will be like shmita for everyone, forever!

Exactly! And now, let’s ask all the other chayolim to think about it: How will you keep the mitzva of shmita in your daily life?
