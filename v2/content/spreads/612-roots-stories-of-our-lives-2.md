Reb Moshe decided that the wisest plan of action was to buy pieces of art. One painting or sculpture could cost hundreds or even thousands of zlotys, and would be easier to store and transport than sacks of coins.

And so, Reb Moshe set out for Rome, the center of the fine arts. There were dozens of museums

and galleries there, all stocked with the works of the most famous artists. And in this way, under the guise of a business trip, he’d be able to store most of his fortune there, in Italy.

But before Reb Moshe left on such an extended trip, he wanted to be sure that his family was fully taken care of. He left them with food, money, and an address in Prague. This address was the home of Reb Moshe’s cousin; a place to which his family could run in case the worst happened, and they had to flee Posen while he was away.

When Reb Moshe got to Rome, in addition to his business dealings, he spent a lot of time at the Vatican library. The library was famous for its large archive of

old manuscripts, and Reb Moshe hoped to find a few sefarim there. During his visits, he got into many long discussions with the head librarian, Benedetto Pamphili\*. Benedetto was a man who appreciated intelligence wherever he found it, even in a Jew. And so although most people in the Vatican wouldn’t respect a Jewish scholar, Benedetto grew to admire the brilliance of Reb Moshe and his Jewish faith.

One day, a few weeks into his visit, Reb Moshe visited the library a bit later than usual. Usually, the

manuscript archive would be closed at this time, but he was hoping that Benedetto would make an exception.

Benedetto was sitting in his usual spot: behind a very long and shiny wooden desk flanked by two large bookshelves. And as always, Benedetto was bent over a towering pile of books, his glasses perched on the tip of his button-nose.

“Buena sera1 Benedetto,” Reb Moshe tipped his hat in respect as he approached.

Benedetto flinched, looked right and left, searching for the source of the voice. Then he looked ahead. A broad smile spread across his face as he saw Reb Moshe.

Other cardinals who were in the library at the time overheard the conversation, and came over to listen. By the time Reb Moshe was done, a small crowd had gathered around the librarian’s desk.
