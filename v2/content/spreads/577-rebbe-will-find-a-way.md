A few minutes later, still on the drive home, Tatty’s phone rang again. This time it was his friend. “You’re still looking to go on shlichus, right? Well, there’s a shliach in an African country who’s looking for a couple to come help him. If you’re interested, I can send you his number.”

The sign from the Rebbe was clear. But still, Tatty wasn’t sure. “I don’t know,” he told his friend. “Africa? I never imagined going on shlichus to such a strange place…”

“How can you say that?” his friend exclaimed. “This week is Parshas Lech Lecha, when Avraham Avinu went to a strange land because Hashem told him to, without asking any questions. How can you say no to this shlichus just because it’s far away and different from what you’re used to?”

Before Chanukah, Tatty flew to Ghana to meet the community. That Pesach, we visited together as a family and became the official shluchim to Ghana.

“Yes, it’s hard living so far from our family and friends,” we answer, “but we wouldn’t trade it for anything. We are so proud to be

the Rebbe’s shluchim here. There are only about five hundred Jews in Ghana and we hardly ever get to see other Lubavitcher

It was a strange and distant land. Would our family have the courage like Avraham Avinu to take the leap?

Our family is on shlichus in an African country called Ghana. You may wonder how we ended up in such a faraway place!

For a long time, Tatty and Mommy were searching for a place to go on shlichus. They looked into many options, but at the last minute, none of them worked out. In the meantime, they worked as teachers in the local Crown Heights schools.

It wasn’t easy to live like this. Tatty and Mommy didn’t feel settled, not knowing if they would move on shlichus or stay in Crown Heights.

As our family was growing, it became harder to live in our small Crown Heights apartment.

Tatty nodded. “Yes, maybe our shlichus is to stay in Crown Heights and work in chinuch.”

That Friday, Erev Shabbos Parshas Lech Lecha, Tatty and Mommy went to the Ohel to ask the Rebbe what to do.

“We both really want to go out on shlichus,” they wrote in their pan. “However, if the Rebbe wants us to work in chinuch, we will accept that as our shlichus b’simcha. Rebbe, please show us a clear sign for what to do next.”

As they were driving home from the Ohel, Tatty’s phone buzzed. It was our uncle, Rabbi Yanky Majesky, a shliach in Florida.

“I just saw an email on the Shluchim Exchange about a shlichus opportunity in a faraway country,” he said. “I thought I’d tell you about it because I know you want to go on shlichus.”
