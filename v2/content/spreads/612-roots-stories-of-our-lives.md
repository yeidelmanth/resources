Reb Moshe and the city council composed and sent out the fateful letter. The next night, after the last of the streetlamps had burnt out, all Jewish men in Posen gathered in that same side room of the shul. Each one drew a paper with a number on it out of a leather pouch. The number would decide their fate: Would they have to leave immediately, with hardly enough time to pack their bags? Would they have a few months? Or would they wait years in Posen before it was their turn to flee?

Reb Moshe’s number was one of the later ones; he estimated that he would only be able to leave in a few years’ time.

But in the meantime, Reb Moshe wanted to start transferring his money out of the country. Any cash that remained was in danger of being stolen or confiscated by the authorities.
