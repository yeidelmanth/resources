Then, Tatty passed a room with many people inside. “Is anyone Jewish?” he called out. Many of them excitedly said yes. Tatty helped them put on tefillin and blew shofar for them.

He continued to the next store, where the workers in the back usually told him not to bother them. “Come on in!” they called. Tatty put tefillin on them, and they were very emotional and thankful. Then, they all agreed to write letters to the Rebbe to ask for a brachos.

As Tatty continued down the street, he bumped into some people standing outside. “Are you Jewish?” he asked.

“Of course!” they responded happily. “We have an office right here.” They put on tefillin, gave tzedakah, and wrote letters to the Rebbe.

back to Yeshiva with an empty pushka. And this week, twenty-one Yidden had agreed to put on tefillin, and his pushka was filled to the top! Many had written letters to the Rebbe as well.

Ever since then, Tatty’s mivtzoyim was much more successful. He had asked the Rebbe for help, and the Rebbe answered him—right away.

Tatty went on mivtzoyim as usual, expecting everyone to send him away. Imagine his shock when suddenly, they were all excited to see him!

It was Friday afternoon, and that meant one thing: going on mivtzoyim! Tatty, a yeshiva bachur at the time, went to the Yidden on his route to help them do mitzvos.

His first stop was at a store. “Would you like to put on tefillin?” he asked the Jewish man behind the counter.

The next stop was an office where a Jewish man worked. But he also didn’t want to put on tefillin.

Tatty went to another store, where the workers in the back were Jewish. “We’re busy,” they called out. “Please don’t bother us.”

Tatty returned to Yeshiva feeling very down. While most of his friends came back excited having helped many Yidden put on tefillin, Tatty's experience was not so uplifting.

The same thing would happen week after week. No matter how hard Tatty tried, he would only get to put on tefillin on one or two people; at most, five.

This made him feel very sad. He wished he could help more Yidden put on tefillin.

One Friday morning in Elul, as he prepared to go on mivtzoyim, Tatty’s eyes suddenly focused on the Rebbe picture hanging on the wall. Feeling as if the Rebbe was looking straight at him, Tatty became very emotional. “Rebbe,” he cried out, “Please bentch me that my mivtzoyim should be with hatzlacha!”

On the way to mivtzoyim that day, one of the bochurim announced: “Let’s ask the Yidden who we’ll meet today to write letters to the Rebbe. Then, we can bring the letters to the Ohel when we go to the Rebbe for Tishrei!”

Tatty’s shoulders slumped. The Yidden I visit each Friday don’t even want to put on tefillin, he thought in despair. How will they ever agree to write a letter to the Rebbe?
