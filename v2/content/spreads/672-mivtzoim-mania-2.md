Local Yidden and tourists passed through it every day, and the ghetto was alive once more.

Then Chanukah came, and an elderly man walked in. He was about ninety years old. Tatty greeted him warmly and asked, “Did you ever have a bar mitzvah?”

A short while later, another elderly man came in. His name was Gabriel, and he was eighty- six. When Tatty asked him the same question, Gabriel gave the same answer: “No.”

Tatty didn’t wait. Together with the bochurim who had come to help for Chanukah, he quickly made a minyan and took out a Sefer Torah.

The narrow building stood quietly in the Roman ghetto. Its walls were hundreds of years old, filled with a long and painful history.

But now, something new was happening inside. It was our family’s Chabad House!

Slowly, the building began to fill with life, as people came to daven, learn, and take part in programs.

For the first time in their lives, the two elderly men put on tefillin. Then, they got aliyos to the Torah and celebrated their bar mitzvahs right there in the Chabad House. Everyone sang and danced with excitement and joy.

A few days later, on the last night of Chanukah, Tatty organized a small menorah lighting outside the Chabad House. He thought that only a few people would come, but when he stepped outside, he saw that an entire crowd had gathered!

Seeing that Gabriel was there, too, Tatty announced,

survivor, no less. We want to give him the honor of lighting the menorah.”

Gabriel stepped forward, and the crowd grew quiet as they looked. He lit one candle, then another, and another, continuing until all eight flames were burning brightly. The menorah stood tall and proud, in a place where, not so long ago, it wasn’t even safe to be a Yid.

After the lighting, Gabriel turned to Tatty and said quietly, “I need to tell you something.” He took a deep breath and began.

“When I was four years old, I lived right here in the ghetto. War raged on all around us, until the Nazis had taken over our city. But I continued to play outside with my sister, too young to understand the

was happening. He ran outside and began shouting loudly at us, ‘'I’ve been looking for you everywhere! Your mother is so worried!’

“He acted like an angry father and even slapped my sister to make it

look real. The Nazi stopped and asked, ‘'These are your children?’ The man answered, 'Yes.’ The Nazi nodded and let us go.”

Gabriel looked up at the building as he finished his story, his voice full of emotion. “That dairy shop was right here,” he said, pointing to our Chabad House. “Eighty-two years ago, this is the exact spot where my sister and I were saved.

“And tonight”, he continued, wiping away a tear, “I stood here as a proud Jew and lit the Chanukah menorah for all to see.”

The flames of the menorah flickered quietly against the moonlit sky. So many years had passed with so much darkness, but now, there was light again.

From then on, Gabriel kept coming back to the Chabad House. He returned every day to put on tefillin. In the place where his life had once been saved, his neshama began to shine once more.

Fellow Chayolim, this Chanukah, let’s all go out and help one more Yid light the menorah. A small flame can reach further than you imagine, and who knows what story it might complete. It’s up to us to spread the light!
