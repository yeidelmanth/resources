The clock had already struck midnight, but the girls decided to do a final status report and make sure that everything was in place for the next day's operation. Mivtzoyim supplies? Check. Metro cards? Check. Routes for each group? Check. Letter written to the Rebbe? Silence…

In all of the hustle and bustle, no one had taken the time to write about the campaign to the Rebbe, our general, and to ask for his bracha. “We can’t move forward without informing the Rebbe!” they all agreed.

“Look,” said one girl, “We all need to get some rest. Why don’t you two head to bed, and I’ll write to the Rebbe on our behalf?”

“It’s a deal,” replied the other two, and off they went, confident that with the Rebbe’s bracha,

their mission would be a success. And even though it was late, their friend made sure not to go to bed until the letter was written.

“Rebbe,” she wrote, “Please give us a bracha that everything will go smoothly, and it should be with hatzlacha.”

Girls were giving each other worried looks, and umbrellas could be seen everywhere. “Are we still going in the rain?”

But the organizers were adamant that nothing will get in the way of soldiers fulfilling their mission. “We have the Rebbe’s bracha,” they told everyone, “and we know that no rain can stop us.”

Outside, dark clouds were gathering, and a light drizzle was beginning to fall. Inside, however, preparations for the operation were in full swing. Girls were gathering from schools all over Crown Heights, picking up their gear, and joining up with their units. By the time the first team headed out at 12:30, the rain was gone.

The Mivtzoyim groups headed all over Brooklyn, spreading light in their specific target area. Group #7 was in Park

Slope, operating at full power. They even carried a secret weapon: in addition to their regular mivtzoyim supplies, they had brought along homemade challah to give every Yid a taste of Shabbos.

Soon, they spotted an old lady walking down the street. “Excuse me, are you Jewish?” they asked.

The girls were not deterred. “Would you like some challah?” they persisted.

The woman’s face lit up. “I love challah!” she exclaimed.

The girls continued walking down the street, happy to have found another Yid. They got to the end of the block when they heard someone calling behind them.

They turned around to see the old lady they had just met. “Girls, actually, I do want candles to light this week—in honor of Israel.”

The girls were amazed at how this woman’s neshama had been lit up from their small act of Ahavas Yisroel. Indeed, all over the city, Tzivos Hashem soldiers were illuminating the dark.

The battle plan called for the girls to head back to base at 2 pm. At 1:57, a light rain began to fall. By 2:30, all units were on their way back to headquarters. At 2:35, it started pouring.

The girls laughed together, sharing stories of the people that they met and the lives that they touched. Each chayol knew without a doubt that she had helped inspire another Yid that day—with the Rebbe’s bracha. Mission accomplished. Now it’s your turn, soldier. Get out there and spread the light!

The night was cold and the hour late. In a doorway on Kingston Avenue, three girls huddled together. Their attention was focused on a pile of boxes loaded with weapons. These weapons were not guns or bombs; the boxes were filled with Neshek—the Yiddishe weapon.

It was a Thursday night, and the girls were preparing for a large-scale offensive. The next day, 50 elementary- aged girls would be hitting the streets for a massive mivtzoyim campaign, to strike a blow against the Golus with weapons of light. They would be fully equipped with Ois B’Sefer Torah brochures, Sheva Mitzvos cards, pushkas, and of course, Shabbos candles.
