It was warm and sunny on the day that Baruch left Yanovitch. The sun had already melted the

winter’s snow like heated wax. Streams of water flowed down from the hilltops and flooded the dirt roads, turning Baruch’s path into a swamp. Each time he took a step, his foot sank ankle-deep into the mud. But Baruch hardly noticed the effort of walking. His mind was elsewhere; totally focused on the people he’d met and the stories he’d heard in Yanovitch.

After a while though, the trudging through the mud took its toll.

Baruch searched for a place to rest. He found it in a large branch, thick and wide, and curved almost perfectly into the shape of a seat. Thick leaves above it shaded the spot from the sun.

Baruch sighed deeply as he hoisted himself onto the wood and let his eyes close. He might have fallen asleep, if not for the clickity-clank of a horse and buggy that pulled right up to his perch.
