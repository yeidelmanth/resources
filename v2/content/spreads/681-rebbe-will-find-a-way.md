“What?!” Tatty exclaimed. “How will we find a different place to live in so little time?” With a heavy heart, he sat down and wrote to the Rebbe again. Is this a sign that we should buy the building, no matter the cost?

Exactly ten minutes later, Tatty’s phone rang. It was the real estate agent!

“Great news!” the agent said. “The owner is ready to sell the building to you—at the original low price you offered!”

Tatty and Mommy were thrilled! That very day, they went into contract for the building. The deal moved quickly, and on the day we needed to move out of our house, we got the keys to the building.

Thanks to the Rebbe’s brachos, Chabad of Prescott finally had a home.

Nothing seemed to be working out. Would we ever have a proper Chabad House?

Our family is on shlichus in Prescott, Arizona. When our parents first came to Prescott, they rented a house to live in. Because they didn’t own a big Chabad House yet, they hosted programs in the house and in other places around town.

In 5775 (2015), someone donated a lot of money—enough to buy a Chabad House!

Unfortunately, there was a problem. The owner wanted an enormous amount of money, a lot more than Tatty and Mommy could pay. Tatty offered a lower price, but the owner wouldn’t hear of it.

Tatty asked his friends what to do, but their answers confused him. “Borrow money from the bank so you can buy it,” some friends advised. Others, however, thought differently. “Don’t go into debt!” they warned, “it’s not worth it.”

All this back and forth made Tatty’s head spin. Looking for some clarity, he sat down to write a letter to the Rebbe. I’m confused, he wrote. People are giving me opposite pieces of advice. Rebbe, what should I do?

A short while later, Tatty heard that the owner of the building was planning to sell it to someone else. Okay, he thought. It must be hashgacha pratis that we’re not meant to buy it.

But in the end, that deal didn’t work out. The building was put up for sale again.

As soon as he heard this, Tatty wrote another letter to the Rebbe, this time asking for a bracha. Then, he called his real estate agent. “Can you tell the owner that we want to buy it?”

The agent passed on the message, but the owner didn’t respond.
