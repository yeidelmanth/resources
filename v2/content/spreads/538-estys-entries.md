It was hard not to talk about the surprise all Shabbos long. But like always, I had lots of smart questions at the Shabbos table, which is a good thing—otherwise I would have slipped for sure.

See, Yom Kippur is coming up and I wanted to know what it’s all about. On Rosh Hashana we went to shul and davened. How is Yom Kippur any different?

Tatty asked, “Do you remember going to shul last Yom Kippur?”

Of course I did. For Yom Kippur, we rent out the big building at the

other side of Blueberry Forest. That’s because a bajillion more people come to shul on Yom Kippur than any other day, and we need a ginormous room to fit everyone.

“So many Yidden!” explained Tatty, “The smart ones and the simple ones, the rich and the poor, the big and the little, all come together on Yom Kippur for one thing: to make Hashem our King.”

“But Tatty,” I said, “We did that on Rosh Hashana!! We already crowned Hashem as King!”

“That’s right,” Tatty said, “We did. And on Yom Kippur we finish that job, by sealing the deal. Like when you make a card for someone and put it in an envelope, and then you glue the envelope closed so

“That’s why,” continued Tatty, “at the end of Yom Kippur, we all shout together, “ׁשְמַע יִׂשְרָאֵל—there is only one Hashem!” Then we say “בָּרּוְך שֵׁם”, announcing that Hashem is the King of the whole universe.”

I get it now. On Yom Kippur, all the Yidden get together like one person with one job: to announce that Hashem is the One Ruler.

Through being one with each other, we can be one with Hashem, too. What a onederful day!

And after Yom Kippur, Mommy’s birthday will be a wonderful day too. I can’t wait to show her all the surprises we’ve got planned.

I can’t believe we managed to keep Mommy’s birthday surprise a secret. She wasn’t expecting it at all! We decorated the room with yellow daisies (Mommy’s favorite flower) and the yellow streamers and balloons that I bought. The best part was when we presented Mommy with our project: a huge yellow polka-dot scrapbook with eight pages. Each page has a picture of a different kid

in the family, and next to the pictures are letters we wrote for Mommy wishing her lots of brachos.

Mommy loved our present. And funny enough, she said what gave her the most nachas was that we all worked together to make it. Aidel was right—the best present for a mommy is seeing all her kids getting along with each other.

True, being nice to brothers and sisters is hard…we

never seem to all agree with each other. So why did this birthday plan work? Maybe because we all had one goal: to make Mommy happy.

Which makes me wonder— imagine how much nachas Hashem has when all His Yidden come together in shul on Yom Kippur, with one project: making Him our King.

There were all kinds of people in shul today. Sitting next to me was a lady with a huge purple hat and a glittery purse. I guess she didn’t know that we can’t bring purses to shul on Yom Kippur. But I helped her find the pages in the machzor and follow along with the chazan, and I told her when to stand up and sit down.

At the end of Ne'ila, we all said “Shema Yisroel,” “Baruch Sheim,” and “Hashem Hu Ha’Elokim” really loud together. We announced once and for all that Hashem is King over us. The verdict has been stamped and sealed. Finally, we did it!

The men and boys sang Napoleon’s March and danced. The lady with the purple hat looked a little confused, so I smiled my biggest smile, and told her that we won over the Yetzer Hara and this is our victory march.

Suddenly I heard the loud blast of the shofar, and everyone shouted, “לְשָׁנָה הַבָּאָה בְּיְרּושָׁלָיִם—Next year in Yerushalayim!”
