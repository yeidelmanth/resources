Focused Attacks

There’s an extremely important first step that every army takes, way before making an attack: getting to know the enemy! This includes sending spies or spying devices to find out how they fight, what weapons they have, and where they are stationed, so that you can best prepare yourself. Getting to know the enemy before you attack is called “reconnaissance”, or

“recon” for short.

In Hashem’s army, we also take time for recon.

“Preemption” is when an army is proactive, meaning they don’t wait to respond to an enemy attack, but they go out and attack the enemy first. Offense, not defense. And in Tzivos Hashem, we want to attack the Yetzer Hara before it gets to us.

In Week 2, you will learn from the best of Hashem's soldiers: our division heads. How did they outwit their enemy?

Week 1: Reconnaissance

This includes meeting with our squad to discuss the sly tactics of the Yetzer Hara. How does the Yetzer Hara try to infiltrate (get into) our camp and win us over? What thoughts could he put into your head, what feelings can those thoughts create, and how might those feelings make you behave?

For example, if we are focusing on the southern border this month, we might discuss what the Yetzer Hara would say to you if your parents are out of town, and you want to stay up past your bedtime.

When you know the tactics of the Yetzer Hara, you can recognize an attack from him right away!

Week 2: Preemption

<span class="mc" style="color:#b56a10">For example, during the months focused on the southern border, you may learn what it says in Chumash and Midrash about Sara Imeinu’s consistency.</span>

In any army, the best way to win a battle is by making a pre-emptive strike on the enemy. Attacking the enemy before the enemy gets to attack you gives you the upper hand! We must do the same in Hashem’s army, and leave no room for the Yetzer Hara to win!

Here’s how it works:

There will be four Avos Squad meetings every month, covering the four main steps in a war, which are: getting to know enemy tactics, learning the battle strategy, making the attack, and regrouping to see how you’ve done. These four battle steps are also called reconnaissance (pronounced “rekon-isins”), precedence, attack, and evaluation.

Just like children get their genes from their parents, we all inherit ruchiniyus genes from our Avos and Imahos. These are special kochos that have been passed down to every Yid, giving us the ability to be like them.

In Week 3, you will make a battle plan of how to apply what you’ve learned from that month’s division head to be powerful soldiers of Hashem.

For example, if you are conquering the south, you can learn from Sara Imeinu’s tactic of being consistent in our avodas Hashem, and how we too can stand strong against our Yetzer Hara before he tries getting us to behave in a scattered way!

Week 4: Evaluate

After an attack, the army comes together to evaluate. This means to discuss how the attack went, and how they can do even better next time.

During the last meeting of each battle, you will discuss your squad’s hachlata from Week 3. What went well? What do we still need to work on? Is there a more powerful or effective way to fight this battle? After evaluating together, you are ready to apply what was discussed, and move on to another focused attack!

You will learn more about these steps.

For example: Month One will focus on the Yetzer Hara’s tactic of convincing us to focus only on ourselves, and how Sara Imeinu teaches us to overcome this battle and be Hashem-centered. Month Two will focus on another tactic of the Yetzer Hara, and how Avraham Avinu teaches us in the sections below.

Throughout each month, you will go through all four battle steps—one step per week—as you fight a specific tactic of the Yetzer Hara with guidance from one particular division to fight, and so on.

Week 3: Attack!

And here’s where the action comes in:

Your Squad will pull up the scenario from back in Week One (which described the sly strategies of the Yetzer Hara) to discuss amongst yourselves. You can now proceed with the fight, and identify which good thoughts in your mind can create good feelings and cause good actions to win against the enemy.
