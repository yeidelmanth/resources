<p class="lead">In this week’s parsha, Moshe Rabbeinu says that the “revealed things” are for us. What does this mean? Rashi explains that when something not good is done out in the open, it becomes our responsibility to take care Yid does matters to every other Yid.</p>

It’s not enough to say, “I’m doing fine.” Even if you learn Torah, do mitzvos, and behave the way Hashem wants, your job isn’t finished! You are connected to other Yidden. You have the power—and the responsibility—to help them do the right thing, too.

Which choice fits the message of this week’s parsha?

As long as I’m doing the right thing, that’s good enough.

What other people do matters to me. I can help them do the right thing, too.

The revealed things are for us and for our children.

*Rashi: To get rid of any* *evil that’s among us.*

<span class="mc">But how do you help others do the right thing? Chayolim, this is done by showing and explaining that there is a Creator of this world: Hashem. He made every single detail, and He takes care of all of us, too.</span>

For example, when you stop and make a Bracha before taking a drink, you show that Hashem is the one Who gives us everything we have and need.

And when you live this way, others will take notice! When you speak about Hashem naturally and behave as the Torah says, you influence the people around you. They’ll see how wonderful it is to be in Hashem’s Army, and they, too, will want to behave as a Tzivos Hashem soldier. This is how you take care of the “revealed \[not-good\] things” and add more light to the entire world.

Why does Hashem make Yidden responsible for one another? And are there better or worse ways to influence each other?

How can saying a bracha help remind someone about Hashem?
