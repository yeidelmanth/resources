As the days passed, Tatty continued feeling better. However, he still needed a machine to help him breathe.

To his dismay, the doctors didn’t have good news. “It seems that there is permanent damage to your lungs,” they told him. “You’ll probably need to be connected to an oxygen machine for the rest of your life.”

A few hours later, another friend called. “I’m going into the Ohel right now. How are you feeling?”

Tatty told him the sad news. “Please ask the Rebbe for a bracha that the damage to my lungs should go away and I should be able to breathe on my own, without an oxygen machine.”

Five minutes later, the doctor came in and looked at the breathing monitors.

“Wow, your breathing just got much better! If things continue this way, you’ll be able to go home without the machine.

“And you know what?” the doctor continued, “Let’s do a test right now.”

The doctor unplugged Tatty from the oxygen. For the first time in days, Tatty took a breath for himself! He passed the breathing test with flying colors.

His roommate, Shmuly, was amazed. “I heard that the Lubavitcher Rebbe does great miracles, and now I see it with my own eyes!”

We were all thrilled to welcome our Tatty home the following day. As we hugged and kissed him, our hearts filled with gratitude for the Rebbe’s brachos that brought our Tatty back to us healthy and well, baruch Hashem!

Tatty lay in his hospital bed with beeping machines all around him. Would he ever be healthy again?

When Covid began, Tatty got very sick. Eventually, his health got so bad that he had to go to the hospital. With every passing day, his situation got worse.

Things were not looking good when Tatty received a call from his friend, Sholom.

“I heard about your situation,” Sholom began, “and I wanted to tell you about the Sefer Torah we’re writing to help heal people who are sick with Covid.

“We’re doing this because many years ago, the Baal Shem Tov had a Sefer Torah written to stop a magaifa (epidemic). The Rebbe also said that buying a letter in a Sefer Torah brings health and success. Would you like to buy a letter?”

Just twenty minutes later, the doctor walked into the room. “Sir, I’m happy to tell you that for the first time since you’ve arrived at the hospital, your condition is improving!”

A week later, Tatty was feeling well enough to leave isolation. Now he had a roommate—a chassidish man who was also sick with Covid.

Tatty and his new roommate, Shmuly, began to speak. Shmuly mentioned that he hadn’t put tefillin on all week because of how sick he was feeling.

As the Rebbe’s chossid, Tatty knew what he had to do. Even though he himself was feeling very weak, he got out of bed and helped Shmuly put on tefillin.
