In Liozna, there was a Chassid called “קֶענֶער דֶער חַּיִים—Chayim Who Can”. He was poor and had a hard life, but he was always smiling. Whenever anything difficult would happen to him, he would say “I can!”

Reb Chayim once explained what he meant with those words. It was a hora'ah he had learned from the Alter Rebbe on a piece of Gemara: In Gemara Maseches Nidda, it says that when the neshama is in shamayim—before it is born into a body—it’s made to promise that it will be a tzaddik. The word for “they make it promise—מַׁשְּבִיעִים", has the same letters as the word "מַׂשְּבִיעִים- they give it the ability.” The Alter Rebbe explained that this means

our neshamos are given the ability to overcome all of the challenges that we will encounter in life!

Chayim der Kener took this to heart. Whenever something not good happened to him, he would remember that his neshama was given the ko'ach to handle it. He

would smile and shout “I can!”, no matter what challenge he faced.

Chayolim, all of our neshamos were given that same ko'ach. We are all able to face any challenge or struggle in life. Hashem gave us the ability to overcome it even before we were born. Let’s always remember that “we can”!

Just like on every Yom Tov, we don’t say tachanun on Pesach Sheini. But here’s a difference between Pesach Sheini and other Yomim Tovim: Usually, besides not saying tachanun on the Yom Tov itself, we also don’t say it during mincha of the day before, which is Erev Yom Tov, as a preparation. However, we do say tachanun during mincha of Erev Pesach Sheini (Yud-Gimmel Iyar). Here’s why:

Pesach Sheini is really like Erev Pesach, rather than Pesach itself. This is because it’s not a second chance to celebrate the entire Yom Tov; it’s specifically a second chance to bring the Karban Pesach. The Karban Pesach is brought on Yud-Daled Nissan—Erev Pesach—and Pesach Sheini is exactly one month later, on Yud-Daled Iyar. Both times, the karban is brought on Yud-Daled and eaten that night, on Tes-Vov. We see that Pesach Sheini is already more like an “erev” itself—and we do say tachanun in mincha on the day before an “erev” (i.e. Erev-Erev Yom Tov).

However, it’s interesting to note that the Rebbe discusses the night after Pesach Sheini—the night of Tes-Vov. The Rebbe says it’s possible that we should not say tachanun in Krias Shema she’al Hamita that night, because it’s still a special time; it’s when the karban was actually eaten.

How is Pesach Sheini different from all other Yomim Tovim? It’s the only Yom Tov in the Torah that was created because we Yidden asked for it!

Here’s what happened: When the Yidden were in the midbar, some people couldn't bring a Karban Pesach because they were tamei. But they also didn’t want to miss out on this precious mitzvah! So they asked Moshe: “נִגָרַע לָמָה— why should we miss out?” Give us a second chance! When Moshe told Hashem what they wanted, Hashem agreed to give them another opportunity! He said to bring the Karban Pesach exactly one month later, on Yud-Daled Iyar. We call that day Pesach Sheini.

The Rebbe shows an important lesson here: If the Yidden at that time did everything possible so they wouldn’t miss out on just one mitzvah, how much more so nowadays, when there are so many mitzvos that we can’t fulfill without a Beis Hamikdash, should we demand from Hashem to bring the Geulah now! נִגָרַע לָמָה?!

The theme of Pesach Sheini is that it’s never too late to fix what we’ve done. Even if we make mistakes, there’s always a second chance. In fact, Hashem gives us second chances even if we mess up on purpose! As soon as we do teshuva, we can come close to Him again. It’s never too late to change.

The Rebbe points out that Pesach Sheini happens in Iyar, not in Nissan. Nissan is the month of miracles, when Hashem takes us out of Mitzrayim in a wondrous way. Iyar, however, is the month of avodah, when we work hard on our middos.

This teaches us a deep lesson: Sometimes, Hashem gives us extra-special brachos and energy in a “Nissan” way. But if we miss them, we can still make them up— with extra effort and working on our middos, we can get a second chance.

Do you miss eating matzah once Pesach is over? Well, there is still one more day when we’re supposed to eat it: Pesach Sheini! On Yud-Daled Iyar, we eat matzah to remember this second chance that Hashem gave us to bring the Karban Pesach. But, even though on Pesach we eat matzah at night, our minhag on Pesach Sheini is to eat it during the day, when the karban was brought on the mizbei’ach (as you read in the previous section). However, the Rebbe also mentioned the minhag to eat matzah on the night following Pesach Sheini, the night of Tes-Vov, which, as you know, is when the karban was eaten.

Can you think of three things that are called “שֵׁנִי—second”? There’s a saying of Chassidim about what those three “sheini’s” are:

1\. The Nefesh Elokis: Our neshama is called the “הַשֵׁנִית נֶפֶׁש— the second neshama”, because it comes in addition to our Nefesh Habehamis.

2\. The Cheder Sheini: Many Chabad shuls have a second room called a ׁשֵנִי חֵדֶר, where Chassidim could spend a long time davening and doing hisbonenus (thinking deeply about Chassidus) undisturbed.
