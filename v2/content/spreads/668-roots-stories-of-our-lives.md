It was late one night at the shop; so late that all the streetlamps in Hatinka had burned out. Reb Dovid Leib, who had just finished working, held a candle in his right hand and a bag of shoes in his left as he headed out the door. Earlier that month, he had seen several neighborhood children—the milkman’s twin boys and the widow’s

daughter—walking barefoot, even though the weather was starting to turn cold. Every night since then, after the day’s orders were done, he had used bits of wood and cloth from old projects to make them new shoes. Tonight, he had finished with all three pairs; Baruch Hashem, the poor children wouldn’t go shoeless this winter.
