Hi there! We’re the Levin Family: Basya, Mimi, Chaim and

Yossi—chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Parshas Bereishis! Who should we bring to our podcast? Adam or Chava?

Come on, Yossi. You can’t be serious. The moon is sooo far away, and it’s not even a person! What could the moon teach us?

Didn’t the Rebbe say that we have to learn from each day of creation? Well, today is Wednesday, the fourth day of the week. And the moon was created on Day Four, so let’s find out what we can learn from Day Four.

He’s right. We do have to learn what Hashem, our Commander-in-Chief, made each day, so that we can learn from Hashem’s example. Plus, the moon is a good example, because the Yidden are compared to the moon.

Okay, okay, let’s call the moon. I just hope there’s good reception in the moon’s atmosphere.

Hey, hey everyone! Thank you for bringing me to the Parsha Podcast. You kids are awesome—I listen to your podcast every week on my long trips around Earth. But what do you want from me? I’m just a little moon from the big dark sky.

We want to know what we can learn from you. Why did Hashem make you?

Hmm, it’s a very simple story from a very simple moon, but if it’ll help you, I’ll tell it.

Well, Hashem made me to be ‘the little light. My job is to come out at night when it’s very dark, and share my light.

Good question. Since I don’t have any light of my own, Hashem gave me a special power: I can reflect light from the big sun! So when it’s dark, and the sun is far away, I can still come and bring the sun’s light into the world.

Whoa. I think I just counted three things we can learn from you.

Number one: The Yidden are compared to the moon, and just like the moon is small, we are also called small. And just like parents love and get special nachas from their small children, Hashem loves and gets nachas from every Yid.

Ooh, I think I know the second thing! Just like the moon lights up the dark night, we Chayolim in Tzivos Hashem need to light up the dark Galus. We spread Torah and mitzvos to every part of the world!

…And number three ties them all together! This is neat, listen closely: the little moon can only light up the world when it reflects light from the great big sun.

Us too! We’re just Hashem’s little children, but we can light up the night of Galus. How? Because Hashem gave us the power to reflect His great big light into this world.

Wow. Okay, let's talk practical. We know that we light up Galus by spreading Torah and mitzvos with mivtzoyim.

But how do we reflect Hashem’s light the way the moon reflects the sun’s?

Oh, you chayolim do it all day long, by doing mitzvos the Torah way. When you make a bracha on kosher food, say Modeh Ani in the morning, and Shema three times a day—

—And all the mitzvos in our Tzivos Hashem missions!
