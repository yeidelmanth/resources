chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Who's our guest for today? Basya, did you say you arranged it?

Yup! Mommy, Mimi, and I were on mivtzoim, and we met this lady who Mommy said would be perfect for our Sukkos podcast. I don’t really get why, though.

Yeah, she was just saying a bunch of history things. I think that’s her job— to know stuff about history. She was talking about victory marches or something. It sounded smart, but I didn’t really get it. I was kind of distracted by her giant green glasses.

Well, let’s call her. It says here that her name is Mrs. Duchstein.

Hi, Mrs. Duchstein! This is Mimi Levin. Are you ready to join us in recording our podcast?

Well, Mrs. Duchstein, our mother said that you had something important to teach us? Maybe the history you were talking about with her?

Well, according to my research, whenever an army would win a battle or conquer a city, all the soldiers would march through the city holding up their weapons and celebrating their victory.

Wouldn’t they want to just rest after their victory? Fighting a war sounds exhausting.

Well, according to my research, the armies were so proud and happy of what they accomplished that they had more than enough energy to celebrate and parade their weapons around.

But weapons are scary! Why couldn’t they leave them behind?

Well, according to my research, they knew that they couldn’t have won without weapons. And they weren’t scared of the weapons which they were holding, because those weapons helped them win against their enemy.

So they celebrated with their weapons to show how important they were.

Well, according to my research, that is exactly the case.

That’s all really great information, but what does that have to do with us?

Yeah, our mother said that there was a really important lesson you could teach us, but I don’t see the connection.

This is a special episode for Sukkos, so it’s got to have something to do with that. But Sukkos isn’t celebrating winning a war, is it?

Well, according to my research, the Lubavitcher Rebbe said that the arba minim are like weapons. Instead of rifles, we have a lulav; instead of bullets, we have an esrog; and instead of spears and daggers, we have—

Well, according to my research, the biggest enemy, which we all have, is our Yetzer Hara. He’s a tough enemy indeed, but the arba minim aren’t the only weapons we use to fight him. There are a lot more.

All mitzvos are our weapons! Like learning Torah, having Ahavas Yisroel, and making brachos.

Yeah, that makes sense, Chaim! But unlike the weapons that other armies use, our weapons win wars without hurting anybody.
