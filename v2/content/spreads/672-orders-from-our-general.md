<p class="lead">Can you imagine what it was like for Yaakov to live with Lavan for so many years? When Yaakov sent messengers to Eisav, he explained: “All this time, I lived like a stranger.” Yaakov meant that he didn’t get comfortable in Charan; he never considered Lavan’s house to be his real home.</p>

Lavan’s house is compared to Olam Hazeh. Yaakov was hinting to Eisav that he never got comfortable with all the gashmiyus things of this world. While most people care about having lots of money or getting pleasure from nice shoes, toys, or games, Yaakov didn’t think those were important. This world was not his real home.

Instead, Yaakov cared about Torah and mitzvos. That’s how he tried to spend most of his time. Torah and mitzvos were like Yaakov’s “home”

that’s where he felt most comfortable.

Later in the parsha, the Torah tells us that Yaakov built a house for himself, but for his cattle, he made little huts, like sukkos. A sukkah is much less sturdy than a house. We see from here that Yaakov considered “himself”—his neshama and ruchniyus avodah—the priority. And his “cattle”—his wealth and gashmiyus things—were much less important.

And by putting his neshama’s needs first, Yaakov accomplished something amazing: he managed to keep the entire Torah, even while living in the home of the rasha, Lavan!

\[Yaakov said,\] “I lived with Lavan.”

“And I \[still\] kept all 613 mitzvos.”

<span class="mc">We, too, must look at the world the way Yaakov did. When we remember that our neshama is what’s important and our gashmiyus is just there to help, we’ll be sure to change the world into a beautiful home for Hashem!</span>

How are Yaakov's challenges in Lavan's home like the challenges we face today?

Why is it hard to make Torah and mitzvos our priority?

What does it look like to treat our neshama’s needs more important than our gashmiyus?

What is most comfortable for a Yiddishe neshama?

Having lots of fun and expensive things.

Living with Torah and mitzvos as our real home.

Yaakov kept 613 mitzvos while living with Lavan.

Go around in a circle and everyone names a mitzvah. Keep going around without repeating. If you can’t think of something, you are out! Keep playing until the table agrees on who the winners are.

Pretend you are a home designer. Can you draw a gorgeous bedroom using these examples?

Yaakov built a home for himself—his neshama—and a hut for his cattle.

Can you match the items to their shadows? Then draw a line from each into the proper home.
