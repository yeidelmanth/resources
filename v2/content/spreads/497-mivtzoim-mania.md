But then, we weren’t just talking to one guard anymore. Because, suddenly, we were surrounded by twenty guards. Twenty very big, strong guards. With weapons. And handcuffs. Who simply appeared out of nowhere. “You’re under arrest!” announced the biggest guard with the twirliest mustache you’ve ever seen.

Within seconds, all three of us were handcuffed. Gulp.

“I can explain! I can explain!” Yossi cried, wiggling his hands to break free.

“Mr…?” Began Tatty, politely, looking at the twirly mustache man.

“Mr. McCormack. I am so sorry about this disturbance. You see, my son and his friend here are in the army of G-d. This is a weapon free army. We are simply here to blow the shofar and add light to the Jewish residents here at the hospital!”

Within moments, the handcuffs were all off. “Well, my mother was Jewish,” said Mr. McCormack gruffly. “But I’m only half.”

“I knew it!” exclaimed Yossi. Yossi knows everything somehow.

“Well,” said Tatty. “You are 100% Jewish! Would you like to hear the shofar in honor of Rosh Hashanah?”

And that’s how, before we even entered the hospital building, we had already blown shofar for three Jewish people.

Some time later, after blowing shofar for six more grateful patients, the sun began to set and it was time to go home. We were walking down a long hallway towards the stairwell, faces glowing from such a smashing day, each lost in our own thoughts, when my ears suddenly pricked up. “Mr. Adler…Room 312…Critical condition…Just admitted five minutes ago…”

I must be dreaming! I wasn’t hearing straight. It had been a long day. I shook my head out of my reverie and ran down the stairs, three at a time, the words I had just heard all but forgotten.

Little did I know what those words would mean… and how my life was about to change, forever!

“Sruli!!” said Ta, shaking me from my spaced- out-ness, “We need to daven mincha and do tashlich if we want to get to the hospital before shkia.”

I looked up from my bowl of pareve ice-cream soup, and sighed. Most of my family had already bentsched and were saying tehillim on the couch. “Ta… My stomach is so full…” I groaned. “How will I be able to walk an hour and a half to the hospital? And I’m sooo tired…”

“Sruli, isn't your friend Yossi Cohen relying on us? And all the Yidden in the hospital? How will they be able to crown Hashem as King - to hear shofar - if not for you!?”

But then I thought about Yossi, who was indeed waiting for us to pick him up to join us on mivtzoim. I thought about all of the precious Yidden in the hospital, who had no one at all to blow shofar for them. And I thought about how, when I heard shofar just that morning in 770, I had committed with all my koach to be a soldier in Hashem’s army.

I thought about how the children in Hashem’s army have a special power that is needed to bring Moshiach! I jumped up on my chair and pumped my fist into the air.

“I am a soldier of Hashem! I have a mission to do! I gotta put on my mivtzoim sneakers, grab my Tzivos Hashem water bottle, and then off I go!”

“General Sruli, not on the dining room chairs, please!” laughed Mommy from behind her giant tehillim. Too late. My ice cream soup was now an ice cream carpet. Whoops.

“I’ll clean it up, don’t worry,” offered my older sister Mussi. “You have an important mission to do!”

“Don’t forget your Tzivos Hashem general hat!” little Leibel called after me as I ran up the stairs to my Cheder Tzivos Hashem. Good point!

After mincha and tashlich, we set out in the direction of the hospital. Tatty quizzed us on all of the reasons we blow shofar (I knew three!), and Yossi sang ‘We want Moshiach Now’ at the top of his lungs. I can’t say it didn’t make me feel a little shy, but eventually I joined in. Because after all, a soldier going to battle has an anthem to sing, no?

The hospital was huge, with so many entrances. Ambulances were driving in and out, a stretcher was being wheeled into one of them, and sirens wailed in the distance. We walked towards a bustling doorway with a red awning.
