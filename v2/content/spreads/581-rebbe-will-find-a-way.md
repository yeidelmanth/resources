Tatty thanked his friend and couldn’t wait to give the dollars to our family.

When I woke up the next morning, Tatty greeted me with a big smile. “Yossi, I have a very special present for you—a dollar from the Rebbe!”

My eyes opened wide in surprise. I ran to my room, brought out the letter I had written to the Rebbe the night before, and read it out loud to everyone.

My family was speechless. They had no idea I had written this letter.

I held the dollar from the Rebbe close to my heart, feeling that the Rebbe was right there with me. He had heard my tefillah, felt my pain, and sent me the encouragement I needed to continue when I was feeling so uncertain.

Now that I knew that the Rebbe believed in me, I was able to believe in myself. I suddenly felt strong enough to take the Chidon test

In the end, even though it wasn’t easy, I stayed in Chidon and took all the tests. I never gave up, because I knew

that the Rebbe was proud of me and wanted me to continue.

I feel so lucky to be a chossid of the Rebbe, who cares for each of his chassidim. With the Rebbe at our side, we are unstoppable!

It was the night before the Chidon test, and I was ready to give up. I probably wouldn’t pass the test anyway…

I’ve always wanted to join Chidon, but never thought I could. I was worried that learning from the Yahadus book and taking the tests would be very hard, because here in Brazil, we speak Portuguese, not English.

But last year, I decided to try anyway, and joined Chidon. To be honest, it wasn’t easy. Sitting and learning for so long was a challenge, and Mommy wasn’t always available to help translate the English for me.

I did the best I could. But on the night before the first Chidon test, I felt very unsure of myself. I was nervous to take the test in English, and I didn’t think I would know all the answers.

After supper, I told Mommy how I felt. “I want to give up,” I said sadly. “I don’t want to take the test tomorrow. I probably won’t get a good mark anyway.”

Knowing how hard I had worked to learn the material, Mommy tried encouraging me. “Yossi, I hear how nervous you’re feeling. You’re ready to give up. But maybe try taking the test anyway! Even if you don’t get a good mark, at least you’ll know that you tried your best.”

Before going to sleep, I wrote a letter to the Rebbe. Rebbe, I don’t think I’ll get a good mark on the test tomorrow, so I’m thinking of not taking the test. If you want me to continue being in Chidon, please show me a sign.

At the bottom of the letter, I signed, from your chossid, Yossef Yitzchak Sigal.

Mommy didn’t know that I wrote a letter to the Rebbe. She just thought that I went straight to sleep.

That same night, Tatty’s friend called. “I have a present to give to your family,” he said. “Come to my house!”

Tatty went right away. Imagine his shock when his friend gave him five Rebbe dollars!
