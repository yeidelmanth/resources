Some days, he would go straight to the beis midrash, spend the night there learning, and catch a few hours of sleep on his bench. Most days, Devorah Leah only heard her brother’s voice from the ezras nashim in shul, when she listened to him chant kaddish yasom.

Devorah Leah was the one who kept the household running. She did the chores, earned money by sewing, and cared for her ailing father. She made him cups of hot tea to warm his ever-cold body, and cooked up a fresh vegetable broth each day—the

only food that her father could comfortably keep down.

Often, Devorah Leah needed to stay up late into the night to finish it all. But no matter how late Devorah Leah finished her work, she made sure to do two things before she went to sleep. First, she would take out a sefer and learn for at least an hour or two. Second, she would take out a sheaf of papers and write. In her beautifully curved script, she began to transcribe all of the stories her mamme had told her.

Devorah Leah wanted to remember the stories forever. One day, when she would have children, she wanted them to know these stories as well. Devorah Leah also hoped that one day, she’d have the opportunity to show her manuscript to her brother, Baruch, and finish the job that her mother never got to do.
