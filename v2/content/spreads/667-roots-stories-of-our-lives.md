Despite the not-so-subtle pressure from Shimon and Etta over the next few months, Reb Dovid Leib and Mirel remained firm in their decision. Influenced by the works of Mussar and Kabbalah that he was learning, Reb Dovid Leib wanted a simple life of earning money through the work of his hands, without the pomp and honor of a rav. And Mirel wanted to join him on whichever path he chose.

Reb Dovid Leib told his in-laws that he’d learned nearly all he could about shoemaking from Feivel, and intended to open up his own shop soon.

They felt they had one last resort: calling upon their mechutan. The one person who could have an influence on Reb Dovid Leib, they figured, was his own father. And so, they sent a message to faraway Minsk, summoning Rav Tzvi Aryeh, the dayan, for a visit.

But despite their high hopes, when Rav Tzvi Aryeh arrived, he agreed with his son’s decision.

One day, a customer in the shop told Reb Dovid Leib about a tiny village just outside of Kalisk. It was called Hatinka. It did not yet have a cobbler of its own, the customer said, so Reb Dovid Leib could make a comfortable living there.
