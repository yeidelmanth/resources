As the winter nights grew longer, Baruch got into the habit of learning through the night. He liked the quiet, and it allowed him to keep his Torah scholarship a secret. During the day, when there were people around, Baruch stuck to reading from a Chumash and reciting words of Torah he knew from memory. It

was only at night that Baruch allowed himself to delve into the more complex sefarim. No one stuck around much after ma’ariv, so Baruch didn’t have to worry about prying eyes.

Baruch flinched when he heard the voice and the knock at the door. He quickly closed his Gemara and slid it to the other end of his table. Then, he leaned his head on his shoulder to make it look like he’d been dozing off.

With one half-opened eye, he turned to face the visitor. Both of his eyes flew open when he saw who it was.
