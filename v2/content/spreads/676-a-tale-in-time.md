Throughout Cairo and the surrounding lands, the Rambam was famous as both a great talmid chacham and a brilliant doctor. So when the sultan chose him to be his personal doctor, everyone was happy.

I used to be the sultan’s doctor, Hakim fumed. Now nobody thinks I’m important, all because of the Rambam!

Each day, Hakim grew more jealous. I must get rid of him, he thought.

He rushed to the palace and bowed low before the sultan. “Your Majesty,” he declared. “I have discovered that the Rambam is planning to poison you!”

“I will prove it.” Hakim’s eyes flashed. “Tell both of us that someone is trying to poison you, and order us to prepare a medicine that will save your life.

“All doctors know,” the evil man continued, “that a stronger poison can stop a weaker poison from working. The honest doctor will bring the strongest poison possible, because he wants to save you. But the one who is really plotting against you will bring a weaker poison, so that the poison in your food will still work.”

The sultan narrowed his eyes. “How will I know whose poison is stronger?”

“We will do a little test,” Hakim said, his smile widening. “I will drink the Rambam’s poison first, and then my own. If I live, it will show that my poison is stronger. Then the Rambam will drink mine, and then his own. If he dies, you will know that his poison was weaker, and that he was the one plotting against you.”

The sultan agreed to the plan. The next day, he called both doctors to the palace and told them about the dangerous test.

As the Rambam rode home, he wondered why Hakim looked so smug. Surely Hakim knew that the Rambam could prepare a stronger poison. What trick was he hiding?

But at home, there was no more time to wonder. His waiting room was full of patients and people with Torah questions. The Rambam helped each one, then davened ma’ariv, learned, and wrote late into the night.

Only when he lay down to rest did the Rambam understand Hakim’s plot. I know what to do, he thought.

The next morning, after davening and learning, the Rambam prepared a mixture of red wine and water. Then, he rode to the palace.

At the sultan’s signal, the two doctors exchanged bottles and drank both mixtures.

For a few moments, no one moved. Suddenly, Hakim cried out in pain and fell

“Your Majesty,” the Rambam said, “I didn’t kill Hakim. His own plan killed himself.”

“What do you mean?” The sultan asked in amazement.

“You see,” the Rambam explained, “Hakim drank a slow poison before coming to the palace. Then he gave me a harmless drink, expecting me to bring a strong poison. He thought my poison would save him, but would make me die.

“I realized his trick, so I also brought a harmless mixture. Since Hakim never received the poison he was counting on, the slow poison he had already taken caused him to die.”

The sultan laughed in amazement. “Rambam, you are not only the greatest doctor. You are also the wisest of men! From now on, you will be my advisor, as well.”

Indeed, the Rambam faithfully served the sultan as both royal doctor and advisor. Yet, despite the honor, he stayed humble; helping the sick, teaching Torah, and writing the sefarim that continue to guide Yidden today.
