chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

I was riding my brand-new blue bike, and then I fell, and—

How annoying, Basya! I have some colorful Band-Aids. Do you want to put a Band-Aid on the scratch?

Or a pretty blue sticker? I even have one that matches your bike.

No. It’s too sad. I love my bike. And now it’s scratched! My beautiful brand-new bike is ruined forever!

I’m sorry that happened to you, Basya. I really am. Maybe you’ll feel a bit better after we talk to our guest. That usually cheers you up.

Yossi, who are we calling today? I hope it’s someone nice and happy.

I’m calling Eisav. He was really rich. Maybe he’ll get you a new bike!

Eisav?! But Yossi! He’s not nice and happy—he’s scary!

Nah, I’m not that bad. In fact, I just made peace with my brother.

Hey, kid, calm down. I don’t even have an army anymore. They all left. Your Zeidy Yaakov scared them off.

I sure am! Listen, I was going to attack Yaakov to get him back for tricking me out of the brachos. And I thought I had the perfect chance: after he had spent twenty years in Charan, surrounded by resha’im like Lavan. I was sure that by then, he had lost his kedusha-power. But do you know what he told me?

Yes! Do you understand what that means?! “ּגַרְּתִי” has the same letters as “תרי”ג”! 613! He kept all of the 613 mitzvos even while living with resha’im. Do you know how powerful that makes him?

When he said “ּגַרְּתִי”, he meant that the whole time he was in Charan, he was there in a way of “גֵירּות”, like a stranger. He didn’t become part of the people; he knew he was just traveling.

He did. He built a house for himself and his family to live in and learn Torah. But for his gashmiyus belongings, he only made temporary huts. That’s the thing: Yaakov kept all of his belongings at a distance. To him, they were just a bunch of stuff that he needed while traveling. His only real concern was his family’s ruchniyus life. And so, his kedusha-power remained with him stronger than ever.

The Mezritcher Maggid would say that golus is like when you’re traveling and staying somewhere random. In your home, you want everything to be perfect, but you don’t care as much when you’re travelling. You know it's just temporary.

Right. Torah and mitzvos are ruchniyus, so they last forever. But everything else is just temporary.

Unless we use gashmiyus things for ruchniyus. ‘Cause that’s the whole point! That’s what brings Moshiach!

Y’know, I guess it’s not so bad that my bike got a scratch. It’s only gashmiyus. At least my D’var Torah didn’t fall in the mud! I’m gonna try to remember that gashmiyus things are temporary, and we should just focus on the ruchniyus.

…And on using the gashmiyus for the ruchniyus, just like Yossi said.

You kids got it! Your Zeidy Yaakov must be so proud of you. And now let’s ask all the other chayolim to think about it: How will you use your gashmiyus in a way that brings Moshiach sooner?
