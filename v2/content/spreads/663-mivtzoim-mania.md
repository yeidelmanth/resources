The Lulav That Started It All

As shluchim to Sumy, Ukraine, we have the z’chus to help many Yidden connect to Hashem. You’ll never believe who we helped in this story…

A fancy envelope lay on top of Tatty’s mail pile, waiting to be opened. It was an invitation to the governor’s yearly meeting with all the religious leaders of the city.

I’ll send the head of the community instead of me, like I always do, Tatty thought. Usually, not much happens at these meetings, anyway.

He opened the letter and began to read. As his eyes moved down the page, he suddenly stopped.

What’s this?! The city plans to teach about Goyishe religions in all public schools?

Tatty’s mind began racing. There are Yiddishe children in the public schools. Many of them don’t know anything about Yiddishkeit. Some don’t even know that they’re Jewish! And now, they’re going to be taught about Goyishe religions?

Operation Lulav

Tatty knew there was no other choice. He had to go to the meeting himself and convince them not to go ahead with their plans. He took out his calendar and marked down the date: Friday afternoon.

The day of the meeting arrived. To Tatty’s dismay, all of the other leaders agreed to the plan. When it was his turn, he stood up and said, “There are Jewish children in these schools who don’t even know that they’re Jewish.”

The others looked confused. “How can that be?” they asked.

“A Jew is someone whose mother is Jewish,” Tatty explained. “And so, whether he knows it or not, he is still a Jew.

That mitzvah changed something inside the galach. It seemed to wake up his neshama to want to connect more with Hashem.

A galach, a priest, had followed him out.

The next year, on Simchas Torah, we were dancing outside the Chabad House. It felt very special, because not too long ago, Yidden in Sumy could get thrown into jail for keeping mitzvos. And now, we were dancing openly in the streets, happy and proud for everyone to see!

The boy said no. The shliach convinced him to get one, and amazingly, at eighteen years old, the boy finally got a bris milah!

No one could have imagined how far that first mitzvah would go. From dancing on Simchas Torah to putting on tefillin, a Jewish burial, and even a bris milah, it all began when Tatty helped another Yid shake lulav and esrog.

As we danced, a car pulled over nearby. “Shalom!” the driver called. Before we could answer, he jumped out and joined the dancing.
