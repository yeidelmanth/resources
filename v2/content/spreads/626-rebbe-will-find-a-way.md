Guess what? Just three days later, the camp director emailed Mommy. "A spot opened up for Srolik!"

Mommy quickly replied that I would come. She felt bad for Shmulik, though. There was still no room for him, which meant that he would have to stay home.

"Please confirm that you’re only sending one child—Srolik—since we don’t have space for both boys."

Mommy thought hard. Why was there suddenly room for me, but not for Shmulik?

Then she remembered: only I had written to the Rebbe!

Right away, she gave Shmulik a pen and paper, and told him to write to the Rebbe, too. He wrote his own letter, asking for a bracha to go to camp.

Two days later, the director emailed Mommy again: “Good news! We now also have space for Shmulik!”

After writing to the Rebbe, our dream of going to camp came true.

It was the winter of 5784, and I was so excited. I had been waiting for two whole years to go to overnight camp, just like my big brother Shmulik. This year, I was finally old enough to go!

But there was a problem. Camp registration opened on Sunday afternoon, and that day, Mommy and my baby brother were very sick with the flu. Mommy felt too weak to sign us up.

By the time she felt a little better on Tuesday, it was too late. She tried to register us, but the camp was already full!

Mommy emailed the camp director, hoping he could help. But he said there was nothing he could do. “Registration closed after just twenty-five minutes!" he told her. "Many, many kids want to come.”

I was very disappointed. I had waited for so long to go to overnight camp, and now I couldn’t go.

Mommy kept emailing the camp every few weeks hoping for a spot, but they continued to say the same thing: "Sorry, there’s no room."

Then, one night, Mommy had an idea. “We should write to the Rebbe for a bracha,” she said.

I ran to get a paper and pen and wrote my letter, asking the Rebbe to help me go to camp.
