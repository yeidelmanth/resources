Time passed. One day, Tatty’s friend came to work with a completely different energy. He was beaming, and even before he said anything, Tatty could tell that something good had happened.

“My wife is pregnant,” his friend exclaimed, unable to contain his excitement. “We’re going to have a baby!”

Tatty promised that he would, and he fulfilled his promise. Not long afterward, he traveled to the Ohel to say thank you for the miracle. Standing in awe before the Rebbe, he thought about the power of having emunah in our tzaddikim, who are higher than nature.

My friend isn’t even Jewish, he thought, and yet he was able to connect with the Rebbe and receive brachos simply by writing a letter. How much more so for us chassidim!

Tatty’s friend had never heard of the Rebbe before. Would he agree to ask for a bracha?

Remember the story from last week, about Tatty helping his business partner connect to the Rebbe? Well, here’s another story of Tatty having a positive influence at work.

Tatty is friendly with another non-Jewish man at his office. One day, as they were chatting, his friend sighed deeply.

“My wife and I have been waiting for ten years to have children,” he shared, his voice filled with frustration and sadness. “We’ve been to so many doctors and tried all types of treatment, but nothing worked.”

The hopeless look in his friend’s eyes touched Tatty deeply. He sat quietly for a moment, unsure of how to comfort his friend. There has to be a way to help. But how? If the doctors couldn’t do anything, what could I possibly offer?

At once, Tatty knew the answer. If nothing natural had worked, a bracha from the Rebbe

Tatty cleared his throat, trying to find the right words. “Why don’t you write a letter to the Lubavitcher Rebbe?” he asked gently.

“The Rebbe is a very holy rabbi; a wise and respected leader.” Tatty explained. “You can write a letter to him about how deeply you want a child. Maybe his blessings can give you the good news you are waiting for.”

“If there’s even a chance that the Rebbe’s blessing will help, then I’ll write to him.”

A few days later, he told Tatty that he had written the letter with a lot of faith and hope.
