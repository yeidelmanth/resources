chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Mimi, can I tell everyone about the second sandwich?

Do you guys know about the special chessed that Mimi does? Every day, when she prepares her lunch for school, she makes an extra sandwich. Mimi, you’re so nice!

Um, well, one day I noticed that my friend only brought small snacks to school and no lunch. When I asked her about it, she said that her family doesn’t have the money to buy so much food. So, I asked Mommy if I could bring her a sandwich every day, and Mommy said yes.

Mimi, that’s so thoughtful of you! But how do you remember to make the extra sandwich every single day?

It’s kind of a habit now. I mean, I had to think about it the first day I did it, and then the next, and maybe also the next. But by now, it’s just normal for me to prepare two sandwiches every day. I don’t even have to think about it—it’s automatic.

That’s really great. I bet that since your friend is eating better, she also has more strength to learn Torah in school. Which reminds me of something in this week’s parsha … Yossi, could you call our guest for today? I’m pretty sure it was gonna be a Levi.

Hello, hello chayolim! I wish I could chat, but I must be off.

Wait! You were supposed to teach us a lesson from the parsha! Where are you going?

I’m on my way to do my special job as a Levi: helping the kohanim to serve Hashem. Just like every Yid should do!

Every Yid should help the kohanim? But that’s just for Levi’im. I’m not a Levi!

Yes, but when you see another Yid trying to serve Hashem, you should help them. Since every Yid is called a kohen, when you help others, you’ll be just like a Levi! It’s really that simple.

Mimi does that very well! Every day she makes an extra sandwich for her friend so she won’t be hungry.

Every day? That’s amazing! Have you done it three times yet?

Perfect! A chazaka! Three times of something makes a chazaka. After three, it becomes a strong and powerful part of you. Just like it goes Reuven, Shimon, and then Levi!

Uhh, what? What do Reuven and Shimon have to do with this?

They were the first two shevatim. Levi was the third. Levi was the chazaka! When Levi was born, his mother Leah said, “עַּתָה הַּפַעַם יִּלָוֶה אִישִׁי אֵלַי—This time, \[with a third son,\] my husband will be joined to me.” But “ishi” doesn’t only mean “husband”.

It also means Hashem, the Head of the Yidden. I get it! We are connected to Hashem by doing Torah and Mitzvos.

Exactly! And when we make this bond a chazaka, it becomes a connection that lasts forever.

Ooh, now I understand. Levi is the third shevet, which was the chazaka, and they made a chazaka of being attached to Hashem forever by helping the kohanim serve Him. So all of us can be like the Levi’im by—

—I know! By being like Mimi! Helping other Yidden serve Hashem, and making it become a chazaka, too!
