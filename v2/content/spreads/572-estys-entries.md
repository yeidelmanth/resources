about Pesach Sheini Source: Toras Menachem 5743 Vol. 3 pp. 1404-1406 (Sicha of Pesach Sheini, 5743)

Today I learned a new trick. It’s a trick that can help me be a happier person. It can help me not to be such a kvetch. Maybe … just maybe … it can help me send that YH flying.

I first tried this trick at lunch when Rosie had pizza delivered

to the class in honor of her birthday. You know when those

boxes open and the smell of steaming, cheesy deliciousness

fills the classroom … everyone is thinking just one thing: I

Of course, the slices are never cut evenly. The pizza man

doesn’t use a ruler to measure each one (that’s what I

would do). And of course, I didn’t get a big slice; my

teacher handed me a perfectly medium one. Now, usually I would grumble, at least to myself, that everyone else got more pizza than I did. But today I noticed that some of the other slices in the box were, in fact, smaller than mine. And I appreciated that mine was bigger, and I didn’t look around to see if anyone else got more than me. I was much happier.

I tried this new way of thinking again when I got off

the school bus. My house has always seemed so small

I looked at it and counted the floors: one, two, three. I thought about the people in Africa who live in tiny wooden shacks and sleep on the floor. I thought about the robin who squeezes herself into the birdhouse hanging from our tree. I would not want to live there. Suddenly, when I walked through my front door, my house felt like a palace.

You may be wondering where I got this whole idea from. It all started with a mishna in Pirkei Avos class today: “Who is truly rich? Someone who is happy with what they have.”

Today I wanted to feel really rich, so I tried to be really happy with what I have. I woke

my desk. All twelve of them! Usually, I would look at my rank and say,

I get to the top. How am I ever gonna finish?! But today, I decided to be

happy with what I had. After all, so many kids have way less medals than me.

Before davening, I thought I’d be happy with the Tanya I’ve already memorized

this year, so I didn’t sing the new lines with my class.

I took my Chumash test, but I don’t think I did very well. I told myself I didn’t need to study, because if I just passed, I could be happy with my small accomplishment. After all, I’m sure there’s someone else in the universe who didn’t do as well as me.

I didn’t choose any vegetables from the school lunch today, since I’ve already eaten so many healthy foods in my lifetime, and I thought I could be happy with what I’ve already had.

But by the end of the day, I didn’t feel so happy and I realized that the Yetzer Hara was getting stronger.

This can’t be what the Mishna was talking about. I don’t think being satisfied with our mitzvos is a good thing.
