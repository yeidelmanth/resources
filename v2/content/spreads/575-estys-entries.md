Every Shabbos, after davening in shul, my friends and I head downstairs to the kiddush. At a table in the back, we have cup-stacking contests, read the jokes in the shul

newsletter, and finish off trays of steaming kugel.

my mother welcoming them into shul. I asked her about them a few minutes later, and she told me that they had

By the time I skipped down the stairs after davening, I had forgotten all about the new family. But when everyone was settled at their tables, talking and eating, I saw them sitting quietly at the table in the back. One of the girls glanced in our direction—kind of like she wanted to join us, but was too shy. I was about to invite her over, but just then, my friend Malky asked me to pass the kugel, and I got completely distracted. Anyway, I was nervous. I’ve never met anyone from France before, and I don’t speak a word of French. What would I say to her?

Only six days till Shavuos! Do you know what happened on this day 3,336 years ago? The Yidden arrived at Har Sinai, and something unusual happened. As they set up their tents, washed their clothes, and got ready to receive the Torah from Hashem, nobody fought with each other! That might not sound like a big deal, but we’re talking about millions of people—from old bubbies to little babies—all camping out together.

Last summer, my family went on a camping trip to the forest and we all slept in tents. It was a lot of fun, but let’s just say it’s not easy to get along with all your sisters in one squishy tent…

Actually, as I’m writing this, I think I know how all the Yidden got along so nicely. It’s like that time my whole family piled into our van and drove to New York for Uncle Berel’s wedding. We were packed like pickles in a jar, but we were so excited for the wedding that we forgot to kvetch!

When everyone is excited for one thing together, being nice kind of just happens. And that’s what it must have been like counting down those last few days to Matan Torah. I mean, what could be more exciting than getting the Torah itself?

You know, I’ve been thinking about that new girl from France all week long. I would love to just play with my usual friends and laugh at our jokes (in English!) together, but with Shavuos coming up, I can hear my Yetzer Tov whispering: “Don’t forget about that French girl. Be nice to her, too!”

My sister Mushky had a good idea, as always. She said I don’t have to know French in order to be nice to the new girl in shul. I could just say “Good Shabbos,” and smile. That feels like something I could easily do. I’ll try it, and see what happens next.

Mushky’s idea was great. As it turns out, Rivka Algazi (the

new girl) actually knows lots of English, and she had no

Rivka taught me how to say “bonjour,” which means “hi.” It took me all day because you have to put your teeth together in a weird way to make the French “j” sound. Rivka couldn’t stop laughing while my friends and I tried copying her.

Rivka invited me to her house for “seudat shlishit.” Her family is Sefardi, and they have a big meal at the end of every Shabbos. Well, everything was going great ‘till I saw some cute baby peppers on the table and decided to try one. I took one juicy bite and suddenly my whole mouth felt like a fiery furnace. Rivka quickly passed me some water, but her baby sister couldn’t stop giggling at my face (which was as red as hot tamales).

Aidel is back from Eretz Yisroel (she came for Yom Tov and her best friend’s chasuna), and today she showed me lots of pictures she printed from her year in seminary. I never saw so many different-looking Yidden before. There were men with big, furry hats and long, curly peyos,

and kind before Shavuos, even with people who

all Yidden together. Aidel said that the people in her pictures come from all around the world. They speak different languages and dress in different ways. But they are all Yidden, and all Yidden share the same Torah. That’s what makes us all like one person. On Shavuos, we receive the Torah from Hashem. And the best way to prepare for that is by bringing lots of Yidden together with Torah.

I’m glad I’ve started to be friends with Rivka. It’s easier now that I understand how we’re all one nation of Yidden, like one person, even if we seem different on the outside.

I’m going to ask Rivka if she wants to join Tzivos Hashem. That’s a great way to bring Yidden together to receive the Torah.

I’m sure that when we all unite “like one person with one heart” to receive the Torah, happy and excited, Hashem will give us the greatest brachos.
