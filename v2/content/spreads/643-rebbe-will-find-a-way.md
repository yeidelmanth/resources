The next day, on Shabbos afternoon, there was a knock at the door. It was Terry!

“Rabbi,” he said, “I thought about it all night. We must have a minyan in Wasilla for Yom Kippur. I’ll pay for the tickets—one thousand, two thousand, even five thousand dollars. Whatever it costs, I’ll do it!”

Tatty got the chills. The Rebbe heard my tefillos! He realized with wonder.

After Shabbos, Tatty found two bochurim to come, and booked their tickets. The night before Yom Kippur, they landed in Alaska. For the first time ever, there was a minyan in Wasilla for all the Yom Kippur tefillos!

One of the people who joined the davening on Yom Kippur morning was a 99-year-old man. Tatty and Terry were so grateful that they were able to help him—and many other Yidden—daven properly on Yom Kippur.

The Rebbe's brachos were clear to see. Far away in our small town in Alaska, Tatty had turned to the Rebbe for help. And only two days later, he was able to book flights for the bochurim!

Flying bochurim to Alaska would cost thousands of dollars. Could we really have a minyan for Yom Kippur?

It was Rosh Hashana תשע"ט (2018) in Wasilla, Alaska. Many people came to daven at our Chabad House, and more Yidden than ever before heard the shofar in our area of the Mat-Su Valley.

When Yom Tov ended, Tatty began thinking about Yom Kippur. If only we could bring some bochurim to help us have a minyan, he thought. It would make Yom Kippur so special.

But when Tatty looked into buying tickets, his heart sank. They were very expensive. There’s no way we can bring bochurim, he sighed.

That Thursday night, Daled Tishrei, Tatty sat down to write a letter to the Rebbe. He described the beautiful Rosh Hashana at our Chabad House and then wrote, I really want to bring bochurim to help with the tefillos on Yom Kippur, but flights are too expensive. Please give me a bracha to make the right choice.

He sent the letter to the Ohel, but still didn’t feel sure that it would work out.

The next day, on Friday, Tatty was invited to a special event. A Jewish man in the Air Force was retiring, and he asked Tatty to come.

After the event, Mr. Terry Gorelik, a man who had just moved to our area, came over to Tatty. “Rabbi,” he said, “I want to join you for Yom Kippur this year. Will there be a minyan?”

Terry thought for a moment. “I know some Israelis who work in Anchorage. I can ask them to come.”

But Tatty shook his head. “If they’re ready to come to shul on Yom Kippur, they should go to their local Chabad House,” he said. “I was actually thinking of bringing bochurim from New York, but flights are too expensive.”

Submitted by Chana, 11 Levi, 10 Ari Greenberg, 7 MyShlliach, Wasilla, Alaska
