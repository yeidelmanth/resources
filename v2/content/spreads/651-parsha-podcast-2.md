You won’t believe what happened! My teacher kicked me out of class for like, the most annoying reason ever.

Uh huh. The teacher tried telling me that I couldn’t draw pictures on my desk. Like, hello? I can do whatever I want!

Basya? I don’t even know if you’re joking. That’s so unlike you! What happened to my sweet, respectful sister?

Basya, you’ve been acting like this for a week already. Ever since that new kid came to school.

She’s my friend. And she’s so cool! She says that everyone needs to stop telling us what to do, because it’s our life and we just want to have fun!

Oh, boy. Basya, it seems like your friend has some very different ideas in her head. “I can do whatever I want” is a very goyishe thing to say—it can chas v’shalom lead to someone going against Hashem!

Yeah. In Golus, it can be very easy to get influenced by non-Jewish mindsets. It can happen to all of us, and it’s really dangerous.

Well, let's see if today’s guest, the Rambam, has something to say about it.

It’s Parshas Metzora, where we learn that tamei people become tahor by going to the mikva. The Rambam was a doctor. Maybe he can help Basya.

Hello children, I hear you called for a doctor. What may be the problem?

They think I’ve been badly influenced. My friend taught me that no one else can be the boss of me, and I think she’s right.

Ah, well I’m sure you know what the Torah says about derech eretz and kabbalas ol. You certainly don’t mean to go against the Torah. Right, Basya?

Oh! You’re right. But I can’t stop. I already started thinking like my friend, and now I get mad whenever someone tells me what to do. I feel like I can’t be the old me ever again! I’m gonna cry…

Oh Basya! It happens to all of us! We’re in Golus, surrounded by ideas that are against Torah. It’s very hard not to get influenced. And I’m sure the Rambam can help you!

Well, when a person is tamei, he goes to the mikva. Then, by covering his entire body with water, he becomes tahor. Water is compared to Torah. In fact, a mikva is also compared to Torah. A mikva is filled with forty se’ah-measurements of water, which are like the forty days and nights that it took for Moshe Rabbeinu to give over the Torah to the Yidden.

Hmm … So we need to cover ourselves in Torah? From head to toe?

Exactly. When our mind gets filled, chas v’shalom, with a bad thought, we need to wash it away and replace it with Torah. This doesn’t just mean to learn Torah, but to actually use its lessons in every idea, conversation, and talent that we have. Then, everything about us will be filled with Torah!

So the goyishe ideas will leave my mind, because the Yiddishe ones will take over?

Exactly, Basya! It’ll be like you went to the “mind mikva”—all the tum’a will be washed away!
