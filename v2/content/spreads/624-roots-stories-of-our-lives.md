After that, things went from bad to worse. The noises grew ever louder, and the sheidim started making trouble during the day, as well as at night. All of the families in the house cleared out as quickly as they could. Many of them squeezed into relatives’ homes, as they had no other place to stay.

It was between mincha and ma’ariv, in a hole-in-the wall shul on Market Street. It was a small minyan, housed in a tiny room between the butcher shop and free loan gemach. Men usually chose this shul for its location, not its company. It was the sort of shul which one would duck inside to catch a mincha, and still have enough time to head back to the market and finish

up business before ma’ariv. Most days, by the time the chazzan finished kaddish d’Rabanan, half of the minyan was out the door and the other half wasn’t far behind.
