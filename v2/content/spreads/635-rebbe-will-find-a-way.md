So Tatty and Mommy traveled to the Ohel and wrote to the Rebbe: We are ready to go on shlichus to the desert of California.

After coming out of the Ohel, they went to watch the video of the Rebbe. And just at that moment, they heard the Rebbe say these words:

"דִי גאַנצֶע וֶועלְט אִיז אַ מִדְּבָר אּון וואּו אַ אִיד גֶעפִינְט זִיך דאַרף עֶר מאַכְן פּון אַ אֶרָץ ֹלא זְרּועָה אַ מָקֹום זָרּועָה—The whole world is a desert. Wherever a Yid finds himself, he must turn that place from a land where nothing grows into a place full of growth \[and Yiddishkeit\].”

Tatty and Mommy looked at each other in shock. The Rebbe was giving them a clear answer—Bakersfield was their makom hashlichus!

Six weeks later, they packed up and moved. Their new shlichus in Bakersfield had begun!

Ever since he was a bochur, Tatty dreamed of being a shliach of the Rebbe. When he and Mommy got married, they were sure that they would move on shlichus. But where?

“I know of a Yid who lives in a beautiful California beach town,” Tatty told Mommy. “There’s no Chabad House there yet. It would be the perfect place for us to go on shlichus!”

Every Shabbos Mevarchim, Tatty brought up his idea to Rabbi Cunin, the head shliach of California. “Call my office on Monday,” Rabbi Cunin would say. But even though Tatty called again and again, nothing ever happened.

One day, Tatty hung up the phone and turned to Mommy. “I think it’s time to try something else. I’ll go to Rabbi Cunin’s office and speak to him in person.”

That Monday morning, Tatty was up bright and early. By 8 a.m., he was already standing in front of Rabbi Cunin’s office.

Soon, Rabbi Cunin arrived. He noticed Tatty right away, and smiled. “Wait here,” he said. “I’ll be with you soon.”

Tatty waited outside, hoping that this time, he would finally get the go-ahead.

After some time, the door opened and Rabbi Cunin’s son stepped out.

“Drive to the city of Bakersfield,” he told Tatty. “Check it out, and let us know if you’d like to go on shlichus there.”

Bakersfield? Tatty blinked. That’s not the town I’ve been asking about. I’ve never even heard of Bakersfield!

Still, Tatty drove the two hours to see what it was like.

When he arrived, he met a local Yid who told him that there was a small Jewish community in town. Tatty drove around and looked out the window. No beaches. No palm trees. Just dry, dusty roads and desert air.

The next day, Rabbi Cunin asked. “So, what did you think of Bakersfield?”

Tatty hesitated. “I’d rather go to the beach town I’ve been asking about.”

But Rabbi Cunin smiled warmly. “Yungerman,” he said, “right now, I need a shliach in Bakersfield. I think it will be a good place for you.”
