Tatty’s heart began to pound loudly in his chest. He was sure that this meeting would end very badly. As he went inside the construction site, he quietly said the Rebbe’s kapital and pleaded, “Rebbe, please help!”

Tatty walked up to the angry donor and tried to calm him down. At one point, he mentioned that he was friendly with a certain rabbi from Moscow.

“I know him,” the donor replied. He took out his phone and began to dial. “Hi Rabbi! I’m in Smolensk now.”

“Very nice!” came the reply. “There’s a shliach there who’s building a shul.”

Tatty held his breath, afraid of what the donor would say next.

To his relief, the donor’s face suddenly softened. “I’ve decided to help him finish building his shul. I will send my team from Moscow to draw better building plans, and, as

construction workers to build the shul as fast as possible. I’ll pay for it all, of course.”

True to his word, the donor helped Tatty and Mommy finish building the shul. Today, this beautiful shul stands in Smolensk and is named Bais Menachem in honor of the Rebbe—who takes care of his chassidim whenever we call out for help.

The donor looked around at the unfinished construction site, an angry expression on his face. Would he demand his money back?

The city of Smolensk, Russia used to have a lot of Yidden and many shuls. Sadly, by the time Tatty and Mommy moved there on shlichus, there was barely any trace of the beautiful Jewish life that used to be there. The shuls had all been destroyed, and there were hardly any Yidden left.

Getting right to work, Tatty and Mommy rented a small office to use as a shul. As time passed and more Yidden joined our community, it was time to build a proper Chabad House.

But in Russia, construction projects are very complicated. It would take a lot of work to get permission from the government, make building plans, and find skilled construction workers. On top of that, it would cost a lot of money, which Tatty and Mommy didn’t have.

Finally, after a few years, Tatty and Mommy raised enough money to buy land for the shul and begin construction.

Tatty hired local workers, but the construction kept stopping because there wasn’t enough money to pay them.

At one point, someone from Moscow gave a large donation towards building the roof.

But Tatty first had to use the money to pay other building expenses, and he planned to finish the roof later on.

One freezing winter day, Mommy got a frantic call from Tatty.

“The donor from Moscow just called,” Tatty told her anxiously. “He’s in Smolensk and is coming to check on the construction.

“But the roof is still not built! There aren’t even any workers at the site because I have no money to pay them!

“Please,” Tatty continued, “write a letter to the Rebbe and ask for a bracha. We need a big miracle. If this donor sees what’s going on, he might demand his money back!”

Of course, Mommy agreed to write to the Rebbe immediately. Tatty then hurried to the construction site to face the donor.

As he got closer, he heard the donor screaming from inside the half-finished building, “What kind of terrible work is this?! I have a diploma in building construction, and I know how things should be done!”
