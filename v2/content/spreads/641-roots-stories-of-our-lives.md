Over the winter months, as the Dvina River froze over and layers of icy white covered the streets of Vitebsk, Yosef Yitzchak and Devorah Leah busied themselves with preparing for their wedding. Not the mundane planning of arranging the wedding feast, setting up house, and buying a trousseau; Reb Kaddish and Freida made sure that those things were well taken care of. The chosson and kallah both

wished to focus on the spiritual preparations for their big day.

To this end, they agreed that Yosef Yitzchak’s teacher, Reb

Malkiel, would teach Devorah Leah until after the wedding, at

which point she’ll continue her studies with her new husband. Reb Malkiel mainly taught her the fundamentals of Kabbalah and Chassidus, for although Devorah Leah had a passion for the ways of Kabbalah, she had never studied the ideas behind it.

As for Yosef Yitzchak, the brilliant young chosson received an offer to head the yeshiva in Vitebsk. Yosef Yitzchak accepted the offer,

arranging to start only after his wedding. He wanted to devote this time as a chosson to personal learning and growth. He learned both on his own and with Reb Malkiel. The two of them found a side room in the beis midrash

which was used mostly for storage, and was therefore empty for much of the day. There, at a wobbly wooden table between the boxes of geniza sefarim and old benches, was the perfect spot for them to learn Kabbalah—away from prying eyes.

At one learning session, Reb Malkiel and Yosef Yitzchak were immersed in a difficult passage of Sefer Hapardes when they heard a commotion coming from the main room. The noise was particularly strange since the shul was normally empty at this time; it was after the last shacharis minyan, and before the first mincha.

While the community gathered in shul, the remaining council members gathered for an emergency meeting. They wrote a declaration urging any Jews with connections to nobility to do their best to utilize their contacts. They also put together a delegation to approach the governor directly.
