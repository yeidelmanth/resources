Do you learn the Rambam’s Sefer Hamitzvos every day? Do you study it for Chidon? Maybe you’ve seen your Tatty learning the Rambam’s Mishneh Torah, too! The Rambam (who was born on Erev Pesach, ד’תתצ”ה (1135) and passed away on 20 Teves, ד’תתקס”ה (1204) wrote sefarim that shape our daily lives. At the Shabbos seuda today, Tatty told us something very surprising: The Rebbe said at the farbrengen that sadly, there are Yidden who learn Torah all day long, but still don’t know core parts of Torah, like the inyan of Achdus Hashem (that there’s nothing in the world besides Hashem)! Can you believe it?

The Rebbe said that even some Roshei Yeshiva (!) don’t know these basic Torah concepts—and they’re all clearly written in Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah, right at the start of the Rambam’s Mishneh Torah!

These halachos are called “Yesodei HaTorah— foundations of the Torah”. They’re supposed to be the first thing we learn!

The Rebbe added, “May it be Hashem’s will that everyone starts learning Rambam in the right order, starting with Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah.”

I can’t wait till l get older so I can also learn Hilchos

One of my classmates said that learning Sefer Hamitzvos isn’t as important as learning Mishneh Torah, but I told him what the Rebbe said a few weeks ago—that Sefer Hamitzvos has a special bonus, because it tells us where each mitzvah is discussed in Gemara.

So really, the people learning Sefer Hamitzvos and the people learning Mishneh Torah need each other!

The Rebbe just said something amazing: learning Rambam every day isn’t something new. It’s actually a return to the old days! In the Rambam’s times, communities used to learn Mishneh Torah together. Even after the Rambam passed away, the minhag lasted for generations!

The Rebbe also said that we need to learn all the halachos in the Torah, even those that we don’t keep today (like the ones about karbanos), because every Yid has a mitzvah to know the entire Torah. And the Rambam’s sefarim are the only ones that include Kol HaTorah Kula—the whole Torah. That’s why we must learn Rambam!

This is especially important now, as we prepare for Geulah.

Wow, we did it! The Rebbe made a grand siyum for the first cycle of learning!

Two days after Yom Tov, during a Yechidus Klalis for the Pesach guests, the Rebbe explained two ways to split up the daily Rambam learning. And then today, at the Shabbos farbrengen, the Rebbe added a third one!

Phew, am I glad about Track Three! After opening the Mishneh Torah, I wasn’t sure I was old enough yet to learn it by myself… Sefer Hamitzvos lists short summaries of every mitzvah—much better for a kid like me!

The Rebbe said that everyone learning and discussing the same part of Torah each day will unite all Yidden! The cycles start tomorrow.

While learning today’s three perakim of Rambam, my roommate, Menashe, asked me which perakim we’re up to.

“Guys!” Dovber called from the corner of the room, “Did you know today is exactly ten years since the Rebbe first spoke about learning Rambam?”
