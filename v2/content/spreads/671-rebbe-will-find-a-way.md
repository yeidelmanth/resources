When they arrived, a nurse brought a hospital gown for Shaya. “We need to take a new X-ray,” she explained.

Shaya became upset. “We already took X-rays last week,” Mommy said.

Suddenly, Tatty felt a spark of hope. “This is how miracles start happening,” he told Mommy. “We tore up the old results at the Ohel. They’re going to find something different today.”

The doctor took new X-rays and was very happy with what she saw. “There’s no need for bigger tests,” she said. “Shaya will need extensive bone surgery to remove the tumor, but I’m confident that he will be fine.”

Baruch Hashem, the surgery was successful, and Shaya went home that same day. Four months later, he was walking on his own two feet, completely healthy!

Faced with a big challenge, Tatty and Mommy turned to the Rebbe and followed his guidance. And Hashem sent Shaya a miraculous refuah!

This story reminds us that when we go through hardships, we are never alone. We can always turn to the Rebbe and hold on to hope. Hashem can bring revealed good in ways we never imagined!

Tatty tore up Shaya’s test results at the Ohel. What would the new ones show?

In Adar תשפ”ד (2024), my brother Shaya’s leg hurt terribly. The pain kept getting worse, so Tatty and Mommy took him to a bone doctor.

The doctor took X-rays and studied them carefully. His face grew very serious. “These don’t look good,” he said quietly. “Shaya needs an emergency MRI.”

At first, Tatty hoped the doctor was simply being extra careful. He showed the X-rays to a radiologist friend, but the friend was also worried. “This really doesn’t look good,” he said. “Shaya may need even bigger tests.”

So Tatty and Mommy took Shaya for the MRI. The next day, the results came back: Shaya had a serious tumor in his thigh, and the doctors were very concerned.

Tatty and Mommy began to cry. “Will Shaya be okay?” they asked.

“We won’t know until we do bigger tests,” the doctors replied.

The tests were scheduled for the following week, so Tatty quickly booked flights to New York to daven at the Ohel first. Remembering the Rebbe’s advice, he brought all of our mezuzos along to be checked.

Throughout the flight, they cried, not knowing what would be. Tatty reminded Mommy of the niggun “Fort a Yiddele”, in which the Rebbe tells a Yid in danger, “You will not drown.”

“We are going to daven at the Ohel,” Tatty said. “The Rebbe will make sure that we don’t drown.”

At the Ohel, Tatty, Mommy, and Shaya each wrote a letter and davened for a long time. Before leaving, Tatty tore up copies of Shaya’s MRI and X-rays and dropped them into the pile with everyone’s letters.

“Rebbe,” he said, “please make sure Hashem tears up this entire problem and it should just go away!”

The Rebbe often told people to get a second opinion for medical problems, so Tatty searched for a top doctor and heard about one at the Texas Children’s Hospital in Houston, where we live.

The next day, Friday, Tatty called Rabbi Lazaroff, who helps many patients in Houston. “Can you help us get an appointment?” Tatty asked.
