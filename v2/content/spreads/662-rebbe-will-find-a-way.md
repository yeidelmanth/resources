He was about to start writing, but then he stopped again. The Rebbe’s brachos don’t have limits, he thought. With strong bitachon, he began to write: Rebbe, please give us a bracha that the cyclone shouldn’t even reach Cairns at all.

This all happened the night before the cyclone was supposed to hit. Many people in the city were worried and afraid, but we went

amazed to see a clear, blue sky and bright, shining sun. There was no sign of the cyclone at all!

We soon heard the news that the cyclone had faded away over the water without ever reaching Cairns. Everyone was safe, and our community event took place as planned.

We were awed by the clear miracle, which showed us how the Rebbe's brachos are more powerful than nature. We saw clearly that when we turn to the Rebbe, our greatest challenges—even a mighty cyclone!—can vanish and fade away.

A powerful cyclone was heading straight for our city, but we weren’t afraid.

My family is on shlichus in the tropical city of Cairns, Australia, very far from other frum families. The closest shliach lives so far away that it takes more than a whole day to drive there!

Cairns is a beautiful place. Our Chabad house is surrounded by wonders of Hashem’s world, including the ocean’s Great Barrier Reef and the leafy-green Daintree Rainforest.

But living in Cairns also means living with something dangerous: cyclones. A cyclone is a huge storm with strong winds and heavy rain. Cyclones are so powerful that they can flood houses and push trees to the ground. In other parts of the world, this type of storm is called a hurricane.

Years ago, a cyclone swept through Cairns, and many people lost their homes. Baruch Hashem, our Chabad House remained safe, and we opened our doors to Jewish families who needed somewhere to stay until they could find a new place to live.

In the month of Shvat, 5781 (2021), just before one of our big community events,

we heard worrisome news: Tropical Cyclone Kimi was on its way, headed straight toward Cairns.

The whole city began to prepare. Shops closed, windows were boarded up, and many people left for safer places.

Inside of our house, though, everything felt calm. Tatty told us that he was going to write to the Rebbe for a bracha, and we knew that the Rebbe would help.

Tatty went into his office and sat down at his desk. If the cyclone comes, he thought, the streets will flood and the event will be canceled. He picked up his pen to ask for a bracha that the event should go on as planned.

Then, Tatty paused. There’s something more important than the event: I should ask that no one gets hurt.
