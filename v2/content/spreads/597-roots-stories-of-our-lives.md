Word of this wandering tzaddik soon reached the ears of the chief rabbi of Pinsk himself, Rav Naftali Katz. Rav Naftali sent two of his students to the hekdesh. After just a few minutes, the students were awed by the old traveler’s obvious ability to quote from all of Shas, Rishonim, and the Shulchan Aruch

from memory. The man also shared a few novel chiddushim, unlike anything they’d read in any sefer before.

Rav Naftali arranged a seudah to welcome the man to town. All of the greatest talmidei chachamim of Pinsk attended. After the event, the man was invited to speak at each of the three great yeshivos. Each time he spoke, he captivated the crowd with his breadth of knowledge, engaging manner of speech, and unique insights. Soon, a couple of bachurim came to sit at his table in the beis midrash, eager to learn from him. Those couple of students turned into half a dozen, and then one dozen, two dozen … by the end of the month, close to fifty students counted themselves as this man’s talmidim.
