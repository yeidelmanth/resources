The nurse started asking questions, and Mommy was able to answer!

When Tatty came back to the room, he was shocked when Mommy greeted him. He was filled with an indescribable feeling of relief and joy. Mommy was finally getting better!

Ten days after giving birth, Mommy was well enough to leave the hospital and go to rehab. Eight days after that, she was finally able to come home. We greeted her with blasting music and big yard letters across the lawn that said: WELCOME HOME MOMMY! THANK YOU HASHEM!

At home, Mommy rested while family, friends, and shluchim continued to help our family. We had plenty of food for Shabbos, and supper was delivered for ten weeks. People continued to visit, taking us to the park and on trips so Mommy could recover.

When she felt strong enough, Mommy went back to the hospital to thank the staff. They were overjoyed to see her—a living, breathing miracle!

Seven weeks after Mommy came home, we made a big seudas hoda’ah to thank Hashem for the big miracle. A few months later, Mommy flew to the Ohel to personally thank the Rebbe for his

She stood at the Ohel and read her pan, tears filling her eyes. Thank you, Rebbe, for bringing me to this moment. Thank you for carrying me and my family through that difficult time. As Chassidim, we are never alone. Ashrienu mah tov chelkeinu!

After eating the Rebbe’s matzah, Mommy finally began to heal.

It was Chol Hamoed Pesach, when families usually spend special time learning Torah together and having fun. But instead of going on trips with our family, Mommy lay in a hospital bed surrounded by many doctors. Tatty sat nearby, saying Tehillim with tears in his eyes.

You see, something had gone wrong when our baby sister was born, and now Mommy was dangerously ill.

Tatty wrote to the Rebbe, and our aunts in New York went to the Ohel. People around the world davened for a miracle. Finally, after an endless twenty-four hours, Mommy’s life was no longer in grave danger.

But she was still very sick. And then, on Shvi’i Shel Pesach, after having surgery, something went wrong again. Mommy needed yet another surgery to save her life.

On Acharon Shel Pesach, Mommy was finally able to breathe on her own. But she didn’t know what was happening around her. She couldn’t even answer simple questions like, “What’s your name?”

Tatty tried staying strong in his bitachon. He thought about how everything is in Hashem’s hands and therefore, is good.

The next day, on Isru Chag, Mommy’s friend, Mrs. Mushki Shmotkin, came

to our house. She had driven two whole hours with her family just to deliver a most precious gift: a tiny piece of matzah from the Rebbe, which was over thirty years old.

“During Moshiach’s Seudah, I read about the Rebbe telling someone to give matzah to a friend who was sick,” she told Tatty. “I knew right away that I should bring this matzah for your wife.”

It was perfect timing, because Mommy had just passed the swallowing test. For the first time in five days, she would be able to eat real food.

That night at 9:00 pm, Tatty crushed the special matzah and mixed it into a puree of soft food to feed to Mommy. Though she still didn’t know what was happening around her, Mommy ate the entire thing.

At 6:00 am on Monday morning, just nine hours after eating the matzah from the Rebbe, Mommy suddenly began to talk to the nurse.
