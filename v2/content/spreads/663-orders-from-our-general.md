<p class="lead">When Moshiach comes, Hashem will make a very special sukkah for the Yidden. Chazal explain that it will be made from the skin of the Livyasan fish, and all Yidden will sit together inside. Hashem Himself will be there, too. Just imagine: every Jew together in one sukkah, with Hashem right there with us!</p>

The happiness that we’ll feel will be even greater than the joy of Rosh Hashana. Sitting together in the sukkah of the Livyasan will be a time of pure closeness, unity, and simcha between Hashem and the Yidden.

But here’s something amazing: Even now, while we are still in Galus, we already have a chance to feel that even nowadays! And by fulfilling this mitzvah in Galus, we will be zocheh to sit in the special sukkah of the Geulah.

The same is true of the great achdus that we’ll feel. When you sit in the sukkah nowadays with your family, friends, and guests, you show how all Yidden truly belong together. Differences don’t matter when it comes to sitting in a sukkah. What matters is that we’re sitting together.

Which choice fits the message of Sukkos?

I focus only on helping myself enjoy the Yom Tov.

I share the simcha, welcome others, and focus on our achdus as one Yiddishe nation.

All Yidden are fit to sit together

When you celebrate Sukkos, remember that your actions now prepare the world for what’s coming next. Be sure to fill your sukkah time with simcha, achdus, and caring for other Yidden. Because with that, you bring closer the day when we’ll all sit together with Hashem in the sukkah of the Livyasan!

Why does sitting in a sukkah create more achdus than other mitzvos do?

How can simcha bring people closer together?

A Mitzvah’s Reward Berditchev, Ukraine

The weary traveler to Berditchev shook his head. “No,” he said. “You cannot have my esrog. Let me continue on my way, for I must hurry home for Yom Tov.”

“But you can spend it here with us, in Berditchev,” pleaded the Chossid. “And then Reb Levi Yitzchok and our entire town will be able to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav and esrog!”

Despite the Chossid’s heartfelt pleas, the traveler refused to stay.

Word of the esrog's departure reached Reb Levi Yitzchok, who hurried to meet the traveler. “Please, consider staying with us for Yom Tov,” Reb Levi Yitzchak begged. “There is not one esrog in the entire town of Berditchev. You are our only hope!”

<span class="mc" style="color:#e85d38">The traveler, determined to spend Yom Tov at home, wasn’t convinced.</span>

Reb Levi Yitzchak’s desire to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav and esrog overcame him, and he turned to the visitor once more. “If you stay and allow us to use your esrog, you will share in my portion of Olam Haba.”

Stunned, the traveler agreed. The people of Berditchev were overjoyed.

A few hours before Yom Tov, a messenger appeared at the traveler’s inn. “I have instructions for you from Reb Levi Yitzchok,” he informed the innkeeper. “Don’t allow the traveler with the esrog inside your sukkah!” The messenger continued on his way, passing on the instructions to everyone in Berditchev.

On the first night of Sukkos, the traveler returned from shul ready to make kiddush and eat the Yom Tov seuda. But as he tried to enter the sukkah, the innkeeper blocked his way!

“Please let me in,” said the traveler. “I can’t make kiddush outside of the sukkah!”

But the innkeeper stood his ground.

The traveler had to look elsewhere for a place to eat. He walked to the neighbor’s sukkah and asked if he could celebrate Sukkos with them. To his utter bewilderment, the neighbor refused. As he continued looking for a place to make kiddush, the answer was always no.

<span class="mc" style="color:#e85d38">The traveler grew suspicious. Why was everyone turning him down? He questioned some of the residents, pestering them until they revealed Reb Levi Yitzchok’s instructions.</span>

Frustrated, he ran to Reb Levi Yitzchok, and cried, “Is this how you repay me?”

“If you disregard my promise of being with me in Gan Eden,” replied Reb Levi Yitzchok, “I will let you enter my sukkah.”

The traveler stood rooted to the spot. How could he choose? It took some moments before he concluded: fulfilling the mitzvah of sitting inside a sukkah is more important than all the rewards of Olam Haba! “I agree,” he said.

Reb Levi Yitzchok invited the traveler to eat in his sukkah. He turned to him and remarked: “I made a promise to you, and I will keep it. You will indeed get a share in my Olam Haba. But it was necessary that you should work to deserve it. Once you showed that you were willing to give up the share in order to do a mitzvah, you were able to receive this lofty reward.”
