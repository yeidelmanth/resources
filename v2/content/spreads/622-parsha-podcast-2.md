chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

It’s the Aseres Yemei Teshuva now. I bet our guest is someone really special.

He is! His name is Dovid. He’s in Yossi’s class and he’s a real lamdan. He probably knows more than me! And he’s a big chossid with Yiras Shamayim and Ahavas Yisroel. He may not be as old as our other guests, but there’s a lot we can learn from him. Right, Yossi?

Ummm, I don’t know if that’s the best idea. I was kind of … mean to him today during recess … I, uh, told him that he couldn’t join our team for basketball because, well, he’d make us lose.

Oh no! That’s really not nice. But you sound like you’re sorry. Isn’t now the best time to call and apologize?

Uh, okay. You’re right, Basya. I’ll call him now. I just hope he answers when he sees that it’s me calling…

Hello, Dovid, welcome to our podcast. How’re you doing?

Hi, Dovid. I know you must be upset at me for being so mean to you today at recess. I was just so carried away by the game that I forgot that Ahavas Yisroel comes first. But that’s no excuse. I really am sorry. And I want you to join my team tomorrow. Will you please forgive me?

Of course I forgive you, Yossi. And I look forward to playing with you tomorrow.

See, Yossi? When you apologize and really mean it, then your friend will forgive you.

Yeah! And then everyone is much happier! You, and Dovid, and all of us on the podcast, and the Rebbe, and Hashem!

Thank you, guys, for teaching me this important lesson. And thank you, Dovid, for accepting my apology.

Actually, this is exactly what I was planning on talking about for the podcast.

Well, it’s the Aseres Yemei Teshuva. The ten days from Rosh Hashana through Yom Kippur are the easiest time for teshuva.

Oh, yeah! Rabbi Schwartz was talking about that. But why are they the easiest time for teshuva? Doesn’t Hashem accept our teshuva anytime?

Of course Hashem does! Teshuva means returning to Hashem. First you think about what you did, then you correct it and make sure to actually change your behavior for next time.

Like you did, Yossi, when you apologized. You realized that what you did wasn’t nice, and so you’ll make sure to do better tomorrow!

But if Dovid wasn’t our guest for today, it would’ve been harder for me to apologize quickly and properly. Knowing that you were just one call away made it much easier.

That’s exactly it, Yossi! During the Aseres Yemei Teshuva, Hashem is right here, hoping and waiting for us to talk to Him and say that we’re going to do better and better.

Oh, I get it! Hashem is always close by and we can always talk to Him, but now He’s extra close. It’s like He’s ready and waiting for our phone call!

Yes. But remember, as soldiers in Hashem’s army we don’t keep such important things to ourselves! We have Ahavas Yisroel, which means we share what we learn with other kids. So let’s ask all of the chayolim listening: Hashem is right here, waiting for you to talk to Him. What will you say?
