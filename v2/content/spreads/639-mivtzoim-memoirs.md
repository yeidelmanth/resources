What an amazing Chanukah rally! It’s already quite late, but I have to tell you about it!

The Rebbe said that we must make our home into a Beis Tefillah (a place where we ask Hashem for everything we need), a Beis Torah (a place where Torah is learned and reviewed), and a Beis Gemilus Chassadim (a place where tzedaka is given). This way, it’s a place where the Shechina can rest.

After the rally, Mommy picked me up from 770. As soon as I got in the car, I asked

her—respectfully, just as the Rebbe had taught us—if she could help me do this as extra “Chanukah gelt” (not real money, but the special “gift” of making our home full of holy light).

“I would love to help you!” Mommy gushed. (She was even more excited than if I had asked her to help me with homework!)

We started brainstorming on how to add in all of these three things to make our home the brightest on the block!

Mivtzoim—they feel like they’ve been around forever! Can you even imagine life without them? Yet, unbelievably, before the Rebbe introduced each of the mitzvah campaigns that we know so well, they simply didn’t exist.

Join us as we explore imaginary old diaries that could have belonged to kids just like you— kids who might have lived during the time when the Rebbe first announced many of these mivtzoim.

Join Sara as she transforms her bedroom from ordinary to extraordinary; from just a place to sleep and relax into a full-on Cheder Tzivos Hashem a mini Beis Hamikdash! כ"ו כסלו, תשמ"ז

I was sitting cross-legged on my bed this afternoon, spacing out (as usual), when I started looking around my room. The soft pink curtains, the decorations on the wall … that’s when I noticed the picture I had drawn of my friend Leiba and me in Tzivos Hashem uniform. I continued looking around—my mirror, headbands, and art set. Hmm… I thought to myself. I’m a soldier in Hashem’s Army. If that’s the case, then my room must be my army base!

Did you know that when a Jewish home is a Beis Torah, Tefillah, and Gemilus Chassadim, it actually becomes a mini Beis Hamikdash (a.k.a. a Mikdash me’at)? I just found that out from a letter the Rebbe wrote to all chassidim! In another letter just for the kids, the Rebbe said that our bedrooms should have these three things, as well! When we learn Torah, daven and give tzedaka in our bedroom, it also becomes a home for Hashem! So, every bedroom needs to have:

1\. A Chumash 2. A siddur 3. A pushka In the sefarim and on the pushka, we should write “לה' הארץ ומלואה” (or, “לה"ו” for short) and our names underneath. I’m heading to Kingston Avenue to buy myself a new, pink pushka and a good marker to write with. Love, Lieutenant Sara

My class and I just went on the best trip to 770 ever! The Rebbe gave out nickels just to us children to put right into our new bedroom pushkas! We all came wearing Shabbos clothes and pushka-decorated crowns, and proudly held our new pushkas.

I felt like a million dollars when the Rebbe focused on me as I passed by his shtender. Of course, I put the nickel straight into my pushka so the Rebbe could watch me do the mitzvah.

Mommy even brought my baby brother, Tzemach, to get a nickel from the Rebbe. The Rebbe put the coin into his teeny tiny hand, and Mommy helped him place it into his new pushka! Precious!

I’m heading over to buy myself a new hagadah, because the Rebbe just said that our bedrooms need a hagadah, too. I’m actually going to get a couple more sefarim besides that, because I want my room to be my Bayis Malei Sefarim, and no way are three sefarim enough.

On Shabbos, the Rebbe gave us a powerful strategy for fighting the Yetzer Hara. I tried it out, and it worked! Here’s how it goes: On Sunday morning, I hung a brand new sign on my bedroom door that says "Cheder Tzivos Hashem". I taped it right at eye level so I can see it every time I walk into my room. After all, my room—and everything in it—is a place for the Shechina to rest! When I was walking into my room to pick up my Chumash and learn, the Yetzer Hara tried to stop me with his kvetchy voice, “Why are you working so hard to learn Torah? You don’t even understand everything yet like your parents and teachers! Go relax and play like a 9-year-old should!” But have no fear: I saw the sign on my door. I told him, “It doesn’t matter that I’m so young! I’m part of Tzivos Hashem! In an army, what matters is kabbalas ol and ma’aseh b’po’al—doing what Hashem wants. And in that, every soldier is equal!” Boom! Kvetchy voice stopped. It felt so good to win the battle.

It’s been quite the week! Last Tuesday, the judge ruled that the sefarim belong to the chassidim (Didan Notzach!) and we danced like it was Simchas Torah. Then, yesterday, at an extra- special Didan Notzach Shabbos farbrengen, the Rebbe said that everyone, big and small—actually, davka the littlest kids—should get involved in making their homes a Beis Chabad. How proud I feel, knowing that my little Beis Chabad is connected to the giant Beis Chabad of 770, and from there to all Chabad Houses around the world! I could never be lonely again! And here’s the best part: The Rebbe shared that one Mommy and Tatty wrote a nachas note to the Rebbe, describing how their son was so excited to turn his room into a Beis Chabad that he hung a sign on his bedroom door with the words “Beis Chabad”! I’m getting my markers out right now. Love, Lieutenant Sara

I continued thinking: What would a Tzivos Hashem army base look like? Full of heilige things, that’s for sure. Well, I already have a mezuza. I wash negel vasser by my bed. My closet is full of tzniusdik bas-melech dresses. What else should I add? Love, Lieutenant Sara
