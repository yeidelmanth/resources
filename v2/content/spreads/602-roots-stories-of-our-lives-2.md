R’ Asher Yehonasan, Moshe’s teacher, was called “Der Eizener Kop” because of his brilliance. There is a fascinating story behind Reb Asher Yehonasan’s birth.

R’ Asher’s father, R’ Yisrael Chaim, was a simple storekeeper who loved children. He would visit the chadarim each Friday and distribute candies and raisins to the children in honor of Shabbos. He would remind all the boys— both in school and those he passed on the street—to come to shul for Kabbalas Shabbos and Shabbos Day. And during davening, he would gather all the little ones around him and help them say “Amen”, “Baruch Hu Uvaruch Shemo”, and the words of kedusha at the appropriate times.

Unfortunately though, one by one, all of Reb Yisrael Chaim’s children passed away when they were still babies. One son, Simcha, lived longer. Reb Yisrael Chaim and his wife were so hopeful that this time, Hashem had granted them what they were waiting for! But sadly, at age five, little Simcha passed away as well.

The whole community showed up to Simcha’s funeral. There wasn’t a dry eye in the crowd; everyone felt the pain of Reb Yisrael Chaim and his wife. Reb Yisrael Chaim himself appeared quiet and calm. “We hoped this son would live,” said Reb Yisrael Chaim, his eyes downcast. “But Hashem gives, and Hashem takes; may Hashem’s name be blessed.”

Just then, a very old, disheveled- looking man pushed to the front. His name was Asher Yehonasan; everyone in Posen knew him as the town meshugane. He never talked to anyone, and mostly spent his time in the attic of the shul muttering to himself and pacing back and forth. So everyone was shocked when they heard Reb Asher Yehonasan give the following speech:

“My fellow Jews. It has been some fifteen years since the Satan

“The Satan therefore asked permission to put Reb Yisrael Chaim to the test, and see if he would continue to be as dedicated to Torah and mitzvos even through pain and suffering. That is why they have been struck with this terrible tragedy. Rabosai, I am now ninety-four years old. Two months from now, I will be leaving this world. When I arrive in the olam ha-emes, I promise to call the Satan to a din Torah for having caused Reb Yisrael Chaim and his wife so much suffering! Enough is enough!”

Reb Asher Yehonasan turned towards Reb Yisrael Chaim. “I will insist that you be blessed with a healthy son; a son who will live for many years, have great talents, and grow up to be a leader among our people. On one condition: you must name your son after me, Asher Yehonasan.”

With that, Reb Asher Yehonasan disappeared back into the crowd.

People didn’t know what to think. Was Reb Asher Yehonasan really a hidden tzaddik, someone with access to what was going on in shamayim? Or was this all just a fantasy he made up, another symptom of his craziness?

At first the townspeople were split. But when exactly two months later, a healthy-looking Reb Asher Yehonasan passed away,

there were no more doubts. Reb Asher Yehonasan’s crazy behavior had all been an act to disguise his tzidkus.
