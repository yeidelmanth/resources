chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

I finally finished making a scrapbook for Bubby! I’m so excited; I love the patterned paper that I used. Have you all finished writing your cards?

I did. Did everyone make sure to end them off with “ּכְתִיבָה וַחַתִימָה טֹובָה”?

K’siva vachasima tova! It means, “May you be written and sealed for a good year,” and it’s what we wish each other beginning from Rosh Chodesh Elul.

But I can’t find the glitter pen that I wrote my card with, and adding another line in a different style might ruin the design! Why do we say that, anyway?

Ari Baker. Loves cars and cupcakes. Age: 5 years and 1 day. He will be featured on our podcast today. Yossi, please place the call.

Hi Ari, and thank you for joining the Parsha Podcast!

Can you tell us what happened right before your birthday party yesterday?

So my mommy made my favorite chocolate cupcakes, with cars on top and wheels made out of candy and a little Ari in each driver seat! They looked so yummy, I couldn’t wait till my party to eat one.

I said, “Please Mommy could I have one now? Pleeeease???” But that didn’t work. So I said a very, very nice bracha mezonos, clear and loud … and, well, the cupcakes were the only food on the table…

Yup. It was yummy! I love cupcakes! I love my Mommy!

And she said amen to my bracha, and she smiled, because it was such a nice bracha.

Thank you, Ari, I think we learned an important lesson! And happy birthday!

Wait, what lesson? Those cupcakes sound so good, now I want some too!

And I still don’t understand the point of wishing each other “k’siva vachasima tova”...

Ari just explained it to us with his great cupcake plan!

Well, Ari saw the cupcakes. He knew that his mother wouldn’t want him to make a bracha l’vatala, so once he made the bracha, his mother had no choice but to give it to him.

Ooh, I see where you’re going with this! By wishing each other a k’siva vachasima tova starting all the way from Rosh Chodesh Elul, we make sure that Hashem will give us a good year, because He wouldn’t want us to make a bracha l’vatala!

Wow! I want to show Hashem my confidence that He will give us a good year! A year sweeter than any cupcakes … a year with Moshiach! I’m gonna make sure to wish everybody a k’siva vachasima tova! My rebbi, my friends, my family…

Me too! I’m going to start by writing it on Bubby’s card! Did anybody see my glitter pen?

Fellow chayolim, we hope you enjoyed our podcast. And now we have a question for you: How can you show Hashem that you’re confident He will give us all a shana tova umesuka?
