Now, you may be wondering how I—Reb Sender—know about this whole thing. Well, let me introduce you to my long- distance, super-sophisticated, WePhone enabled, voice- detecting machine: it’s a

portable gadget that can pick up the voices of chayolim up to 24,901 miles away in good weather and 24,899.5 miles away when it’s stormy. The machine is programmed to alert me when certain keywords are said by Yiddishe children.

In this case, I was finishing the tale of Rabbi Akiva and his students (which, b’hashgacha pratis, was part of my daily shiur) when the machine crackled to life. Apparently my name (one of the keywords) had been mentioned. The boys were busy discussing whether or not they should call me for help.

Within a minute, I was on my kfitza-board, wasting no time.

I zoomed right by the pizza delivery man’s car, bounced off the back of a school bus turning at the intersection, flipped over a branch that was sticking out into the sidewalk, ducked under a flying bird, and finally arrived at Darchei Chassidim’s playground. I got off the k-board, turned around, and found Laiby, Gedalia, and Meir facing me, relief plastered all over their frustrated faces.
