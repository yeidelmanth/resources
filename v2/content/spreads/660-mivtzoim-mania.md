Joining the Ranks Operation Tzivos Hashem

Was there ever something that you really wanted to do, but it felt impossible? Something that seemed so easy for other people but so hard for you? That’s how our school felt about joining Tzivos Hashem. We knew that other schools had exciting rallies and great base commanders. Their students could complete their missions and go up in rank, earn cool prizes and medals, and join Chidon. But our school wasn’t part of any of that. We’re far away in Brazil, and joining Tzivos Hashem seemed way too complicated.

Personally, though, our family signed up on our own. We did our missions online, but that was it. No rallies, no excitement in school, and no sense of doing it together. We wished that the whole school could join, but couldn’t figure out how. Then, one day, our friend’s mother, Mrs. Sigal, finally did! “I want my kids to feel more chayus when they do mitzvos,” she told our mother. “And I want them to feel more connected to the Rebbe. I will find a way for our school to join!” So she got to work. The first thing we needed was a base commander. It’s a big job that comes with many different responsibilities: leading rallies; helping with missions, promotions, and programs; and communicating with teachers, students, parents, and Tzivos Hashem HQ to make sure everything runs smoothly. But here’s the challenge: Most people in Brazil speak only Portuguese, but a base commander must also speak English. Mrs. Sigal knew that most of the English speakers in our school were busy shluchos, so finding the right person wouldn’t be easy. What would we do? Without a base commander, we couldn’t have Tzivos Hashem! One day, our amazing mother (who is a native English- speaker from Australia) made a bold decision. “I’ll be the base commander,” she told Mrs. Sigal. Even though Mommy was quite busy herself, she decided to do it as a z’chus for our bubby, who wasn’t well, and later, she continued in Bubby’s honor.

Now we could finally begin! Mrs. Sigal, our mother, and other staff members got to work putting everything into place. They showed everyone how to fill out their missions, made it super exciting to join the rallies, and arranged for the prizes and medals to reach us all the way in Brazil. But it wasn’t simple. There were still many differences between our school and other Tzivos Hashem bases. We didn’t let that stop us, and instead, found creative ways to make it all work. One challenge was the language. Our mother tried translating the rallies into Portuguese, but it was hard because she isn’t totally fluent yet. Instead, she asked students who know English to help translate. (In fact, one time, on our mother’s birthday, our six- year-old sister announced, “Mommy, I give you a bracha to speak perfect Portuguese, so that you can translate the rallies all by yourself!” We all laughed. That was what she wanted most!) Another challenge was the calendar. The seasons in Brazil are opposite the ones in half of the world. For some parts of the year, schools in America are open and Tzivos Hashem campaigns are running at full force. So while they’re all busy memorizing Tanya, learning niggunim, and fighting V’halachta Bidrachav battles, we aren’t even in school; it’s our summer break! But of course, we don’t want to miss out, so we instead learn everything during our winter months (when American kids are on their summer break). Baruch Hashem, our entire school is proud to be part of Tzivos Hashem. When Chidon started this past year, every single student who was able to participate, did! Kids from our school made it to the game show finale in New York—and some of us even won trophies!

Music blasted, Tzivos Hashem flags waved, and the boys even performed with a marching band! General Shimmy first led a special rally just for us, and then, at a Tzivos Hashem-themed lunch, he farbrenged with us, sharing uplifting stories and powerful messages. We even got to play soccer together! (And boy, is General Shimmy an excellent goalie!) After he left, each of us got a special gift: a picture magnet showing each chayol receiving rank medals from him. We still have them hanging on our fridge at home, and so do all our friends! The magnets remind us of those two special days spent with General Shimmy and the excitement he gave us to keep fighting our Yetzer Hara and continue on as a proud Tzivos Hashem base. Today, every one of the 70 children in our school who are eligible to join participates in Tzivos Hashem! We cheer each other on, work hard on completing our missions, and feel so proud to be part of Hashem’s Army. It’s hard to believe that not so long ago, it had all seemed truly impossible. It wasn’t easy, because all the Chidon books and tests are in English. But that didn’t stop us! One girl signed up for English classes, some families hired tutors, and some of our friends used AI translations to help them study. Everyone was excited to be involved. One day last year, something unbelievable happened. General Shimmy Weinbaum came to our school for a visit—in person!

Chayolim, do you know someone who isn’t part of Tzivos Hashem yet? A friend, a neighbor, a cousin … maybe even a whole school! Go help them join, and show them that it is possible!
