when Mr. Mikhailov came to discuss The Great Parade? And that he wants to sponsor it this year, but only if 100,000 kids attend?” “Yes, I remember that.” “So Rabbi Benjaminson clarified that he doesn’t mean getting 100,000 kids to the parade here in New York, but 100,000 kids in parades all over the world! Everyone has to get someone to come! It’s—” my words were tumbling out fast. My mother smiled. She knew me well. “So how’s it going?” “Baruch Hashem! The plan is that each kid in Chayolei Tzivos Hashem gets a TGP (The Great Parade) T-shirt for himself, plus 20 more to give to kids who don’t yet know about the parade. Kinda like an invitation to come, and to spread the word to their own friends. And that’s what I was saying—I finally gave out all 20 T-shirts!” Whew, I was still speaking a million words a minute. Sloooow down! I took a deep breath. “So Ma, now I’m asking for your help. We need to reach parents to make sure that they bring their kids. Any ideas?” “Hmmm…” my mother thought for a moment. Then, her eyes sparkled as she reached for her keys. “If you want to reach mothers, Sruli, I know just the place!” A few minutes later, we pulled up to the largest supermarket in Brooklyn. Wow, I thought to myself, This place is huge! How can we get all of these shoppers’ and their kids to the parade?

I needed to find one commonality here…what could be used to reach everyonel? “Ma, can we walk quickly through the store? I want to look around for ideas.” “Of course, Sruli. I’m right here with you.” My mother is amazing—totally supportive of me and my creativity! We hurried up and down the aisles, but I didn’t notice anything in particular. When we neared the exit, I slowed my pace, my eyes lighting up. Shopping bags! Things moved quickly from there. With a wink to my mother, the two of us rushed to the door labeled “Staff Only”, and found the manager. I explained the parade to him, and asked if TGP logos could be printed on his store’s bags. Not only was he amazed, but he loved the idea. He even offered to speak to managers of other stores in the national chain! Numbers and equations started spinning in my head…10…20…30 million bags! Within a few days, every person coming out of that supermarket was transformed into a walking advertisement for The Great Parade. Well, bags are nice, but we needed something more. And I wanted every chayol and their family to feel responsible for the parade’s success. As I had learned many times, the Rebbe wants everyone to be a recruiter. That evening, I organized an emergency conference call with Tzivos Hashem bases across the world. Australia, England, Chicago, LA—even South Africa and Brazil. The world needs to know about the parade! Together, we brainstormed

ideas. Mr. Mikhailov's money was backing us, but what can guarantee people? After some discussion, a plan was born: plaster the world! Days later, trucks filled with all sorts of advertising material pulled up in front of every TH base. We’re talking flyers, bumper stickers, bags, T-shirts, and more, all sporting the TGP logo. Before they realized what was happening, the world media was bustling with excitement! Reporters all over couldn’t keep track of the largest advertising campaign ever seen. Pictures of malls, highways, parks, and even gas stations appeared in every form of newscasting. The plan was working! Chayolim across the globe were given a mission: pick an area, and cover it with TGP advertisements. Order whatever you need through your school rep, and you will receive it within four days. Chayolim picked highways, shopping malls, public parks, and every area in between. Those who chose highways contacted the billboard companies. Those who picked malls focused on bags; plastic, paper, canvas—you name it. (Can you imagine every person in the mall walking around with TGP bags?) Together with their parents or base commander, many chayolim drove up and down the streets, stopping at busy locations and handing out flyers and T-shirts to every Jewish kid in the area. A reporter standing outside the largest mall in America made headlines in major newspapers:

Behind him stood a giant 50-foot TGP banner, and each of the 50,000 cars parked in the lot had a TGP bumper magnet placed on their windshield. Similar news items went viral from Israel, Russia, and France. The world was vibrating with TGP excitement! As manager of the advertising campaign, the work was non- stop. Each day after school was spent ordering, coordinating, and making sure that things were under control. Three days before the parade, I collapsed into bed for some much-needed sleep. When I awoke, all I could see was a huge TGP logo. I thought I was still dreaming…until I heard my giggling brothers. While I was asleep, they had hung a huge TGP poster right on top of my head. Okay, I’ll give them credit— that was a pretty good prank. Sit back up, negel vasser, jump out of bed. Suddenly, I realized that I had forgotten one very important detail: the hookup! Our goal to reach 100,000 people was great, but we’ll need to connect all these people at the event! I frantically called Rabbi Benjaminson. He calmed me down and told me not to worry: with the

advertising taken care of, organizers of the actual parade focused on the rest— including the live hookup. Phhhhhyew! Baruch Hashem! The day of the parade finally arrived. No words can describe the surge of excitement. What a magnificent celebration of the Rashbi: True achdus, and abundantly powerful Jewish pride! As you probably guessed, the numbers soared far beyond 100,000. When the parade in Moscow was featured on the screen, I noticed Mr. Mikhailov smiling broadly, pointing to his TGP T-shirt. What a special z’chus he has in all of this! I marched alongside chayolim of my school, and saluted as I passed by the Rebbe’s place. I knew that the Rebbe was smiling with us, too. To be continued…
