For many years, Reb Levik was the rav of Yekaterinoslav, a large city in Ukraine, helping Yidden stay strong in their Yiddishkeit. But one day, everything changed. The police arrested Reb Levik for spreading Torah and mitzvos, and sent him to a small, faraway village in Kazakhstan called Chi'ili. His wife, Rebbetzin Chana, bravely traveled there to be with him.

Sukkos was coming, but all that Reb Levik and Rebbetzin Chana had was one small rented room. There was no shul, no minyan, and hardly any other Yidden. How would they get a sukkah for Yom Tov?

She looked at the unfinished hut. “And where is the roof?”

“We don’t have enough money yet,” Reb Levik answered. “For now, we will use leaves. Before winter comes, we will build it properly.”

Sukkos soon arrived. One other Yid joined them for the Yom Tov meals. They ate the little food they had, feeling so happy to be sitting in a sukkah.

Back home, they would celebrate with hundreds of Yidden at Reb Levik’s shul. But here, there was no shul, no crowd, and not even a Sefer Torah. It was just the two of them and their small room.

The Very Stones Were Dancing ֲעֲֶצֶֶרֶת תש״ג ְׁשִׁמִיִנִי

The familiar words of ַָאַָּתָה ָהְָרְֵאֵָת filled the room. Then, Reb Levik began to dance. Step by step, he danced around the narrow space between his bed and the table, singing the same special tune that he would sing back home—the one famously called “Reb Levik’s Niggun”. As the night continued, his voice rose higher, his steps grew quicker, and his dancing became more alive as his deep love for the Torah shone brightly on his face.

Rebbetzin Chana was filled with emotion as she watched her husband dance. She remembered how it was back at home: When Reb Levik would dance on Simchas Torah, it seemed like the very stones danced with him!”

And that night, as Reb Levik danced alone in the tiny room, it felt the same way.

Over the following year, more Yidden arrived in the village. On Rosh Hashana, they all gathered together to daven. They even got hold of a Sefer Torah! But who would lead? None of them knew how.

And so, Reb Levik stepped forward. He led the davening, leined from the Torah, and blew the shofar that Rebbetzin Chana had brought from home. He did it all in his special, heartfelt way, leaving many people deeply inspired.

Thankfully, that Simchas Torah, he didn't have to celebrate alone. Full of joy, Reb Levik sang and danced together with the other Yidden. And in those moments, many of them forgot their troubles. They felt uplifted to a higher, holier place.

This is how Reb Levik spent his last two years of Simchas Torah, תש”ג )1942( and תד"ש )1943(:

One year, he danced alone in a small room, and the next, he shared the joy with fellow Yidden, carrying them above their pain and filling them with simcha.

No matter his surroundings, Reb Levik rose above them all and celebrated with holiness and joy, uplifting others along with him— until the very stones were dancing, too.
