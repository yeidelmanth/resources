"Oh no!" Aharon said, looking worried. "We're going to miss class! What should we do?"

We both wanted to do the right thing and go inside, but we were stuck.

Then, I had an idea. "Let's say the Rebbe's kapitel," I told him. "The Rebbe will help."

“Yes!” I said confidently. “When we ask the Rebbe for help, he finds a way!”

My family is on shlichus in Nashville, Tennessee. Since there’s no Chabad school nearby, I go to the local Jewish day school.

There are all types of kids in my class. Some know a lot about Yiddishkeit, while others are just starting to learn. As a shliach of the Rebbe, I try to be a dugma chaya of what it means to live as a Yid and a Chassid. Whenever I can, I help my classmates learn Torah and do mitzvos.

One boy, Aharon\*, became my good friend. He was very curious and loved asking me all kinds of questions. I really enjoyed helping him learn, and it was so special to see his excitement for Yiddishkeit grow.

But there was one thing Aharon couldn’t understand. Whenever I told him about the Rebbe, he would shake his head. “That can’t be true!” he’d say. “How can the Rebbe help so many people? How can he know what everyone needs?”

No matter how many amazing stories I shared, he just wouldn't believe them.

Then, one day during recess, Aharon asked me again about the Rebbe. I was telling him about how the Rebbe is connected to every Yid, when suddenly I noticed something strange.

“Where’s everyone?" I asked, looking around in confusion.

We had been so busy talking that we didn't hear the teachers calling everyone to come inside. All the other kids had already gone to class!

We jumped up and ran to the school doors, but they were locked. We knocked and knocked, hoping someone would hear us, but no one came.
