When it was time for mincha, Baruch chose a small shul on the outskirts of town. He didn’t want to be recognized, but wished to remain as private in Vitebsk as he had been on the road.

After ma’ariv, it was time for Baruch to fulfill the promise he’d made to his aunt. Freida’s directions were precise, and Baruch easily found the house: a well-kept cottage on the corner, two streets to the right of the yeshiva.

Devorah Leah and Yosef Yitzchak were home. They were both overjoyed to see Baruch, and would have loved to speak with him for quite a while. But Baruch kept his conversation polite and brief. He let them know, just as he had told Kaddish and Freida, that he was just in for a short visit, and they shouldn’t count on him staying for longer.
