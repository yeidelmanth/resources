Did you know there’s a fast day this Friday? I didn’t even know there could be a fast on Erev Shabbos. But on our kitchen calendar, the Friday box says “Fast of Asara B’Teves” with a picture of Yerushalayim.

So I asked my mother, “What’s this fast all about? It can’t be

the destruction of the Beis Hamikdash—that’s Tisha B’Av! Are you really gonna fast even though it’s Erev Shabbos?”

“Sure,” Mommy said. “Asara B’Teves is so serious that even if it fell on Shabbos itself, we would fast. Luckily, that doesn’t happen because of the way our calendar is set up. It’s so serious because it’s the day that Nevuchadnetzar’s army surrounded Yerushalayim,

That made sense in the moment, but now that I think about it, I’m not sure I get it. I mean, I could think of some awful places to be stuck in—like the elevator at my school that barely works, or a giant can of mushrooms (not my taste!). But being stuck in Yerushalayim sounds wonderful to me. So why in the world is it a fast day? ׳ טבת ה

Sometimes I wonder: Do other kids have a sneaky, pesky, stinky Yetzer Hara like me? Does he also get other kids so frustrated at their siblings, that they’ll almost do something really, really not nice to them?

This morning started off like any good Sunday morning. I said Modeh Ani, washed negel vasser, davened, and did all the important things a chayol should do—especially on a day like today, Hey Teves.

I looked out the window and greeting me was the most luscious, sparkly sea of snow I’ve ever dreamed of. Before you could say “Meet you outside!” I was bundled up and knee-deep in that cold, delicious frost.

Then came the snowman: I rolled each ball carefully, from one end of the yard to the next, back and forth, till they were perfectly round and big as bike tires, and stacked each one neatly on top of the other.

Just then, little Menucha ran outside. “I wanna make a snowman!” she squealed, and, as fast as she could trudge through the snow, came hurtling towards my masterpiece.

“No Menucha,” I said, “this is my snowman! You can make your own, over there.”

But Menucha didn’t listen. She reached my snowman and started patting on her own snow.

Take Menucha away!!!” But Mommy didn’t hear. I could feel my heart pounding. And then it happened— Plop. Crunch.

My perfect, shining piece of art came smashing down, head first into the snow.

I wasn’t even thinking. I saw my gloved-up hands lift themselves and aim for Menucha, ready to push her down. No one was watching.

I looked up and remembered something important. Even though I can’t see anyone around, there is always Someone watching. And I wanted that Someone to be proud of me.

I took a step back, and breathed in real deep…then out…and in again.

It had just been my Yetzer Hara trying to take hold of me. Getting Menucha back wouldn’t make anything better anyway.

“Okay Menucha,” I heard myself say, “don’t worry about it. It’s just a snowman. We can make a new one.”

I’m still upset about my snowman breaking. But I sure am glad that I didn’t get into a fight with Menucha. At lunchtime, Mommy helped me talk with her about not touching my things and I hope she understood.

But…why is it so hard to hold myself back? This time I did it, but I’m not always successful. Why did Hashem give us a Yetzer Hara anyway?

It’s all coming together now. The reason why we have a stinky Yetzer Hara, why it picks on me so much, and why there’s a fast day on Friday.

It all happened this morning. I was getting my coat on quickly so I could make the school bus on time, when I saw Menucha running off down the hallway with my boots on her feet. I was about to really give it to her. Enough is enough! But before I could, I heard my smart big sister say, “Esty, I know you’re frustrated and in a rush, but don’t let your Yetzer Hara get you, once it does—you’re trapped!”

I wondered about that all the way to school. I had a long way to walk, since I did miss the bus. What does that mean, once the Yetzer Hara gets you, you’re trapped?
