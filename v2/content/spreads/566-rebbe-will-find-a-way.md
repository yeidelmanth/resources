It was a forwarded email from Chabad on Campus, written by a student at the University of Minnesota!

Tatty read the email aloud. “Can you please send a rabbi to our university? We could really use one here.”

“This is truly unbelievable!” Mommy said. “What greater sign could we have asked for?”

At that moment, all their worries and doubts disappeared. With confidence and excitement, Tatty and Mommy moved to Minnesota and opened a Chabad House at the university. Even though there have been many challenges along the way, our parents always remind us: “We have the Rebbe’s brachos. There is nothing to be worried about.”

On the way back from the Ohel, a notification popped up on Tatty’s phone. Was this the sign they were looking for?

When Tatty and Mommy got married, they were excited to go on shlichus. They had learned the Rebbe’s sichos that said, “The mission of our generation is to bring Moshiach through spreading Yiddishkeit and Chassidus.” And they wanted to be involved!

Now, they just needed to find a place. One of the options was the University of Minnesota.

“I don’t know if we should go there…” Mommy said doubtfully. “If it’s such a big university, why have no shluchim moved out there yet?”

“Also, the winters in Minnesota are icy cold,” added Tatty. “I’m not sure we can handle that kind of weather.”

Mommy had another concern: “I don’t think they have as many Jewish students as other universities. Will there be enough students to support a Chabad House?”

Tatty and Mommy continued their search, but they kept thinking about the University of Minnesota. Even though it wasn’t a perfect option, it still seemed like the best choice.

So with hope in their hearts, they made their decision: they would go on shlichus to the University of Minnesota.

But deep inside, they still felt worried. What if it would be too cold? What if there weren’t enough students? What if they couldn’t raise enough money to support the Chabad House? They weren’t completely sure that they had made the right choice.

‘You know what?” Tatty said one day. “Let’s go to the Ohel and ask the Rebbe for guidance.”

Mommy agreed. Our parents went to the Ohel and asked for a bracha, telling the Rebbe all their hopes, dreams, and fears about their soon-to-be shlichus.

As they sat in the taxi on their way home, Tatty got a notification on his phone.
