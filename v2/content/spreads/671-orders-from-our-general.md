<p class="lead">Connection Become Stronger?</p>

When we do something three times, it becomes strong and lasting. In halacha, something done three times in a row is in a different category than something done only once or twice. And when we make a hachlata, it becomes a true part of us after we’ve kept it three times in a row.

Since Levi was Leah Imeinu's third son, he represents this idea of a chazaka. And that's why, after giving birth to him, Leah knew that she would have a stronger connection to Yaakov Avinu.

also hints to the entire Yiddishe nation and our connection to Hashem, who is sometimes called our "Husband”.

What helps a mitzvah become part of who you are?

Doing it once with lots of chayus.

Doing it again and again until it becomes a chazaka.

And \[Leah Imeinu\] gave birth to a son, and she said, "Now, this time, my husband will be attached to me, because I gave birth to three sons."

Therefore, He named him Levi.

How do we become more "attached" to Hashem? This happens by doing mitzvos. The special attachment between the Yidden and Hashem is a strong one, and it lasts forever.

<span class="mc">The children of Levi also have their own special mission. The job of each Levi is to attach with, with their avodah in the Beis Hamikdash.</span>

Shevet Levi's job teaches us an important lesson: We must help every Yid in their avodah, too. After all, every Yid is called Hashem’s Kohen! And so, just as Shevet

Levi helped the Kohanim with their holy work, every Yiddishe child must help other Yidden learn more Torah and do more mitzvos. How lucky we are to help other Yidden become more attached to Hashem!

Where else in Yiddishkeit do we find the idea of a chazaka?

How can you get friends to join you in doing mitzvos?

Go around the table, and everyone shares a way that you can become more “attached” to

Hashem. The first person starts with an Alef, then a Beis, then a Gimmel. Keep going until you’ve gone through the entire Alef-Beis!
