Do you want to know more details about the miraculous story with Sancheiriv? This wicked king from Ashur was extremely powerful. He conquered many nations and mixed them all up. He even sent ten of the shevatim into golus, and to this day, we don’t know where they settled! But Sancheiriv didn’t stop there. He also wanted to conquer Malchus Yehudah—the remaining Yidden who lived in and around Yerushalayim. He came with a massive army— far bigger than Nevuchadnetzar’s army—and positioned his men outside the city walls. Truthfully, Sancheiriv could have conquered Yerushalayim that very day, but Hashem in His kindness gave the Yidden more time. Sancheiriv decided to first rest for the night. When the Yiddishe king, Chizkiyahu, heard what was happening, he immediately did teshuva. Hashem accepted his tefillos and sent a malach to kill thousands of Sancheiriv’s soldiers that night. The rest of the army, including Sancheiriv himself, was filled with fear and ran away. As we see, in just one night, one hour, and one moment the entire siege was gone and the Yidden emerged with great victory!

Asara B’Teves is the first of the four fasts connected to the destruction of the First Beis Hamikdash. On this day, the armies of Nevuchadnetzar surrounded Yerushalayim with a siege, meaning that nobody and nothing was allowed to come in or go out. For two and a half years, the city was sealed. Much of the food and water had run out, and people were dying of starvation. Seeing that the Yidden were now too weak to properly fight back, Nevuchadnetzar’s men broke into Yerushalayim. They destroyed homes, killed many people, and sent the rest into golus as slaves. Finally, on the tragic day of Tisha B’Av, our holy Beis Hamikdash was set on fire.

As we see, what happened on Asara B’Teves started the entire process leading to the Churban. It is an extra-strict ta’anis because of that. For this reason, we fast and beg Hashem with extra effort to speedily build the third Beis Hamikdash, now!

Do you know why we fast on sad days? It isn’t just to make us hungry. Fasting is supposed to refocus us to the sadness and pain of the day so that we’ll do proper teshuva. Many kehillos had a minhag that on every ta’anis, the rav would speak to the community after mincha, encouraging them to do teshuva. These speeches were called “דִבְרֵי ּכִיּבּוׁשִין—words that conquer the heart.” But for whatever reason, as the years continued, most places no longer kept this minhag. On Asara B’Teves of (1977) תשל”ח, the Rebbe announced that we should bring it back. From then on, the Rebbe would say a sicha after mincha of almost every fast day. As chassidim today, we can listen to or learn a sicha after mincha of the ta’anis. This will encourage us to do teshuva and bring Moshiach, transforming the fast day into a true Yom Tov!

Close your eyes and try to remember what life was like for you two and a half years ago. What games did you play? Which snacks did you eat? What did you learn in school?

It’s probably hard to remember so many details from back then. Two and a half years is a long time! Do you know what else took two and a half years? That's right: the siege around Yerushalayim. But why did it take so long?

The Midrash tells us that Hashem made it take such a long time to give the Yidden a chance to do teshuva. Because if they would have done teshuva, there would’ve been no need for the Churban. Hashem would have protected the city and the Beis Hamikdash would not have been destroyed.

If you knew that a special day was coming, how far in advance would you begin preparing? Yidden have the minhag to prepare for 30 days before every Yom Tov (known as “שְׁלֹושִׁים יֹום לִפְנֵי הַחַג”). We prepare for Rosh Hashana during the 30 days of Elul, and begin learning about Pesach on Purim, which is 30 days before. The same thing is true of Yud Shvat. The Rebbe encouraged us to begin preparing for Yud Shvat on Asara B’Teves. What’s the best Yud Shvat hachana? Adding in more good things, of course! The Rebbe said that everyone, even us children, are to learn more Torah, give more tzedaka, and daven with more kavana. We also strengthen our hiskashrus with the Rebbe by learning his teachings, joining farbrengens, and watching Rebbe videos. So, as Yud Shvat approaches, let’s think of the hachlatos we can take on. When we prepare properly, we will be all ready when the heilige day arrives—ready to greet Moshiach!

That’s actually what happened many years before the siege. Generations earlier, Sancheiriv, the king of Ashur, also wanted to conquer Yerushalayim. But the Yiddishe king, Chizkiyahu, did teshuva, and Hashem ruined Sancheiriv’s plans.

The same thing would have happened to Nevuchadnetzar and his plan if the Yidden would have done teshuva. So, Hashem waited for two and half years to see if they would. Sadly, they didn’t, and the city fell.

The lesson for us is clear. Sometimes, Hashem sends challenges as opportunities for teshuva. He waits to see if we’ll learn from our mistakes and come closer to Him. When we do, Hashem fixes things in a moment.

So, the next time something doesn’t seem to be going right, remember that it might be a reminder from Hashem that it is time to do teshuva! How can we think, speak, or act better? How can we come closer to Hashem? Let’s use the reminder of Asara B’Teves to do teshuva and bring Moshiach now!

Asara B’Teves comes with special lessons and minhagim for us chassidim.
