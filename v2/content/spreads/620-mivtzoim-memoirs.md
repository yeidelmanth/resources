My feet have never hurt this much! But boy, was it worth it.

After shacharis this morning, I joined my cousin and some other bochurim on a long trek to blow shofar in a hospital. He kept asking me if I was sure I could walk it since I’m only 11, but there

was no way I was gonna say no! And guess what? I was so excited to help people do this mitzvah that my feet didn’t even hurt one bit. (Well … maybe a bit. A-lot-a-bit, actually. But I didn’t tell Naftali that.)

Afterwards, we ran back to 770 to join the tashlich parade up Eastern Parkway. By then, my feet were so numb that I couldn’t feel them anymore.

Mivtzoim—they feel like they’ve been around forever! Can you even imagine life without them? Yet, unbelievably, before the Rebbe introduced each of the mitzvah campaigns that we know so well, they simply didn’t exist.

Join us as we explore imaginary old diaries that could have belonged to kids just like you— kids who might have lived during the time when the Rebbe first announced many of these mivtzoim.

Shofar was the first mivtza to be established! As you can imagine, the idea of blowing shofar in the streets just blew people’s minds away. Lucky Yitzi, because he lived when this mivtza first started—and lucky you, because you get to read about it!

I feel like my head is inside the biggest shofar in Crown Heights while it’s being blown, and it’s not because I’m fasting (because I’m just seven, you know). It’s because I think I just heard the shofar four bajillion gazillion times this Rosh Hashana!

I’ll start from the beginning. Tatty came home the other day with big news: The Rebbe wants us chassidim to go out to the streets and blow shofar for people. Imagine that! Shofar outside of shul! Who ever thought of such a thing?!

It made me wonder—what’s next? I mean, if you can blow shofar in the street … I just can’t wait to find out!

Eastern Parkway early Rosh Hashana morning, asking people if they were Jewish. But more than that, it was so special. Tatty asked an old man sitting on a bench, his hand on his cane, if he could blow shofar for him. “אִיְך האָב פאַרגעֶסן אִינְגאַנצֶן—I completely forgot about shofar! ” the old man exclaimed.

P.S. By the way, if you are wondering why I’m starting a diary, it’s because Tatty told me that amazing things are happening now, and I should write them down for my grandchildren to read. I asked Tatty if he also writes a diary, but he laughed and said, “Yitzi look at my gray hairs! You think I have time to write a diary?”

What do gray hairs have to do with writing a diary? I’m not sure, but I’d better get in as much as I can before I go gray!

Wow, you’re dusty. I’m seeing that my last entry was quite a few years back! I’m already thirty- four now … It’s been a while since I’ve picked you up, old pal. And for good reason! I'm a busy tatty, rebbi, and bus driver on the side.

Well, if you are wondering why I remembered you today, it’s because this morning, seven- year-old Chaim came up to me and asked, “Tatty, I also want to have mesiras nefesh to leave the Rebbe for Tishrei just like cousin Chanan, and travel to Paraguay to blow shofar for people!”

I think he was more excited about the plane ride, but, suddenly, I whooshed right back to when I was seven years old and mivtza shofar was first established. It made me realize that I haven’t written in a while, and I think it’s time to catch you up.

Since I last wrote, I have been zoche to hear the Rebbe speak publicly about mivtza shofar several times. Two years ago, the Rebbe spoke about how we should blow shofar in prisons (!) and anywhere Yidden can be found, so that they, too, can crown Hashem as King. Personally, I haven’t done that yet, but some of my nephews have!

The year before that, the Rebbe declared that blowing shofar for patients literally gives life to their neshamos. Indeed, that year, when I went on mivtzoim, I was so moved to hear a patient say, “I knew Lubavitch would not let us down.”

In a different farbrengen a few years back, the Rebbe also asked that chassidim in Eretz Yisroel blow shofar for the soldiers in the IDF. My Israeli cousin, Mendy, lives near an army base, and has walked there in the heat every year since.

To think that in just 27 years we’ve gone from blowing shofar on the parkway to—one sec, is that Dovber crying? I’d better run and give him his pacifier.

P.S. Hey, what’s Chaim writing over there? It doesn’t look like homework. Is that a diary?!

I’ve been so busy these days in yeshiva that I barely ever have time to write. Thing is, I just heard something really amazing, and if my grandchildren do discover my diary one day, I want them to know about this:

I heard that just about every Jewish patient in Brooklyn hospitals heard shofar this year! The Rebbe’s goal is being fulfilled! Even the hospital officials gave permission for chassidim to go to the rooms and blow shofar for people, because they realize that having a refuah shleima in ruchniyus helps the patients to have a refuah shleima in gashmiyus, too!

Do you understand?! Many patients who are stuck in bed had given up on hearing the shofar this year. And yet, the Rebbe’s chassidim were there for them.
