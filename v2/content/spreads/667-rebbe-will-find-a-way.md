That Friday, Tatty traveled to New York for a family simcha. But before going anywhere else, he went straight to the Ohel and asked for a bracha that the mezuza cases should be found.

Less than an hour later, Mommy started getting messages from people with amazing news: A woman who worked in a nearby community had just posted a message online: I got a box for Mrs. Kornfeld. Does anyone know who that is?

It turned out that the box had been delivered to the wrong address. It had been sitting in this woman’s golf cart for over three weeks! She had gone away on vacation soon after it arrived, and only now, right after Tatty davened at the Ohel, did she try to find the owner.

Baruch Hashem, all of the mezuza cases were there, safe and sound. The women were excited to hang them up, and before long, many more homes in our community had mezuzos on their doorposts!

was amazed at the Rebbe’s brachos, and how he always finds a way to help.

The mezuza cases were sent out to be finished. Why didn’t they come back?

A few years ago, Mommy organized a special Jewish women’s event in our community all about the mitzvah of mezuza. Many homes in our area didn’t yet have mezuzos, and Mommy hoped that this would inspire more women to put them up.

For the activity, everyone designed their own mezuza case using a special kit. The cases were made with glass and needed to be finished by an artist before they could be used.

The event was a big success. Everyone had a great time, and the mezuza cases came out beautifully.

Right away, Mommy mailed all the cases to the artist so she could finish them. Then, she waited for them to be returned.

At first, Tatty and Mommy were busy and didn’t think about it too much. But after a few weeks, Mommy checked in with the artist.

“I already sent them back,” the artist said, surprised. “They should have arrived weeks ago.”

The artist gave Mommy the tracking number, and Mommy looked it up right away. “It shows that the package was delivered,” she said. “But we never got it!”

Tatty checked the cameras outside of our house to see what happened on that day, but there was no sign of any delivery.

Mommy’s heart sank. The women were waiting for their mezuza cases, and now, it looked like they were lost! But Mommy was also worried. What if people wouldn’t want to come to future events because of this mix up?

The artist kindly offered to remake all of the mezuza cases, and Mommy sent out an email to everyone, explaining what had happened. Still, it was very upsetting.

Shaya, 9 Chana Kornfeld, 7 Cheder Menachem Mendel Seattle
