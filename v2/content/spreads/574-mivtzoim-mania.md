They sat at the edge of their seats, full of stress and confused by the unknown. Their eyes constantly moved between checking the clocks and watches, and looked towards the door. Their young son was having heart surgery in the next room over.

And, as their own hearts pounded with fear, the worried parents hoped desperately for good news.

After what seemed like forever, the door to the operating room opened. Both parents jolted up out of their seats as the surgeon walked towards them. Sadly, he did not look like he was bringing good news.

“As far as your son’s heart is concerned, the operation was successful,” the surgeon slowly began.

“But during the operation, an important nerve that helps the body breathe was damaged.

“This means that in the worst case, your son will be paralyzed for life and will need a special machine in order to breathe.

“In the best case, he may recover in a few years and start to breathe on his own without the machine, but he will not be able to speak. And even if he does speak, he will remain disabled forever and will need constant medical care.”

The surgeon paused, and then added, “I know that this is not easy for you to hear. We did the best that we could. Your son will be brought to the recovery room shortly. You can see him then.” With that, he turned and walked back into the operating room.

As soon as the surgeon left, the parents collapsed into their seats and burst into tears. It felt as if their whole world had come crashing down on top of them.

By Hashgacha Pratis, the chossid Reb Moshe Rabinovitz was also in the waiting room at the time. When he heard the parents’ heart-breaking sobs, he himself was filled with pain.

There must be something I can do to help them! Rabbi Rabinovitz thought to himself.

Just then, he remembered about Mivtza Ois B’Sefer Torah. The Rebbe said many times that children having their own letter in the special Sefer Torah brings brachos to them and to the whole world.

This is a way I can help! The chossid thought to himself. Armed with the Rebbe’s mivtza and kind words of support, Rabbi Rabinovitz approached the parents.

He gently told them about the Children’s Sefer Torah, and suggested that they should buy their son a letter for protection and brachos. The heartbroken parents were grateful for the idea. They immediately bought a letter for their little boy, hoping that it would bring the brachos he so desperately needed.

A few hours went by. Finally, the boy woke up from the operation. To his parents’ surprise and delight, there were no signs that he had trouble breathing. It seemed that there were no problems at all! The boy’s body had responded well to the surgery, and was working just as a healthy body should. He was a bit weak during his recovery time, causing him to speak in

a quiet voice, but the surgeon’s words did not come true at all. Nothing short of a miracle!

As the boy got back into regular life, his parents told him how Rabbi Rabinovitz’s suggestion had brought such an incredible turn-around. The family remained forever grateful to the Rebbe and his devoted chossid.

Chayolim, do you have an ois in the Children’s Torah? Have you or your parents received the certificate in the mail listing your special name and the parsha containing your letter?

Think of a relative, friend, or neighbor who might not yet have a letter of their own. Reach out and tell them about this special mivtza. As the Rebbe says, and as Rabbi Rabinovitz told the parents in the story, these letters are extremely powerful and important for the child who buys it and for the safety of the whole world!

Whenever we look at our Ois B’Sefer Torah certificates, we remember that the Rebbe looks after his beloved Yidden and knows exactly what we need for a healthy and happy life. We hope that you’ll spread the word, too!
