Many miles away and far across the ocean, we felt the Rebbe and Rebbetzin’s presence clearly in our shlichus.

When I was just a few months old, my family moved on shlichus to Auckland, New Zealand. It was a big change from living in Crown Heights. There were no shuls or Jewish schools in Auckland. There was no kosher meat, chicken, milk, or bread. My parents had to ship kosher food to our house and keep it in big freezers where it would last us for a few months.

Every walk down the block, every trip to the store, and each outing to the park was a chance to find Yidden. Baruch Hashem, after just a few weeks, Tatty and Mommy had met a lot of people who were excited to learn about Yiddishkeit.

By the time I was one, we already had a minyan every Shabbos, many guests for Shabbos meals, and a Sunday Hebrew school.

But none of the Yidden whom we met had kids my age. Each day, when Mommy took me to the park near our apartment, I had to play by myself.

Back in New York, Bubby was worried that I was lonely. The next time she went to the Ohel, she went to daven at Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka’s kever, whom I am named after. She told the Rebbetzin how proud she is that I’m a shlucha of the Rebbe, and that she wishes I would have a friend to play with.
