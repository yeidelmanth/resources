“Just walking the streets of Cracow taught me so much; the kehilla there is like nothing I’ve seen before. Everyone, from the rav down to the tanners and the water carriers, has utmost regard for Torah; both for the revealed part of Torah—Gemara and poskim—as well as Mussar and Kabbalah. Some of the greatest talmidei chachamim in town have ordinary jobs. They are butchers, water carriers, and tailors. Their Torah study does not make them feel that they are ‘above physical labor.

It was one of those rare afternoons when Rav Yitzchak had a stretch of peace and quiet. No one had brought any chickens or cow lungs to examine, there were no letters with sha’alos4

waiting to be answered, and, baruch Hashem, there were few material worries, as well. The Eibershter5 had made King

Casimir III kind to the Jews. They could trade and prosper, and there were none of the dreadful libels and massacres that plagued Rav Yitzchak’s youth back in Germany. Finally, in the rav’s old age, he enjoyed a few hours here and there to devote to his personal Torah learning.
