As they continued talking, Rabbi Chitrik asked Tatty how his sister Dina was doing, because she had learned in his seminary many years earlier.

Tatty told Rabbi Chitrik that she was now a shlucha in a different city in China. Suddenly, there was a knock at the door. It was Dina!

When living in China seemed too difficult to bear, Tatty and Mommy asked the Rebbe for help.

When our family first moved on shlichus to Kowloon, a city in Hong Kong, China, it was not easy.

“We need to rethink this,” Tatty told Mommy after one particularly challenging day. “The problems we have here aren’t getting fixed, and things are just getting harder and harder. Maybe we should leave Hong Kong and go on shlichus somewhere else, somewhere easier.”

“It is hard living in China,” Mommy agreed. “But we’re the Rebbe’s shluchim. Let’s write to the Rebbe about what’s going on. Maybe that will help us figure out the right thing to do.”

Tatty sat down to write a letter. He described all the hardships they were facing, asking for a clear sign to know if they should stay in China or move somewhere else.

Tatty was surprised to see Rabbi and Mrs. Chitrik, shluchim from Tzfas, Eretz Yisrael, standing at his doorstep. “Wow, what are you doing here?” he blurted out.

Rabbi Chitrik smiled. “Let’s say l’chaim together and I’ll tell you.”

Once the guests were seated with mashke and farbeisin, Rabbi Chitrik began to speak:

“In the year תשל”ג (1973), I was zoche to be one of the bachurim who the Rebbe sent on shlichus to Melbourne, Australia. Before we left, the Rebbe called us into yechidus and recited a maamer. He then told us, ‘When the Frierdiker Rebbe would travel, he had a minhag to visit rabbanim in the cities he passed through.’ The Rebbe instructed us to do the same on our way to Australia, so we visited Rabbi Jakobovitz in London and Rabbi Zevin in Eretz Yisroel.

“From then on, I made a hachlata that whenever I have a stopover on a trip, I’d make sure to visit the local rav or shliach. When I realized I’d be stopping in Hong Kong on my flight to Australia, I asked my brother- in-law who often travels to Hong Kong for directions to the Chabad House. So here I am.” Rabbi Chitrik finished.

“We’re so happy to have you, but I must ask—Rabbi Avtzon is also a shliach in Hong Kong. He is even related to you and closer to your age. Why did you come here instead?” Tatty asked.
