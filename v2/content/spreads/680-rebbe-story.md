<p class="lead">Zeidy found the perfect campsite, but the deal suddenly fell apart.</p>

Zeidy stood in the middle of a large, empty field.

There was space here to build bunkhouses, a dining room, and everything else that Camp Gan Yisroel of Florida needed. A generous donor was even offering the land for free.

*Should I take this land to build a campground?* Zeidy wrote to the Rebbe.

It was before Gimmel Tammuz, and the Rebbe gave a clear answer: Only buy a *campsite that’s already been built.*

So Zeidy continued renting campsites while searching for a ready-built one to buy. But many were too expensive, and others weren’t the right fit.

Years passed. Soon, the high costs of rent left the camp owing a lot of money. We can’t *continue like this,* Zeidy realized. We need to *buy a campsite, or the camp will have to close.*

Six months before the summer of (2013), Zeidy heard of a nearby campsite being sold at an auction. He hurried over, and sure enough, it was fully built with everything that Gan Yisroel needed. *Maybe this is it!* he thought with excitement.

The auction started at $930,000, much less than other campsites. Zeidy offered one million dollars—and his offer was accepted!

Zeidy borrowed funds for the down payment and tried to raise the rest of the money. But many people who had promised to donate in the past couldn’t give in the end. Finally, Rabbi Sholom Ber Lipskar a”h found a donor who gave $500,000, and Zeidy raised another $100,000 from other supporters.

It was now days before the closing, and Zeidy still needed to raise a lot of money. A friend offered to lend him the rest, but the money would only arrive the following week.

“That won’t work,” the owners warned. “If the full payment isn’t here by Friday afternoon, the deal is canceled.”

Friday afternoon came and went. The owners gave back the down payment and canceled the deal.

After Shabbos, Zeidy flew to the Ohel and poured out his heart to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha for the owners to change their minds.

When Zeidy returned to Florida, he discovered what had happened.

Seven non-Jewish groups owned the property. When they heard that it

was being purchased by a Jewish camp, six of them wanted to cancel the deal. But they couldn’t legally do that after the agreement had already been made. Once Zeidy missed the deadline, though, they were able to stop the sale.

Zeidy started looking into other options. He hoped that there would be a way for Camp Gan Yisroel of Florida to open that summer.

Two months after Zeidy davened at the Ohel, the campsite was surprisingly put up for auction again. Zeidy placed another bid, this time for $1.2 million, and his offer was accepted!

Now, everything went smoothly. The donor who had given $500,000 before, now offered $1 million so the campgrounds wouldn’t be lost again. And Baruch Hashem, Zeidy quickly raised the rest of the money.

Before long, the keys were finally in his hands.

Zeidy drove straight to the campsite. “What changed?” he asked the manager.

“You won’t believe it,” the manager replied. “Other buyers offered more money. But this time, all seven organizations voted to accept your offer! I don't know what happened!”

Zeidy was filled with awe as he realized the tremendous bracha that had taken place. The same owners who hadn’t wanted to sell the campsite to a Jewish buyer now chose Gan Yisroel, even though others offered more money!

There was no other way to explain it. It was truly a miracle.

We learn from Zeidy what to do when we feel stuck: keep following the Rebbe’s hora’os, even when it’s hard. And when something seems impossible, ask for a bracha and trust that the Rebbe will find a way!
