I’ve been around for quite a while—since the beginning of time, in fact but I proudly chose to have the mitzvah performed, despite the pain. Yitzchak, you were only 8 days old at the time of your bris. You had no say in the matter at all. In fact, a baby that age has absolutely no concept of a mitzvah (or of anything, really)!

1\. I’ve seen many types of people: some righteous, some not; joyous folks, sluggish ones; obedient, reluctant—you name it, I’ve seen it.

Personally, I was usually obedient. Being a hard-working donkey isn’t the easiest of jobs, but it was a real honor to work for Avraham, and I always did my best to obey his every command.

I remember hearing Yitzchak retort: “You only gave up a few drops of blood for Hashem. If He would tell me right now to sacrifice myself, I wouldn’t hesitate for a second!”

My obedience, however—and all the obedience I’ve ever encountered—came nowhere close to Yitzchak’s!

Well, the next thing I knew, we were on our way, early in the morning, to offer a karban to Hashem in a mysterious place. We were a group of five: Myself, of course, shlepping all the supplies; Eliezer and Yishmael; and Avraham and Yitzchak.

8\. With Avraham’s concise reply (Hashem will provide it Himself, my son.), Yitzchak understood that Hashem intended that he, Yitzchak, was to be the karban.

He was ready to accept whatever Hashem sent his way with complete and utter obedience, total Kabbalas Ol b’simcha. If Hashem wanted it, Yitzchak was there to make it happen—joyfully. A true Soldier of Hashem, proud to serve his Master.

Avraham was planning to shecht his own son?!

And Yitzchak was expected to just let it happen?!

Most astonishing was Yitzchak’s reaction!

No, he didn’t run for his life or scream for help. He didn’t try to convince his father that bringing one’s child as a karban went against everything Avraham had ever thought, said or done in his lifelong mission of spreading Elokus. Yitzchak didn’t even ask for proof that this was indeed Hashem’s command, or question why he was to be killed, after Hashem clearly promised that he would father a mighty, everlasting nation!

As you know, Hashem stopped Avraham. He did not want Avraham to actually shecht Yitzchak; it was all a test. Both Avraham and Yitzchak passed the test with flying colors, and the kedusha that Yitzchak received from this test—from happily ascending the mizbe’ach as a karban for Hashem— remained with him for the rest of his life: the kedusha of a man ready to give his all for the will of his Creator.
