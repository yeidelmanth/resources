<p class="lead">Shabbos was getting closer, and Tatty still couldn’t decide. Then, the phone rang.</p>

It was Friday morning, and Tatty looked very worried. He paced from room to room, glancing at the clock again and again. He had a big decision to make before Shabbos, and he didn’t know what to do.

You see, Tatty and Mommy run YJP of West Palm Beach, where young Jewish professionals come to learn Torah, do mitzvos, and connect to Hashem. They also run the Northwood Shul, which is a warm and welcoming minyan for all types of Yidden.

Since they didn’t have a separate place for the minyan and all the programs, everything was happening right in our house. Baruch Hashem, as more and more people joined, it was starting to feel crowded.

Now, Tatty had the chance to lease a bigger place that could welcome many more Yidden. But it cost a lot of money, and he wasn’t sure if this was the right step.

Shabbos was getting closer, and time to sign the lease was running out. Tatty still couldn’t decide.

Suddenly, the phone rang.

It was a woman from the neighborhood. “Can you please come to put up a mezuza on my door before Shabbos?” she asked. “I really don’t want it to wait.”

Tatty paused. There was so much on his mind, but helping another Yid comes first. “I’m on my way,” he said.

As he drove, his thoughts were racing. Quietly, he spoke to the Rebbe in his heart. *Rebbe, please show me a sign so I’ll know what* to do.

<figure class="handimg inline"><img src="https://mashpia.com/resources/assets/img/da316c2849fd7877.png" alt="handwritten note"></figure>

When Tatty got there, he put up the mezuza while the woman watched. Then, to his surprise, she began talking about the Rebbe.

“I listen to podcasts and stories about the Rebbe all the time,” she said. “One thing that I’ve learned is to never push off a mitzvah. When a mitzvah can be done, you do it right away. That’s why I called you so urgently before Shabbos.”

Tatty was shocked. Here he was, so unsure if he should sign the lease, and this woman was telling him what she learned from the Rebbe: never push off doing a mitzvah!

At that moment, everything became clear. If getting a bigger place would help more Yidden learn Torah and do mitzvos, then the Rebbe wants him to sign the lease right away.

Tatty hurried to sign the contract and arrived at home just twenty minutes before Shabbos. He was amazed to hear that at the exact moment when the woman shared the Rebbe’s message with him, Mommy had been writing a letter to the Rebbe, asking for guidance about this decision!

That night, as we sat around our Shabbos table, everything felt calm again. Tatty and

Mommy were no longer unsure; they knew they had made the right choice.

Baruch Hashem, our new space is full of

Yidden who come to learn, daven, and do mitzvos. And we feel so connected to our Rebbe, who helped guide Tatty at exactly the right moment.
