Alex took Tatty to see the building, and showed him large classrooms that had their own separate entrance. “You can use this space for free,” he said.

For free?! That was exactly what Tatty had asked the Rebbe for just a few hours earlier!

Of course, Tatty accepted Alex’s generous offer. Thanks to that beautiful space, forty-six children joined our Hebrew school in its first year! And that was only the beginning. For the next six years, we used the building for Hebrew school and many other programs as well.

Tatty continued to help Alex put on tefillin each week. Then, just two months after that first BLT, Alex called with incredible news. “Rabbi, the doctors found a kidney donor!”

Alex asked Tatty to come to the hospital before the surgery and help him put on tefillin. Baruch Hashem, the procedure was successful, and Alex put on tefillin the next day to thank Hashem.

Since then, Alex has been putting tefillin on every day!

When Tatty wrote to the Rebbe about our Hebrew school, the brachos brought more good than he ever could have imagined.

Our Hebrew school got a beautiful home, where many Jewish children came to learn Torah and do mitzvos. Alex grew closer to Yiddishkeit and got the kidney transplant he so desperately needed.

The beautiful space continued helping our shlichus grow. For six years, we used it for Hebrew School, children’s programs, shiurim, and many other events. By then, baruch Hashem, our community had grown so much that we were able to open our own Chabad House!

After moving on shlichus, Tatty and Mommy were excited to see how many young families lived in our neighborhood. They immediately opened a Jewish Kids Club, and many children came.

A few months later, Tatty and Mommy began thinking bigger. “Let’s open a Hebrew school for next year!” they decided.

It was a great idea, but there was a big challenge: A Hebrew school would need to be in a proper school setting for families to feel comfortable sending their kids. But our shlichus was still new, and renting another space didn’t feel possible yet.

Tatty and Mommy kept coming back to the idea, but it just didn’t seem like it could work.

One Sunday, Tatty finished setting up early. Seeing that he had some time until the men would show up, he sat down to write to the Rebbe: We want to open a Hebrew school, he wrote. Please give us a bracha to find a beautiful space, either free or for a very low price.

He finished writing his letter, and the men began to arrive. One of them was new. His name was Alex.

After the program ended, Alex stayed behind. “Rabbi, I’m worried,” he said quietly. “My kidneys aren’t working properly, and I’ve been very sick for almost two years. I need a kidney donor—someone who can give me a healthy kidney—but it’s very hard to find one. I’m getting weaker and weaker, and I don’t know what will be.”

Tatty listened with compassion. He spoke to Alex about bitachon, and how even in hard situations, a Yid trusts that Hashem will help.

Alex thanked Tatty and then turned to leave. Suddenly, he stopped.

“Rabbi,” he said, turning back around, ““I’m a developer, and I just finished building a new Montessori preschool about five minutes

It has big classrooms, beautiful playgrounds, and brand- new toys. Only a few rooms are being used now, and many classrooms are still empty. Do you have any use for them?”

Shneur Susskind, 7 Cheder Lubavitch Chicago, Vernon Hills, IL.
