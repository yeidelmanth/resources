Needless to say, Tatty went to Eretz Yisrael. After the levaya, Rachamim’s brother told Tatty how much Rachamim had wished for a Chabad center to open in Jamaica.

“Rabbi Raskin, please open one!” he said. “We’ll help you get started, and we’ll give you Rachamim's house until you can find your own. This will be a big z’chus for our dear brother’s neshama.”

Tatty could hardly believe it. Right after visiting the Ohel, the Rebbe had shown them that Jamaica was their makom hashlichus!

Shortly afterward, Tatty and Mommy indeed moved to Jamaica. They hosted many meals and events in Rachamim’s house until they moved to a different home.

A few years ago, on Rachamim’s seventh yartzeit, Michael unexpectedly called Tatty. As they spoke, Tatty told him about the building plans for our new Chabad House, which would include the first mikvah in Jamaica.

Michael was delighted. “Rabbi,” he said, “Please dedicate the mikvah and Chabad House in honor of Rachamim. I’ll send you a large donation to cover the costs.”

Tatty was amazed. Our family’s entire shlichus had started off because of the Rebbe’s brachos, and now, so many years later, the brachos continue to flow!

When Tatty and Mommy weren’t sure where to go, the Rebbe’s brachos guided them to exactly the right place.

Six months after their chasunah, Tatty and Mommy were deciding where to go on shlichus. One of the places that came up was the island of Jamaica.

They decided to fly there to see if it was a good fit. As they planned their trip, they heard about a Yid named Rachamim who lived on the island and kept a kosher home.

But when they called to ask if they could meet, someone else answered the phone. “Rachamim had a ministroke,” the man said. “He’s in the hospital.”

Tatty and Mommy were very sad to hear. As soon as they landed in Jamaica, they went to visit him in the hospital.

Rachamim’s niece, Debbie, welcomed them into the hospital room. Tatty spoke words of encouragement to Rachamim and helped him put on tefillin. Even though Rachamim was too weak to talk, his eyes shone with happiness that he was able to fulfill this special mitzvah.

After spending a week in Jamaica, Tatty and Mommy returned to New York. They continued davening for Rachamim and stayed in touch with his family, offering support and sending them kosher food.

A few weeks later, a different shlichus opportunity came up.

Tatty and Mommy weren’t sure what to do. “Let’s go to the Ohel,” they decided. “We’ll tell the Rebbe our choices and ask for a clear sign telling us where we should go.”

After writing his letter at the Ohel, Tatty’s phone buzzed. It was an email from Debbie’s husband, Michael. Rachamim passed away, it said. His funeral will be in Eretz Yisrael. It would mean a lot to us if you could be there. We will pay for your plane ticket if you are willing to come.

Saddened, Tatty decided to write to the Rebbe again, asking if he should go to the levaya.

Just as Tatty was about to write, he heard the video of the Rebbe playing nearby. He couldn’t believe it—the Rebbe was speaking to someone who would be traveling to Eretz Yisrael! The Rebbe told him to make sure to visit the mekomos hakdoshim (holy places), and bentched him to have a good trip.
