One mitzvah led to the next, and soon, the family became frum. She and her two sisters married frum men, and her parents became big ba’alei tzedakah.

From time to time, the student wished that she could thank Zeidy for his impact on her life, but she didn’t know how to reach him.

Fifteen years passed. Zeidy continued his shlichus at the university, but at a certain point, he faced many challenges.

Rebbe, he cried out, please send me a sign that I should continue my shlichus.

At that very moment, the student, now a middle-aged woman, was in Denmark on a business trip. When she visited the local Chabad House and saw the Rebbe picture on the wall, she was reminded of Zeidy, the shliach who had inspired her to keep Shabbos and change her life forever.

She turned to the local shliach and showed him a picture from the Birthright trip. “Do you know who this rabbi is?”

The shliach asked around and found out that it was our zeidy. The woman eagerly called him up and told him about the ripple effect that her hachlata had made on herself and her entire family.

Zeidy felt tears coming to his eyes as he listened to the woman’s story. This is the sign I asked for, he realized. The Rebbe is showing me that my shlichus is making a difference.

Zeidy suddenly had the strength he needed to overcome his challenges and continue with his shlichus. He knew that the Rebbe was with him!

She wanted to thank Zeidy for fifteen years. When she finally found his number, it was the perfect time.

One year, our zeidy took a group of college students on a Birthright trip to Eretz Yisroel. Their incredible ten-day trip was packed with all sorts of experiences, from davening at the Kosel and exploring the rich history of our nation to jeeping in the desert, swimming in the Yam Hamelach, and more.

It was very special for the students to be surrounded by so many Yidden and connect with their Jewish roots. Many of them kept Shabbos for the first time in their lives, and they all learned more about what it means to be a Yid.

During the trip, Zeidy turned to the students and announced, “If you’ve never had a Bar or Bas Mitzvah, now is your chance! We’ll celebrate at the Kosel!”

Seeing their confusion, Zeidy explained, “A Bar or Bas Mitzvah marks the beginning of our obligation

to learn Torah and do mitzvos. How about choosing a new mitzvah to take on? That can be your way of celebrating this special milestone!”

The students loved the idea. “I’ll kiss the mezuzah in our hotel,” one student said.

“And I’ll start keeping Shabbos!” announced one of the girls.

Everyone stared at her in shock. Starting to keep Shabbos is a big deal, because there are so many detailed halachos involved! Was she ready to do it?

But the student was determined. “Keeping Shabbos here was so wonderful. I’m ready to do it each week,” she insisted.

When she returned home, she learned many halachos and began to keep Shabbos. At first, her parents were nervous that she was introducing so many strange rules to their house. But then, seeing the beauty of the mitzvah, her whole family started keeping Shabbos with her!
