On the first day of Rosh Hashana, which is also called יֹום ַהִַּזִָּכָרֹון—the day of remembering, we read in the Torah how Hashem remembered Sara Imeinu’s barrenness and blessed her with a son, Yitzchak. Here is the story of the haftorah:

Elkana and his wife Chana were childless for many years. Hoping that it would help her be blessed with a child, Chana arranged for her husband to marry a second wife, Penina. However, Penina went on to have ten children, and Chana had none.

Day after day, Chana’s pain grew deeper, her heart aching to have a child of her own. Penina’s constant talk about her own children made it

impossible for Chana to forget her painful situation.

Before each of the Shalosh Regalim, Chana, Penina, and Elkana would travel to the mishkan in Shiloh to spend Yom Tov in the presence of the Shechina. They would bring along their entire family and take a different route each year, gathering Yidden along the way to join them. In this way, Elkana brought many Yidden closer to Torah and mitzvos.

On one of these trips, Chana felt especially sad. As Chana’s pain overcame her, she entered the Mishkan and, facing the Kodesh Hakadoshim, began to weep. Chana poured out her heart, promising that if Hashem would hear her pain and

give her a child, she would dedicate his entire life to Hashem.

As she quietly mouthed her tefilla, Eli the Kohen Gadol entered. The sight of Chana silently swaying back and forth and moving her lips looked strange to him, and Eli thought that she must be drunk. When he rebuked her, Chana answered, “No my master, I am a broken woman. I have drunk no wine, but it is my soul that I poured before Hashem.” Eli, understanding Chana’s greatness, told her that her tefillos would be accepted. Chana left with a happy heart.

A short while later, Chana and Elkana were blessed with a son, Shmuel. For two years, she waited at home, nursing and nurturing her miracle child. When Shmuel was weaned at two years old, Chana once again travelled to Shiloh with the rest of the family. She brought up a korban to thank Hashem, and told Eli, “I am the woman who beseeched Hashem

right here for a son. For this child I davened, and now I’m giving him to you to raise him in Shiloh, the place where Hashem’s Shechina rests.”

Despite having waited so many years to be blessed with a child, Chana willingly sacrificed her own need to bring up her son so that he may be raised in Hashem’s House and serve his nation. She was so overcome with joy that Shmuel would be raised in such a holy way, Chana the prophetess sang shira to Hashem.

In her shira, Chana tells many nevuos. In fact, according to the Targum, her entire song hints to important events in Jewish history all the way to the final geula. She predicts the future of her son, Shmuel Hanavi, who had the great zechus of anointing Dovid Hamelech, the first of all the kings of Yehudah—the last of whom, of course, is Moshiach.
