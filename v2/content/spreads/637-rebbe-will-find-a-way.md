And then... All of a sudden, something changed. “Mommy,” I whispered. “I don’t feel any pain anymore.”

Mommy’s eyes widened in shock. Could it really be true?

Even though my leg didn’t hurt anymore, we continued to the hospital to make sure everything was okay.

At the hospital, the doctor looked at my leg and said, “Since you’re feeling better, let’s wait a little before we take an X-ray.”

An hour later, a new doctor came. He was friendly and very funny. “Can you try to stand up?” he asked.

I was nervous. What if it hurt? But slowly, I stood up—and it didn't hurt at all!

“Let’s see who can jump higher,” the doctor smiled.

Later that night, when Tatty and my brother came home from the Ohel, Mommy asked, “What time did you go into the Ohel to daven for Rikki?”

They told Mommy when it was. And guess what? It was exactly the same time that I had started to feel better!

Just one moment before, I couldn’t move my leg even a little bit without crying. But as soon as they davened at the Ohel and the Rebbe gave me a bracha, the pain was completely gone!

This miracle showed me how deeply the Rebbe is connected to every Chossid, even a kid like me. When we children turn to the Rebbe, he listens to our tefillos and finds a way to help!

I was lying on the stretcher, trying not to cry. But then, in an instant, the pain was gone!

It was the Shabbos before Gimmel Tammuz when I was five years old, and our whole family had come to New York. Tatty and my older brother went to spend Shabbos at the Ohel. Mommy and the rest of us were staying at our cousins’ house in Queens.

On Shabbos afternoon, all of my cousins were playing outside. It was so much fun running around with everyone! I climbed up the ladder to go down the slide. But one of the bars was broken, and I slipped and fell to the ground with a loud thud.

A sharp pain shot through my leg. “Ow! Ow!” I screamed. Tears filled my eyes as I began to cry.

I tried standing, but I couldn’t. My brother picked me up and brought me to the couch inside. Mommy quickly put an ice pack on my

ankle. She saw right away that my foot looked strange—it wasn’t straight, the way it should be.

Right after Shabbos, Mommy called Hatzalah. A Hatzalah man came and looked at my leg. “That looks like a broken femur bone,” he said.

Another Hatzalah man came and said the same thing: “Looks like your femur is broken.”

More Hatzalah men arrived. Every one of them agreed that it looked like a broken femur.

They tried picking me up to put me on the stretcher, but my leg hurt too much. Every tiny movement made me cry. So, they gently slid wooden boards under me, and slowly, slowly moved me onto the stretcher. Then, they lifted me into the ambulance and began driving to the hospital.

Mommy called Tatty, who was still by the Ohel. “We’re on our way to the hospital with Rikki,” she said. “It looks like she broke her leg. Please go into the Ohel and ask the Rebbe for a bracha!”

The ambulance continued zooming through the streets. I was lying on the stretcher, trying not to cry. My leg hurt so much! Submitted by Rikki Lifshitz, 9
