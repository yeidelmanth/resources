<p class="lead">When our baby cousin became very sick, everyone davened for a miracle.</p>

“Mazel tov!” Mommy said happily. “You have a new baby cousin. A girl!”

We were all so excited. We couldn’t wait to meet her!

Mommy told us that our new cousin, Sarah Leah, was born five weeks early. Baruch Hashem, she was healthy and able to go home with her family.

But three weeks later, Sarah Leah’s temperature suddenly dropped very low. She looked pale and weak. Something was wrong.

Our aunt and uncle rushed her to the hospital. “Your baby has pneumonia and the flu,” the doctors said.

Everyone was very worried. A newborn baby is very fragile, and getting so sick is extremely dangerous.

For two long weeks, our tiny cousin lay in a special hospital bed. A machine called a ventilator helped her breathe. She was very ill, and everyone davened for a miracle.

On Yud Kislev, the Mitteler Rebbe's Chag HaGeulah, our uncle went to a farbrengen. During the farbrengen, someone asked how baby Sarah Leah was doing. With tears in his eyes, our uncle told him that she was still very sick and fighting for her life.

“Did you write to the Rebbe?” the man asked.

“Of course,” our uncle said.

“Did you go to the Ohel?”

Our uncle paused. “No,” he answered. “It’s been so hectic with our baby in the hospital.”

“You must go to the Ohel in person and daven for a bracha!” the man urged.

It was already very late at night, and our uncle was exhausted. But he decided to go anyway. At midnight, when the farbrengen ended, he drove to the Ohel.

Rebbe, he wrote, *please bentch our baby to be healthy.* He also added: *Please give us a bracha that we should dance at her wedding when she grows up.*

Inside the Ohel, he davened and read his pan. Then, he went to watch the video playing on the screen.

<figure class="handimg inline"><img src="https://mashpia.com/resources/assets/img/4c879b93c8f9fea9.png" alt="handwritten note"></figure>

When he entered the video room, he froze in shock. The Rebbe was beginning a new sicha with the words

before a kallah at her wedding!

At that moment, our uncle knew that he had the Rebbe’s brachos. He left the Ohel feeling calm, sure that everything would be okay.

With tremendous nissim, baby Sarah Leah began to feel better. She came home nine days later, on Yud-Tes Kislev, the Alter Rebbe's Chag HaGeulah!

But the following week, she needed to go back to the hospital again. The doctors were concerned, but Baruch Hashem, after just a few days, Sarah Leah was ready to come home once more.

Can you guess the date this time? It was the third night of Chanukah, which is the Alter Rebbe’s second Chag HaGeulah!

It was clear how everything fit together.

The farbrengen on Yud Kislev led our uncle to go daven at the Ohel. From there, the

Rebbe’s brachos helped our cousin heal, and she came home on two special days connected to the

Alter Rebbe—from one Chag HaGeulah to the next!

Seeing such a clear answer from the Rebbe helped our aunt and uncle stay strong. They knew that the Rebbe was with their baby, helping her have a complete refuah.
