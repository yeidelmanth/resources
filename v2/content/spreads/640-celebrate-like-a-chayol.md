A kindergarten class once sent the Rebbe a collection of their pictures and drawings. The Rebbe writes back to them:

Rebbe had for all Yiddishe sons and daughters … especially the young ones, as he cared for their chinuch and well- being wherever they may be. In every child, he saw a sapling that will grow into a beautiful tree with good, delicious fruit.

But this \[growth\] is dependent on the will of every boy and girl, as \[we learn from\] the mashal of a tree.

When we plant a seed or sapling, we must guard it from weeds and all sorts of damaging things, and we must supply it with water etc., until it grows and becomes a tree which gives good fruits.

The same is true with every boy and girl, about whom Hashem says through His navi that they are “נֵצֶר מַטָעַי מַעֲׂשֵה יָדַי לְהִתְּפָאֵר—the branch of My planting and the work of My hands, of which I am so proud.”

Hashem, the Creator, has planted within you a holy neshama, a “חֵלֶק אֱלֹוּקַ מִמַעַל מַמָש—a piece of Hashem above, literally.” You must protect this G-dly “seed” from bad weeds and other damaging things, which means inappropriate friends, and you must supply it with “מַיִם חַיִים— healthy, living water”, which means Toras Chayim (“וְאֵין מַיִם אֶלָא ּתֹורָה—and water \[here\] is only referring to Torah”) and its mitzvos (which are “חַיִים הֵם לְעֹושֵׂיהֶם—life for those who perform them”).

And then, Hashem will “יִּתֵן וְיַחְזֹור וְיִּתֵן—give and give again” His bracha that you will succeed in being “trees” that give good, praiseworthy fruit, to the joy of your parents and teachers and to the pride and beauty of our nation, Am B’nei Yisroel…

Our minhag is to celebrate this holy day by eating fruits. Do you know which fruits we eat? There is actually more than one answer!

First of all, it’s important to eat the special fruits of Eretz Yisroel that are part of the Shiv’as Haminim: grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives, and dates.

Second, we eat “חַרּובִים—carobs”. The Gemara tells us that the only food Rabi Chanina ben Dosa ate was just one kav of carobs each week. (That’s like eating only seven apples all week long!) So many miracles happened to this great Tanna that he was known as a "מְלּומָד ּבְנִיסִים—someone who is used to miracles." This shows us that carobs have a special connection to nisim.

The third type of fruit that we eat on Chamisha-Asar B’Shvat is a new fruit; one we haven’t eaten yet this season. This way, we can say the bracha of “ׁשֶהֶחֱיָנו”. There are so many fruits to choose from! What’s the strangest fruit you’ve ever eaten?

Note: Be sure to eat fruits with the bracha “הָעֵץ”, rather than ones which are actually “הָאֲדָמָה”!

There are certain parts of davening known as “tachanun”. These are serious sections asking Hashem to forgive our aveiros. We don’t say tachanun on Shabbos, Yom Tov, and other extra-happy times (like Chamisha-Asar B’Shvat), because the ko’ach of these days turn our aveiros into z’chuyos— through being b’simcha! From mincha on Yud- Daled Shvat until shki’a of Chamisha-Asar B’Shvat, tachanun is not said.

You might be wondering, “What does this have to do with me, a young chayol?” Even though you might not yet say tachanun in davening, there are still parts of your tefilla that are connected to tachanun. Ask your parents or teachers to show you.
