On Simchas Torah morning, malachim have extra time. You see, on this day, minyanim start later, because Yidden normally sleep in. They’re exhausted from all the dancing. But malachim wake up early just like any other day, ready to praise Hashem. The problem is, they can only do so when there are Yidden awake and praising Hashem. And on Simchas Torah morning, the Yidden are all sleeping! So, the malachim must wait. One Simchas Torah, they decided to spend this time cleaning up Gan Eiden. To their utter surprise, the malachim found strange objects there: Shoes! Worn-out and tattered shoes. Piles and piles of them. This left the malachim very confused. In Gan Eiden, there’s often tzitzis, maybe tefillin—but shoes?! So off they went to ask Malach Michoel what this was about. He happily replied, “Ah! This is my merchandise!” He explained to the puzzled malachim that these shoes were from the previous night, worn by Yidden who danced and danced. They danced so much that their shoes got completely worn out. And these holy, tattered shoes flew straight up to Gan Eiden. “These,” he pointed to one pile, “are from the village of Kaminka. “Those,” he said, pointing to another, “are from Mezritch. All are among the most cherished items in Shamayim.” The Baal Shem Tov concluded, “And with that, Malach Michoel proudly crafts a spectacular crown for Hashem—from the tattered shoes worn out from Simchas Torah dancing!”

Torah. But do you know what else it means? It also means “on credit”. Credit is when you buy something now and pay for it later. During Hakafos, Hashem lets us “buy” brachos for the whole year and “pay Him back” later, so they are a special time to ask Him for what we need.

How old does a boy have to be to get an aliyah to the Torah? You guessed it: Thirteen! But on Simchas Torah, all boys—even tiny babies!—get an aliyah, too! This aliya, called “ָּל הַּנְעָרִים”, is given to one adult along with all the boys.

We make sure to kiss the gartel, say the brachos loudly and clearly, and listen closely to the kriah. Many shuls spread out a tallis over all of the boys, and say “הַמַלְאְָך הַגֹאֵל” afterwards. Although it’s not Minhag Chabad to do these two things, the Rebbe said that they are indeed good inyanim. At Lubavitcher shuls, they are not done b’gashmiyus, but they are accomplished in a ruchniyus way.

There is something special that we do on both Shmini Atzeres and Simchas Torah. In fact, it’s something that we spend hours doing! Can you guess what it is? That’s right—dancing!

On this Yom Tov, all Yidden dance together. Whether we are young or old and whether we know lots of Torah or just a little, we dance as one. This is because the Torah is for all of us, no matter who we are.

We also show this by dancing with Sifrei Torah that are all wrapped up in their mantel. On this special Yom Tov, it’s not about who can open up the Torah and learn it better. It’s about the fact that Torah is our life. And for that, we are all equal.

We dance and dance with the Torah, bursting with joy that we are connected to it. And while dancing, keep in mind: Yes, we are happy with the Torah, but is the Torah happy with us? Is the Torah satisfied with our avodah? Think of a hachlata that you can take in limmud Torah this year.

When we dance, the Torah is so happy that it wants to dance, too! But it doesn’t have feet to dance with. Instead, we get to be the Torah’s feet! So, we dance with the Torah and we dance for the Torah.

Have you ever thought of what this dancing accomplishes? The Rebbe Rashab said that every moment of dancing on Shmini Atzeres and Simchas Torah brings down bucketfuls of treasures from Hashem, in both gashmiyus and ruchniyus.

Additonally, dancing on Simchas Torah is so powerful that it causes the Satan to hide in shame. Whenever he tries to say something not good about the Yidden, the malachim exclaim, “Look at them! They bring the whole family to shul just to dance with the Torah!” When the Satan hears this, he has nothing else to say. Case closed.

On these special days, let’s chap arayn and rejoice with the Torah with all our might! After all, there’s nothing that can get in our way now!

There's a special tefillah called “תְּפִלַת גֶשֶׁם” that we add during Chazaras Hashatz of musaf on Shmini Atzeres. It’s said after we have switched to “מֵשִׁיב הָרּוחַ ּומֹורִיד הַגֶּשֶׁם” in the quiet musaf, praising Hashem for sending rain at the right time.

Some Yidden don’t say anything else in this spot of Shemoneh Esrei throughout the summer, so if they miss “mashiv haru’ach”, Shemoneh Esrei is repeated. But since in our nusach we say “מֹורִיד הַטָל” during the summer, if we forget to say “mashiv haru’ach”, we don’t repeat Shemoneh Esrei again.

In many places around the world, there’s no need to ask for rain. In Mitzrayim, for instance, the Nilus overflows and waters the fields without the farmers doing anything. In Eretz Yisroel, however, the rain doesn’t just come automatically. We must daven for it.

This might seem like a bother, but it’s actually a good thing!

Davening for rain reminds us that Hashem is the One in charge, and everything in our lives comes from Him. But some things are obviously from Hashem, and other things are not. Hashem makes the Nilus overflow, but He doesn’t do it in a direct way. He makes it happen through the Mitzrim’s Sar (malach) in Shamayim. Therefore, the Mitzri farmers get everything they need through their Sar, without needing to daven to Hashem.

Imagine your parents hired a babysitter for a few days. They might tell the babysitter to take you to the park or pizza shop, or to do other fun things that you don’t normally do. You have a blast with the babysitter, but you’re much happier when your parents return home. Why is that? Wouldn’t you rather have the fun babysitter take care of you all the time? The answer is, of course, because no matter how nice the babysitter is, they can’t compare to the deep love and connection you have with your parents.

The same is with Hashem. He is our Father Who loves us deeply and always wants our connection.

The Mitzrim (and all other nations), l’havdil, are taken care of by Hashem’s “babysitters”, so it looks like they have it better. We Yidden must daven for what we need and want—but it’s because we’re with our Father.

And now, as we prepare to bring Tishrei’s ko’ach into everyday life, we ask Hashem to “rain down” our gashmiyus needs.

Surely, He’ll send it happily and plentifully, as a loving Father does.
