As soldier’s in Hashem’s army we are Hashem- centered. We focus on doing what Hashem wants. One of the biggest weapons that the Yetzer Hara uses against us is self-centered behavior. “Self- centered” means to focus on what’s best for myself. (This isn’t the same thing as “self care”, which means doing what we need to stay healthy. Self care is an important mitzvah.)

<span class="mc" style="color:#d01d30">Recon Mission</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">Getting to know the enemy’s weapons and tactics before planning</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">In a war, the first step toward victory is to expose the enemy’s strategy. This way, we avoid falling into his trap. In Tzivos Hashem we face the trickiest enemy: the Yetzer Hara.</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#ba1126">Here’s his strategy: First, the Yetzer Hara plants negative thoughts in our heads. Then, those thoughts grow into worse feelings that can lead to even more dangerous actions.</span>

The Yetzer Hara tries to trick us into being afraid that we won’t be able to take care of ourselves if we focus on what Hashem wants us to do.

Look at the following scene and identify how the Yetzer Hara could influence you to think, speak, and act in a self-centered way.

We have just uncovered the Yetzer Hara’s tricks. He tells us to be self-centered and focus only on ourselves. The Yetzer Hara makes us think and feel that by doing what Hashem wants, our needs will be neglected, and we won’t be able to survive. Discuss a time when each squad member fell into the enemy’s trap of self-centered behavior because they felt overwhelmed, angry, or insecure. Identify the thoughts that brought those feelings.
