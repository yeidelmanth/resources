chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

Yossi and Chaim, you guys are sooo different from each other. Chaim, you love to sit and learn deep things, while you, Yossi, love adventures and doing mivtzoim!

That’s true. But I’m pretty connected to our guest this week. It’s Yitzchak Avinu, who shares part of my name: Yosef Yitzchak!

I love Yitzchak Avinu. You might have his name, but I really relate to his personality.

Yeah, I see how you say that, Chaim! It’s a bit funny that Yossi shares a name with Yitzchak Avinu. Of all the Avos, Yitzchak’s life was definitely the least adventurous.

Hello, chayolim! I’m so happy to hear from you. Baruch Hashem, because of the famine here in Eretz Yisroel, I have extra time to talk.

Yossi, famine isn’t awesome. It means there’s no rain or food.

True. Without rain, there’s no food, but there’s also no farm work to do.

Hold on! Yitzchak Avinu, why didn’t you go down to Mitzrayim just like your father, Avraham Avinu, did when there was a famine?

Ah, my son. Hashem commanded me to stay in Eretz Yisroel. Here, Hashem will be with me and watch over my family. Everything will be just fine.

Oh, right! It’s because you’re too pure to leave Eretz Yisroel.

We Yidden belong here. This is our home. We do not belong in golusdik places like Mitzrayim. Golus is a sad place for a Yid, and we should never get comfortable there.

But I don’t get it. Your father went to Mitzrayim, so why can’t you?

My father and I have two very different avodos—ways of serving Hashem. My father focused on reaching out to others. He travelled around to bring people closer to Hashem. My avodah is more personal. Most of the time, I stay in my place and focus on Torah learning and deepening my avodah. I keep myself completely for Hashem.

So, Avraham Avinu could travel to Mitzrayim because his avodah was about going out. But you, Yitzchak Avinu, can’t leave, because your avodah is to stay in a heilige place?

Hmm. Reminds me of the two of you, Yossi and Chaim. As Mimi was saying before, one likes to go out and do mivtzoim, while the other would rather sit and learn!

Hey, “מַעֲׁשֶה אָבֹות סִימָן לְּבָנִים— What our Avos did is a lesson for us.” I’m more like Yitzchak Avinu, and Yossi is like Avraham.

Yes, my child, but everyone should learn from both of us.

You mean, be a Avraham and a Yitzchak all at once? That sounds confusing.

Every Yid should do both types of avodah. Be like Avraham Avinu by reaching out to others. Tell another Yid about the greatness of Hashem, and bring one more person to Torah and mitzvos.
