Same Spot Operation Chanukah

The narrow building stood quietly in the Roman ghetto. Its walls were hundreds of years old, filled with a long and painful history.

But now, something new was happening inside. It was our family’s Chabad House!

Slowly, the building began to fill with life, as people came to daven, learn, and take part in programs.

Local Yidden and tourists passed through it every day, and the ghetto was alive once more.

Then Chanukah came, and an elderly man walked in. He was about ninety years old. Tatty greeted him warmly and asked, “Did you ever have a bar mitzvah?”

“No,” the man shook his head.

A short while later, another elderly man came in. His name was Gabriel, and he was eighty- six. When Tatty asked him the same question, Gabriel gave the same answer: “No.”

Tatty didn’t wait. Together with the bochurim who had come to help for Chanukah, he quickly made a minyan and took out a Sefer Torah.

For the first time in their lives, the two elderly men put on tefillin. Then, they got aliyos to the Torah and celebrated their bar mitzvahs right there in the Chabad House. Everyone sang and danced with excitement and joy.

A few days later, on the last night of Chanukah, Tatty organized a small menorah lighting outside the Chabad House. He thought that only a few people would come, but when he stepped outside, he saw that an entire crowd had gathered!

Seeing that Gabriel was there, too, Tatty announced,

“Standing here with us is a Yid who just celebrated his bar mitzvah for the first time. He is a Holocaust survivor, no less. We want to give him the honor of lighting the menorah.”
