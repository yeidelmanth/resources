Mommy really wanted the girls to have four full weeks to learn about the new middah. But there was still no one to run it.

That week, our family went to New York for Yud Shvat. At the Ohel, Mommy wrote a pan, but didn’t mention this dilemma.

After leaving the Ohel, we went to watch a video of the Rebbe. As Mommy listened, her ears perked up. The Rebbe was talking about leading a group!

Mommy was stunned. It felt like the Rebbe was speaking directly to her!

Baruch Hashem, a bunch of girls signed up, and the meetings were a great success!

This story reminds us that the Rebbe is always there to help his chassidim. At first, Mommy didn’t think that she could take on this responsibility. She hadn’t even written about the squad in her pan. But the Rebbe knew what was on Mommy’s mind, and answered her. His words gave her the ko’ach she needed to take the lead!

A Vehalachta Bidrachav Squad for girls was finally created!

Our family loves reading the feature in Hachayol Magazine called “Vehalachta Bidrachav Squad Drills”. It teaches lessons from our Avos and Imahos, and shows how to use them in our own lives.

The name “Squad Drills” tells an important part of this feature: that the best way to learn it is in a squad—a group. Fighting the Yetzer Hara together is waaay easier than each of us working on our own!

Of course, chayolim without a nearby squad can still learn “Squad Drills” by themsleves, but it’s much better to join up with friends and fight the battles as a group.

At the beginning of last year, the boys’ school in our city had a squad that met each week to learn and grow together. But the girls’ school didn’t have one.

“I wish we could have a Vehalachta Bidrachav Squad, too!” our sister kept begging.

Seeing how much she wanted one, Mommy asked our base commander if there could be a squad for girls. He explained that the boys school had a squad because they had shluchim to run it, but the girls school didn’t have anyone available.

“If you find someone to do it, we can definitely open one for the girls,” he said.

So Mommy asked the principal. She loved the idea, but said the same thing: “If you find someone to run the squad, we’ll be happy to help make it happen.”

It seemed like everyone wanted it—the base commander, the principal, and the girls. The only thing missing was someone to take charge.

Mommy didn’t feel like she was the right person for the job, and besides, it would be hard for our family. The meetings would be right after school, which meant Mommy would have to stay until after 5 p.m. That would make the rest of the day’s schedule much busier and more difficult. So Mommy started asking around.

But she couldn’t find anyone else to lead the squad! The high school girls had class during the meeting time, and the mothers she spoke to were too busy.
