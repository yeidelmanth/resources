Hey! One of the options for this week is the leader of a big, mean highway gang! What can we learn from someone like that?!

I don’t know! Those guys used to rob and kill people. He probably does a lot of aveiros.

Oy vey. I hope this isn’t going to be another scary one…

Don’t worry, Basya. I have a hunch about this. I say let’s go for it!

Okay, I’m kinda curious anyway, so I’ll trust you, Chaim, that he’s a safe person…

Baruch Hashem! I’m in the middle of reviewing a mishna. I must go over it at least forty times before the next shiur from my teacher, Rabi Yochanan.

That’s because it’s not. My hunch was correct! You’re Reish Lakish, right?

That’s what I’m called, yes. How can I help you children? Is there a part of Torah that you’re unclear about? The whole world exists because of the learning of you Yiddishe kids!

Whoa. I am so confused right now. Yossi, didn’t you say that you called a highway gang leader? I’m pretty sure people like that don’t learn with Rabi Yochanan. Reish Lakish sounds like a tzaddik!

Oh—ah, well, it seems your brother wasn’t completely wrong. A few years ago, I was a gang leader.

Yes. See, I was always big and strong. Back then, I used all my strength for aveiros. Big, bad aveiros. Everyone was afraid of me.

But what happened? How did you end up becoming a tzaddik?

Rabi Yochanan inspired me. He said, “You are so strong! If you would put that strength into Torah, you could be a great talmid chacham!”

Well, it took a bit more convincing from Rabi Yochanan, but in the end, yes, I decided to put all my strength into learning. Baruch Hashem, I did teshuva.

But … what about all of those aveiros from the past? How did you just move on?

What a wonderful question! Hashem tells us that when we turn ourselves around by regretting our past, and doing good instead, then our aveiros actually become z’chuyos!

And that’s exactly what we learn in this week’s parsha! It says to admit our aveiros and do teshuva— ”.“וְהִתְוַדּו אֶת חַטָאתָם אֲשֶׁר עָׂשּו

Well said! Sometimes, our Yetzer Hara puts shtus in our head, which can chas v’shalom stop us from doing a mitzvah, or even make us do an aveira. An aveira is also called a “אְחֵט”, which means “something is missing.”

But when we do teshuva, we return everything that was missed, and Hashem forgives us right away?
