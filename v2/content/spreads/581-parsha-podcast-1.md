chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

So, who are we bringing on this week? Yossi and Chaim, you said you have someone?

Yossi and I met this really great guy on mivtzoyim today and I asked if he would come on our podcast. I know I should’ve checked with all of you first, but … I think he might have a powerful lesson for us this week and I didn’t want to miss the opportunity.

Hi Ben! It’s Chaim and Yossi and we’re live at the Parsha Podcast. Remember us?

Oh yeah! Of course I remember you! How are you doing? Did you try any of those exercises I showed you?

Yup, I did! One day I’m going to be strong enough to climb the tallest mountain in the world!

What mountain? Since when are we talking about mountains?

Hi Ben, this is Mimi. Can you tell us a bit about what you do? Yossi and Chaim are very excited to talk to you, but they didn’t really explain why.

He climbed so many mountains! Even some really scary and hard ones.

Haha, don’t worry about it buddy. I’ll explain some more. I love climbing mountains so much that I take every opportunity to do so. Sometimes I go by myself, and sometimes I bring friends along with me. I’ve been climbing ever since I was a little kid. My father used to take me.

But, what are you going to do when you finish climbing all the mountains?

There are like a gazillion mountains, Basya! I don’t think he’s ever going to run out of mountains to climb.

Great question Basya, but your brother is right. Each time I successfully climb a mountain, I take time to celebrate—and then, I call a few friends, find an even taller mountain, and we’re on our way!

See! Isn’t that such a cool lesson? And it matches this week's parsha!

How is that a lesson? I don’t want to climb mountains. There are too many bugs, and it sounds scary.

No, no. Not a lesson in climbing, a lesson in our avodah. About always doing more and going higher.

Oh, I get it. Just like Ben is always looking to climb bigger and taller mountains, we should always look to grow more and more—higher and higher in our Yiddishkeit! Like the name of our parsha, “Vayeilech.”

Exactly, Mimi. Vayeilech isn’t only the name of our parsha; it’s also a lesson for us: To always go mechayil el chayil in our Yiddishkeit.

I think you guys got it. But there’s one more part.

Friends! We have to bring other people along and help them grow, too!

Yeah, like we do with our podcast. We share these messages so other kids can also learn from them.
