Minutes after writing to the Rebbe, they had gotten the sign that they asked for.

All of their worries melted away. Tatty went into surgery feeling much calmer, knowing that he had the Rebbe’s brachos. Baruch Hashem, the surgery was successful and Tatty returned home healthy and well.

Later, when Mommy took a closer look at the dollar, she was shocked to see that this was her dollar from the Rebbe!

Somehow, when she had packed up her dollars before moving to Australia, this one had gotten misplaced. It ended up together with Zeidy’s Rebbe dollars, and was then passed on to our brother Shmuli—who gave it to Tatty at just the moment he needed it.

Everyone was amazed at the incredible chain of hashgacha pratis:

Shmuli went along to the hospital because Mommy wasn’t home, bringing the special Rebbe dollar with him.

Years after Gimmel Tammuz, we felt the Rebbe’s presence in our lives so clearly, helping us get through this challenge with strength and focus.

Mommy stared at the dollar in surprise. Where had it been all these years?

It was Erev Yud-Tes Kislev. Mommy was on an airplane returning home from a simcha, while Tatty was in his office preparing to go to the grand community farbrengen. Suddenly, he doubled over in pain. “My stomach!” he cried.

Hatzalah was quickly called, and they rushed Tatty to the hospital. Because Mommy wasn’t home, our older brother Shmuli went along to make sure that Tatty would be okay.

But when they arrived at the hospital, all of the doctors were too busy to check Tatty. When Mommy came in hours later, she was shocked to find that he was still waiting to be seen. She tried convincing the nurses to help him sooner, but there was nothing they could do.

Finally, a doctor came in to check Tatty. “There’s a large hernia in your stomach,” he said seriously. “You need to have an emergency operation!”

By then it was nighttime, and Yud-Tes Kislev had already begun. Knowing the power of a chassidishe

farbrengen, especially on such a special Yom Tov, Mommy quickly texted Tatty’s close friend to let him know what was going on.

“Please daven for my husband,” she wrote, “and make a l'chaim at the farbrengen that the operation should be successful.”

Next, she wrote a letter to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha and a clear sign that everything should be okay.

Mommy then turned to our brother Shmuli, hoping for some chizuk. “Do you have a Rebbe dollar with you by any chance?”

“Of course,” he replied. “Zeidy gave me a Rebbe dollar when he visited us, and I always carry it with me.”

“Can you give it to Tatty before he goes into surgery?”
