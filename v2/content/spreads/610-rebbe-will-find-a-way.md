She raised her eyebrows. “A rabbi? But I’m not Jewish!”

“The Rebbe helps all kinds of people, including non-Jews,” Tatty replied with a smile. “To receive his blessings, it’s proper to add more good deeds into your life. When you write to the Rebbe, maybe you can commit to following the Sheva Mitzvos B’nei Noach.”

She hesitated for a moment, deep in thought. “I guess I have nothing to lose. I’ll give it a try.”

And so, for the first time in her life, she wrote a letter to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha and hoping for good news.

It was an ordinary day at the office when Tatty noticed that one of his coworkers looked pale and withdrawn. It was clear that something serious was on her mind.

His coworker took a big, shaky breath. “I’m in big trouble!” she cried. “My housekeeper told the police all kinds of lies about me and is taking me to court. None of it is true, but

Her voice filled with despair as she continued, “My lawyer says it’s impossible for me to win the case. What will be?”

Tatty was shocked to hear that his coworker was in such serious trouble. His forehead creased with worry as he tried to think of a way to help.

At last, he had an idea. “I think there is something you can do to win the court case.”

His coworker blinked in surprise. “But I just told you—my lawyer says it’s impossible!”

“Your lawyer is using his logic, which is limited by nature,” Tatty explained. “But there’s a great rabbi who is not limited by nature. He is my Rebbe, and his blessings have helped many people in extremely difficult situations. Maybe you can write to him.”
