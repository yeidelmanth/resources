Early Thursday morning, Tatty flew to New York for the rally. When he landed and saw the gray, cloudy sky, his heart sank. He checked the weather again, but it still said that it would rain all evening. What would be?

Tatty went straight from the airport to the Ohel and wrote a short, urgent letter to the Rebbe: Please give us a bracha that the rain should wait until after the rally.

Then, feeling confident in the Rebbe’s brachos, Tatty headed to Crown Heights.

At 6:15 that evening, hundreds of kids gathered in front of 770. Each child had a

clipboard, pen, and question sheet, ready to listen carefully to the Rebbe’s words. Big screens lit up, showing a Lag Ba’Omer

The Rebbe spoke about Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, and the kids listened closely; answering the questions

and absorbing the Rebbe’s message. Everyone also got ice cream vouchers and chances to win amazing prizes.

To Tatty’s relief, the skies remained clear the entire time.

As soon as the program ended, it began to drizzle. Minutes later, after everyone had gone back home, the skies opened up with a massive rainstorm. The rain had waited until the rally was over!

The open brachos helped hundreds of children experience and understand an entire Lag Ba’Omer rally with the Rebbe. Tatty was so grateful to have the z’chus of helping them strengthen their hiskashrus— and he knew that the Rebbe was proud!

It was supposed to rain all evening. Would the rally be ruined?

Before Gimmel Tammuz, kids our age had a special z’chus: they were able to experience in-person rallies with the Rebbe! They would gather in 770 and listen to the Rebbe’s sichos, getting ko’ach to serve Hashem and chayus to bring Moshiach.

But what about us kids who were born after Gimmel Tammuz?

That’s where Connection Point comes in! Tatty, together with JEM and Tzivos Hashem, created this special program so that kids like us can watch the Rebbe’s rallies and farbrengens in a way we can understand.

A few times each year, schools around the world watch parts of a rally with the Rebbe. Then, each school makes a big event where everyone watches the full rally together!

There are prizes, raffles, and a lot of excitement. Over the last few years, more than 4,000 kids have joined! The kids in Crown Heights are extra lucky, because their grand events happen outside, right in front of 770.

One year, Tatty planned a Connection Point rally for Thursday evening, Lag Ba’Omer, to take place in front of 770.

Since our family had just moved on shlichus to Florida, he planned everything over Zoom with JEM, Tzivos Hashem, and the local schools.

But there was a big problem: It was a week before the event, and he still didn’t know how they would pay for it.

Tatty decided to write a letter to the Rebbe and ask for a bracha to find a sponsor.

Minutes after Tatty faxed the letter to the Ohel, he got a message from Reb Osher Karnowsky: “I’ll sponsor the entire event in memory of my son, Shmuel.”

Tatty was amazed. The Rebbe had clearly given him the bracha!

He continued planning the event, but then there was another challenge: the weather.
