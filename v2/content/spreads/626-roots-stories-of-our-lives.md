With the help of two other men, Shlomo Moshe managed to pin down his son’s arms and legs, and carry him up to his apartment. All the while, Menashe thrashed and screamed. But when they passed the stairs that led down to the cellar, Menashe grew especially agitated.

His face scrunched up and colored red. His lips frothed at the edges. Menashe was clearly trying very hard to say something, but only confused syllables came out of his mouth. “Il … gorsh … get. Onna … gorsh … gel … get … meeeeeee!”

Shlomo Moshe paused. “It’s going to get you? What, what is going to get you?”

“The cellar.” Shlomo Moshe translated his son’s words aloud. Then he fell silent.

He was quiet while they finished carrying Menashe up the stairs. He was quiet while they tied down his arms and legs. He was quiet while they deposited Menashe on the nearest bed, and watched as he resisted for a couple of minutes before falling asleep. Shlomo Moshe muttered just a quick thank you to the men who helped him, and showed them out the door.

Shlomo Moshe then felt around his pocket and took out the crumpled paper inside. Slowly, he unwrapped it, smoothed it, and laid it on the table. At first, he

just stared at it for a couple of minutes. Then, finally, he signed his name on the bottom.
