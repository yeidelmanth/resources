Shavuos is always an exciting time at our Chabad House. The Rebbe wants all Jewish men, women, and children to hear the Aseres Hadibros, so we make a big event for everyone to join. To make it extra exciting, we always serve ice cream!

One year, after everyone had heard the Aseres Hadibros and was enjoying their yummy ice cream, Tatty went over to a man he didn’t recognize.

“Thank you,” the man replied. “I’m Mr. David Eidlitz. I saw your signs around town about this Shavuos event and decided to join. I’m so glad I came. It was very meaningful to hear the Ten Commandments.”

As they spoke, David shared that he hadn’t grown up in a frum house. “I barely know anything about the Torah,” he said sadly. “I never even had a Bar Mitzvah.”

Tatty told him about our Chabad House events and classes, and Mr. Eidlitz began joining. Over time, he became more inspired to live like a Yid.

One morning, Mr. Eidlitz ran into the shul, looking for Tatty.

“I had a dream about the Rebbe!” he exclaimed. “The Rebbe’s face was shining, and it was hard to look at him.

He looked very intently at me and said, ‘If you want to be successful in life, you must fulfill mitzvos nine through thirteen.’

“What can this mean?” Mr. Eidlitz asked. “I thought there were only Ten Commandments, not thirteen!”

Tatty smiled. “Actually there are six hundred and thirteen mitzvos! The Rambam has them numbered in his book, Sefer Hamitzvos. I wonder why the Rebbe told you to keep mitzvos nine through thirteen specifically. Let’s check to see what those mitzvos are.”

Tatty and Mr. Eidlitz opened Sefer Hamitzvos together. “Mitzvah number nine—make a kiddush Hashem,” Tatty read. “Mitzva number ten—read Krias Shema twice a day.”

Mitzvah number eleven was to learn Torah and teach it to one’s children. Mitzvos twelve and thirteen were to put on tefillin shel rosh and shel yad.

But why these specific mitzvos? What was so special about them? Suddenly, Tatty understood.
