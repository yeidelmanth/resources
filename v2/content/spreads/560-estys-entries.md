It’s eleven in the morning, and all I’ve done today is daven and eat breakfast. Now I’m back in my bedroom, but this time I’m sitting on my bed—not on my Mission Carpet. That’s ‘cause I feel like giving up on my big plans to become a five-star general, abolish the Yetzer Hara, and bring Moshiach. I’m just a kid. How is anything I do gonna make a difference anyway? Everyone else around me is doing real and big stuff.

Mommy is on the phone with the bakery, ordering six hundred hamentashen for Purim. It’s more than a month away, I know, but she is planning a huge seudah for our Chabad House with hundreds of people. Mommy likes to be organized and prepared, and she does both really well. Tatty is in his office arranging fifteen different places where the Megillah will be read. Meanwhile, Mushkie is in her room making some kind of huge project with her friend involving a trifold poster and a four-foot model of the Bais Hamikdash.

As for me? I’m not even Bas Mitzvah yet. I can’t even check eggs for blood spots without supervision, let alone cross the street at Route 202 or drive people to mivtzoyim.

So I figured, I’ll leave the big stuff to the big people. For now, I’ll have another bag of Bamba and read comics for the rest of the day.

As I sat at the table doing my homework today, I thought about how Purim Katan is coming up this Friday. “I feel bad for Purim Katan,” I said to Mushkie.

“Well you know what “katan” means, right? Purim Katan is the small Purim. I bet Purim Katan wishes it could be big like the regular Purim.”

“Well, when you’re big, you get to do all the big stuff and make a big difference. Little Purim doesn’t have all the special mitzvos like Big Purim. And you know what? I also want to be big! Being a little kid is boring, especially when you can’t even bring Moshiach.”

“That’s ridiculous,” said Mushkie, “being little is a big deal. And of course you can bring Moshiach.”

Before I could argue, Mushkie explained. “See the moon outside the window? If you would put it next to the sun, you’d see how the sun is sixty million times bigger

than the moon. That makes the moon seem really small. But the moon can take the sun’s light and reflect it back to us, shining brightly even when it’s nighttime—which is when people need it most.

“That’s why the Yidden are compared to the moon. We are a small nation; small like the moon. But we reflect Hashem’s great, big light to the world, like the moon reflects the sun’s light. And we can shine brightly even in this dark Golus, showing the whole world the right way to live.

Hmm. I think I got it. “So that’s how you can be big, even

when you’re small,” I replied. “By shining Hashem’s big light.”

“Yup!” Mushkie smiled. “And don’t forget, when you battle the Yetzer Hara and do your part to bring Moshiach, then our Commander- in-Chief, Hashem, gives you everything you need to win.”

“Okay Mushkie,” I said, “I think I’m ready for this.”
