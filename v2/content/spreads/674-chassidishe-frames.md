<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">The popular niggun of “Didan Notzach” was composed in (1986), during the court case of the sefarim. These powerful words mean “Victory is ours!” But did you know these words are actually much older than that? They come from a fascinating midrash about a holy man named Abba Yosi and the village of Tzitor. During the celebrations of Hey Teves, (1987), the Rebbe explained that this story teaches every Yid how to defeat the Yetzer Hara.</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">THE the sefarim. These powerful words mean “Victory is ours!”</span>

Little Avrahami felt like an adult. For the first time, his father had allowed him to join the men of Tzitor on an important mission. It was still before dawn as they walked quietly village. Cool dew soaked through Avrahami's shoes as he carried a small spade and hoe across his shoulders. The tools felt heavy, but today he was determined to prove that he was old enough to help.

The day before, the villagers had gathered to hear an important message from Abba Yosi. He told them that while learning Torah beside the river, he had resteded peacefully in its waters.

“For many years I have lived here,”

the spirit explained. “I have never harmed anyone. But now, an evil spirit is coming to drive me away.

If it succeeds,

bring harm to everyone who comes near this river.”

“What can we do?” Abba Yosi asked.

“Gather the people before sunrise,” the spirit replied. “Tell everyone to bring an iron tool. Stand by the river and watch carefully. When the water begins to swirl, strike the tools together and call out, ‘Didan notzach!’ Do not stop until blood rises upon the water. Then you will know the evil spirit has been defeated.”

Before sunrise the next morning, the people of Tzitor gathered with shovels, hoes, spades, and other iron tools. Fathers brought sons who were old enough to help, and Avrahami proudly walked beside his father toward the river.

As they neared the water, Avrahami noticed something was wrong. The villagers spread out along the riverbank and waited in silence.

Suddenly the water began to ripple. The current twisted and churned, and the dark river slowly rose before their eyes.

“Now!” shouted Abba Yosi.

At once, the villagers sprang into action. Shovels crashed against spades, hoes struck rakes, and the ringing sound of iron echoed across the valley. Avrahami banged his own tools together with all his strength.

“Didan notzach!” everyone cried.

Again and again they shouted without stopping. The river churned more violently until, to Avrahami's amazement, a single drop of blood rose to the surface.

The evil spirit had been defeated, and the people of Tzitor returned home praising Hashem for protecting them.

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">Many years later, after the victory of Hey Teves, the Rebbe explained that this midrash contains an important lesson for every Yid:</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">Instead of fighting an evil spirit by a river, we fight the Yetzer Hara, who tries to distract us from learning Torah, doing mitzvos, and serving Hashem.</span>

And just as the people of Tzitor triumphed over the spirit by listening carefully to Abba Yosi, we can also be victorious by following the Rebbe's hora’os.

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">The Rebbe requested that Chassidim begin preparing for Yud Shvat already in Teves, 30 days before. We do this by making good hachlatos and choosing a mashpia to guide us, just like the Yidden in Tzitor had Abba Yosi to guide them. Our mashpia should test us on our progress three times before Yud Shvat.</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">When we follow the Rebbe's instructions and continue fighting the Yetzer Hara, we can confidently sing:</span>

<span class="mc" style="color:#813456">Didan notzach—victory is ours!</span>
