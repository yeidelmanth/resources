“Of course! Now I remember you!” Tatty exclaimed. “I’m so glad that you came by.”

Mommy turned to Ayala with excitement. “Ayala, are you by any chance looking for a job? I’ve been searching for a teacher for our new preschool, and you seem like the perfect fit!”

“You know,” Mommy said, “this is really a bracha from the Rebbe. I was giving up hope on ever finding someone for the job and, all of a sudden, you showed up!”

As the conversation continued, Lev and Ayala told our parents how sad they were that they had no children, even after being married for a few years.

“I believe that in the zechus of doing the Rebbe’s shlichus and teaching in our preschool, Hashem will bentch you with a baby,” Mommy said.

time, exactly when Mommy needed a preschool teacher.

It was just a few weeks before school was starting, and Mommy was getting worried. Who would teach the preschool?

Our family is on Shlichus in The Woodlands, Texas. One year, Tatty and Mommy decided to open a preschool for our Chabad House community.

Baruch Hashem, many families signed up – but there was one problem. We had no teacher!

Mommy didn’t want just any teacher. She wanted someone who would give the kids a real love and excitement for Yiddishkeit.

And so, she sat down to write a letter to the Rebbe and ask for a bracha. She was confident that with the Rebbe’s help, she would find the right teacher.

Time continued to pass with no teacher in sight. Soon, the new school year was just a few weeks away. What would be with the preschool?

One day, a frum couple named Lev and Ayala visited our Chabad House. “We want to move to The

Woodlands,” they said. “Do you remember us? We’ve actually met before…”

Tatty was confused. The couple didn’t look familiar at all. “Please remind me,” he said.

“We came here a few years ago,” Lev explained. “We had been living in Honolulu, Hawaii, and wanted to move to The Woodlands. I told you that I had not received a proper Jewish education and had married a non-Jewish woman.

“We explained that my wife and I were learning about Yiddishkeit, and had decided that we wanted to live a Jewish life according to the Torah. My wife wanted to become a Yid.

“You told us that Houston, Texas, would be a better place for us to live because it has a larger Jewish community. We took your advice and lived there for three years. My wife, Ayala, became a Yid, and has been teaching in the preschool there. But after some time, we decided we wanted to move to our dream city, The Woodlands … and here we are!”
