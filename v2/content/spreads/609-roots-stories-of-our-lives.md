Yanush was one of the few Jews living in the small village of Zerkow2. The only time he went to shul was when he went to the big city nearby, Posen. It was usually just once or twice a month to restock his merchandise, in honor of Rosh Hashana or Yom Kippur, or on his parents’ yartzeits.

As a child, Yanush was jealous of the Jews who lived in the big city of Posen. Many gentiles in Zerkow, encouraged by their local

priest, were very anti-Jewish. There were boys who would spit

at Yanush and call him names. His drunk customers at night did the same, or worse. And most of all, Yanush was now getting older and wanted to build a family. He used to think to himself: As soon as I save up enough money, I’m

going to move to the big city and find a Jewish girl to marry.

But as Yanush grew older, life in Zerkow started to improve. The

old archbishop died and was replaced by a new, much younger man named Antoni Zhembek\*. And one of the first things this

new archbishop did was lift the tax imposed on Jews in the area, and preach about Jews and non-Jews living in harmony together. Bit by bit, life in Zerkow became more pleasant.
