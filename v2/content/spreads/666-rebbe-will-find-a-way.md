“The price is still much more than expected,” my uncle answered honestly. “We’re really stuck.”

There was a short pause. Then his friend said, “I want to help you. I’m sending you $25,000 right now. Zei gezunt.”

My uncle sat there in shock, overwhelmed with gratitude for the clear miracle that had just happened.

A few minutes later, my aunt called. “When I was davening, I felt that everything would work out,” she told him. “There’s nothing to worry about.”

“Nothing to worry about?” my uncle exclaimed. “Let me tell you what just happened!”

He told her about his friend’s call, and she was amazed. The bracha had come at the exact moment when she had been davening at the Ohel.

“I think you should go back into the Ohel and thank the Rebbe for this special bracha,” he said.

What had seemed impossible moments before was now taken care of. They were finally able to finish construction, so they could continue their shlichus with enough space for their family.

This story reminds me that when we turn to the Rebbe and share what we’re going through, the Rebbe finds a way to help. And when things work out, we should report back

The construction costs kept going up. How would they manage?

I want to tell you an incredible story about my aunt and uncle, the Marruses, who are shluchim in Texas.

A few years ago, their house was starting to feel too small. Baruch Hashem, their family had grown, and they really needed more space to continue their shlichus.

So they decided to build an addition onto their house, carefully planning out the details to make sure they could afford it.

At first, everything went according to plan. But after a few months, the construction costs started going up. Before long, they realized something very concerning:

They didn’t have enough money to finish the project.

A close friend heard about the situation and kindly gave a donation to help. But even with that, it still wasn’t enough.

The walls remained unfinished. Piles of material lay scattered on their lawn. The construction crew wouldn’t continue without the money, and my relatives didn’t know what to do next.

New York for the Kinus Hashluchos. On Chof- Beis Shvat, she went to the Ohel and wrote a letter to the Rebbe.

Please give us a bracha to finish building our home in the best way, she wrote, and that we should have menuchas hanefesh even in this stressful situation.

Holding her letter, my aunt waited in the long line with many other shluchos. When it was almost her turn, she called her husband so he could say Tehillim at the same time as when she would be in the Ohel.

As soon as he got her call, my uncle stopped what he was doing. He put on his hat, jacket, and gartel, and began saying Tehillim.

After a few kapitelach, his phone suddenly rang. Usually, he wouldn’t answer in the middle of saying Tehillim. But when he saw who was calling, he picked up.

It was the same friend who had helped them before. “How’s construction going?” the friend asked.
