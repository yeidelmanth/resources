"Ah, Rabbi!" Mr. Mancini greeted Tatty. "I've been meaning to get in touch with you! I heard that you teach people how to read Hebrew. Can I join the class?"

"My mother was a Sephardic Jew," he answered. "But let’s talk about that another time. Tell me, what can I do for you?"

Tatty explained that he needed to put up a sign for the mikvah. Before he knew it, he was holding a permit in his hands! When he brought it to the store, the owner was flabbergasted.

"The board never gives permission for these things," he said. "Someone up there is looking out for you!"

Tatty wholeheartedly agreed. He knew without a doubt that the Rebbe was right there beside him, helping him fulfill his shlichus. As shluchim of the Rebbe, we can overcome any challenge!

After we bought a house a few years into our shlichus, our first priority was to build a mikvah in the backyard. Tatty hired a local builder, Johnny, to get the job done.

“I’ll build your mixer!’” Johnny exclaimed, struggling to pronounce the Hebrew word. “I’ll even give you a discount, because it’s for G-d.”

Now, it was time to apply for permits. Tatty knew that it would be a big headache, because we live in a historic district, where all construction projects have to be approved by a special board.

“There are very strict rules, even for basic things like house colors,” Tatty worried. “Will they let us build a mikvah?”

“Don’t you worry,” Johnny assured Tatty. “I know people on the board. I'll make sure they give you the permits."

But even with Johnny’s connections, it wasn’t so simple.

When they arrived at the government office to hear the board’s decision, the official peered over his glasses, flipping through the application. "According to the city laws,” he said, “30% of your property needs to be greenery. If you build a mikvah, it will cover too much of your yard. You can't do it."

The rule was the rule. It seemed pointless to argue.

How is building a mikvah ever going to be possible? Tatty silently wondered. Rebbe, we’re your shluchim. Please help us!

Then, on their way out of the office, Johnny turned to the official. “Just remember,” he warned, “This rabbi is doing holy work. If you refuse him, you’re also messing with G-d!”

The official turned pale. "Wait, come back! I don’t want to mess with G-d. Here’s a solution: If the rabbi takes off the covering from his driveway, he can build the mikvah."

And just like that, we had permission to start building!

When the mikvah was complete, Tatty went to a local sign-making store to order one for the mikvah.

“What’s the point of making a sign?” he said. “The city will never let you put it up. Don’t come back unless you have a permit from the board.”
