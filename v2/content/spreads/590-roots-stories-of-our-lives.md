In 5332 (1572), after twenty years of devoted service at his post as Chief Rabbi of Moravia, the Maharal returned to Prague once more. News of his arrival spread quickly through the marketplace and the batei medrash; scholars and common people alike rejoiced.

Everyone in Prague remembered the Maharal’s genius, and how the Maharal had inspired them in their time of need. And at the time, Rav Yitzchak Melling, who was the chief rabbi during the Maharal’s absence, was getting older. So the talk began to circulate: when the time would come, the Maharal, now nearly sixty, would be a fine replacement.

Meanwhile, though the Maharal did not return to Prague as the official leader, he set his attention to serve the needs of the community around him. The first matter that the Maharal set his attention to was education.
