A moment later, the airline agent suddenly stood up and started waving her arms. “The system is back on!” she announced.

But there was still a big problem: Their flight was supposed to take off in twenty minutes, and there was a huge line of people waiting. How would the workers possibly check everyone in, get them through security, and board the whole plane—all within twenty minutes?

It seemed impossible. The flight would probably be delayed so that the passengers could board, which would make our family friends miss their connecting flights. Will we be stuck in Africa for Shabbos? they nervously wondered.

The flight took off right on schedule. It was a miracle! Baruch Hashem, they arrived home just in time to spend Shabbos with their family.

As they sat around the Shabbos table, they marveled at how quickly the

Rebbe’s bracha had come true. Even though they were so far away in Africa, they saw how the Rebbe was right there, helping them in their time of need.

The minutes ticked by. Would they get home in time for Shabbos?

My family friends went on a trip to a country called Namibia, which is in Africa. After their fun adventure, it was time to go home.

But getting back wouldn’t be easy. Namibia is very far away, and they’d need to take three flights to get home!

The first flight was on Thursday morning. If everything went smoothly, they would be home late that night, just in time for Shabbos.

They got to the airport extra early to check in. But as they stood in line, they realized that something was wrong. The line wasn’t moving at all.

“What’s going on?” they asked the agent at the desk.

“The computer system isn’t working,” she said. “No one can check in right now.”

Uh-oh… They tried to check in on their phones, but in that part of Africa, the internet is very slow. When they finally got on the

website, it was too late—online check-in had already closed.

Time kept ticking by, and the system was still not working. The line behind them got longer and longer.

If we miss this flight, then we’ll miss the next one too, they worried. And if that happens, we won’t get home in time for Shabbos!

They tried calling the airline, but no one could help. They asked airport workers, but no one knew when the system would start working again. They even searched for different flights with other airlines, but there was nothing available.

Feeling helpless, they called their parents at home and told them what was going on.

Their father quickly sent an email to the Ohel, asking the Rebbe for a bracha that they should get home in time for Shabbos.

Leib, 7 Moshe Elimelech Ortiz, 6 Beit Menachem, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
