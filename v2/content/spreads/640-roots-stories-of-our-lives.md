It was a fine summer day, when Hersh Leib was sitting in his seat in Zalman’s beis midrash. It was between his cheder classes, when he had a few rare minutes to study Torah alone.

Hersh Leib stood up from his seat, and embraced the visitor who had just walked in. The man looked like many of the others in the beis midrash—he wore the clothes of a laborer and a simple cap on his head. But Reb Kehos was no ordinary man. He, too, was a messenger of the Baal Shem Tov.

Reb Kehos glanced around the beis midrash; it was full of men learning, due, in large part, to Hersh Leib’s work. “Is there a place we can talk, in private?”

Hersh Leib led Reb Kehos to the woods behind the building.
