The Rebbe then asked him, “You live in the Bronx. I’d like to send matzah to people in the Bronx. Is it too far for you?”

Zeidy replied that it’s not too far. So the Rebbe continued, “Here is the matzah for them. Go to Rabbi Hodakov (the Rebbe’s secretary), and he will give you the address.”

When Zeidy got the address from Rabbi Hodakov a couple of minutes later, he was quite surprised. It was actually on the other side of the Bronx, quite far from his house. He called his mother and told her, “Ma, I’m going to be late for the Seder. Don’t wait for me.” With that, he was off to the train station.

After a long and complicated journey, he finally made it to the address. Zeidy noticed that this particular building was set aside for families with blind children. He looked again at the paper in his hand. It read, “Apartment 3D.” He climbed the stairs, found the right door, and knocked.

‘Hi, are you Mr. So-and-So?” Zeidy pleasantly asked.

“The Rebbe sent something very special for you and your family.” Zeidy told him.

The man’s face instantly lit up with excitement. ‘‘The Rebbe? Oh, please come in!’’

Zeidy walked in. As they were about to sit down, the man’s family came in. His wife was visibly pregnant, and his two daughters were both blind.

“Ok,” Zeidy announced, “We’re going to make a Pesach Seder together right now.”

They each took a piece of the matzah that the Rebbe had sent, and, instead of four cups of wine, they had paper cups of water. Zeidy took the yarmulka from under his hat, and placed it on the father’s head. He looked around the table, wondering what to say. He asked himself, “What would the Rebbe say?”

Zeidy took a moment to gather his thoughts, and then began. He spoke about emunah, about how Hashem freed our ancestors from slavery, and that He gives us freedom, too. We each have our own personal Mitzrayims to get through. Yet, even when it feels extremely hard, Hashem never gives us more than we can handle. And He is right there, lovingly holding us throughout the challenge. Once you know that—and when you really believe it— you’re already free.

They spoke, sang, and cried together until late at night. Finally the girls went off to bed, and Zeidy got ready to leave. Before he could go, however, he needed the answer to one question that was glaring in his mind. He turned to the

man and asked, “What is your connection with the Rebbe?”

“I work in a slaughterhouse,” the man replied, “and one of my co-workers is a rabbi. One day, I came into work feeling completely lost. I was very worried and confused. I guess it showed on my face, because my rabbi friend immediately asked what was wrong. I explained to him that my two children are blind, and my wife is now pregnant again. The doctors are pressuring us to terminate the pregnancy, chas v’shalom, because otherwise the new child may be born blind, as well.

“After hearing my story, my rabbi friend lovingly urged me to write to the Lubavitcher Rebbe and ask for blessings and guidance. I had nothing to

lose, and I did as he suggested. After a short while, I received a response. The Rebbe said that the baby will be fine, and what’s more, it will be able to see.

“But until you came tonight,” continued the man, “I didn’t know how to have faith in the Rebbe’s blessing. But now, after hearing everything you shared, we understand.”

Zeidy was extremely touched by the story. He wished them a warm “Gut Yom Tov!”, and finally headed home for his own seder.

Over the years, they stayed in touch. Baruch Hashem, the baby was born healthy and able to see, just as the Rebbe promised. The family grew more in their Yiddishkeit, and have become frum Yidden. Both the daughters and the son have created Jewish families of their own, full of beautiful children who can all see.

Chayolim, the lesson for us is clear. Matzah is the food of emunah and refuah. When you reach out and give Shmurah Matzah to your fellow Yid, you are deeply touching his/her neshama. You are giving them power to have trust in Hashem!

This Pesach, be a messenger of emunah. Help ensure that every Yid in the entire world has matzah for the Seder night.

Every year before Pesach, people would come to 770 from far and near to receive a piece of matzah from the Rebbe. This was special matzah—personally hand- baked by the yeshiva bochurim—and as the Rebbe gave it out, he would bentch each person.
