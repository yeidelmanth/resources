It all started when my brother Sholom zoomed into the living room on his roller skates, waving a messy tree branch and a lemon from the fridge. He skidded to a stop right in front of my doll tea party.

“Rina!” he exclaimed, thrusting them toward me. “Here, shake them!”

I blinked at him, flicking a leaf off my toy teapot. “Sholom, it's not Sukkos yet!”

Sholom grinned. “Nope. But I’m just way too excited! The Rebbe has just introduced a completely new idea: to go out and help people bentch lulav and

esrog! All the bochurim in yeshiva are getting ready to go. And I need to practice!”

I tilted my head. “Wait, you’re going to take these to other people?”

“Yep!” he said proudly. “Well, not these,” he laughed, “A real set. We’re going to show Yidden how to make the bracha and shake the arba minim. Isn’t that amazing?”

I felt a little shiver of excitement. “Wow,” I whispered. “The Rebbe really cares about every single Yid.”

P.S. Today, a new girl joined my class. Her name is Ahuva, and she just moved here from somewhere called Vermont. She’s … different. Her uniform isn’t quite right, and during Chumash, she didn’t seem to know where we were up to.

Mivtzoim—they feel like they’ve been around forever! Can you even imagine life without them? Yet, unbelievably, before the Rebbe introduced each of the mitzvah campaigns that we know so well, they simply didn’t exist.

Join us as we explore imaginary old diaries that could have belonged to kids just like you— kids who might have lived during the time when the Rebbe first announced many of these mivtzoim.

(1953( י״ז תשרי, תשי׳׳ד MOTZEI SHABBOS - 2ND NIGHT OF CHOL HAMOED SUKKOS\* Dear Diary,

It’s so late at night, but now that the first days of Sukkos are over, I must tell you about it!

On the first day, Tatty took me and Sholom to do what the Rebbe asked! We walked all over Crown Heights, stopping people on the streets to see if they wanted to bentch lulav. We saw tons of bochurim holding their lulavim too. Some of them were even walking all the way to Brownsville or the Bronx … anywhere a Yid could be found.

Sholom was so brave, running up to people and saying, “Excuse me, would you like to do the mitzvah of lulav and esrog?” At first, I was nervous, but after a while, I joined in. We even knocked on some doors!

Lots of people gave us strange looks. They couldn’t understand why we cared if they did the mitzvah or not.

But other people were very happy to see us. One man told us he hadn’t done the mitzvah since he was a little boy. He went back into his house to put on a yarmulka and wash negel vasser, and then bentched lulav with tears in his eyes.

A lady told me, “Thank you so much for doing this. Please come back every day of Yom Tov!”

After all the walking, asking, and bentching, I went to my class Sukkos party.

Morah taught us that even though the lulav, esrog, hadasim, and aravos are so different from each other, we hold them all together. That is just like the Yidden. We may look different from each other, but we are really all part of one people.

P.S. Ahuva came, which is nice, because she’s been too shy to come to class parties until now. I asked her if she wanted to sit with me. She smiled and said yes.

)1957( י״ז תשרי, תשי׳׳ח MOTZEI SHABBOS - 2ND NIGHT OF CHOL HAMOED SUKKOS\* Dear Diary,

Wow, what a Yom Tov this was! It’s already 10:30 at night, but I have so much to tell you.

I can’t believe it’s been four years since the Rebbe first introduced Mivtza Arba Minim. Now that Sholom and I are older, we even joined Tatty yesterday when he went to bentch lulav with the patients and Jewish doctors at the hospital, just like the Rebbe wants.
