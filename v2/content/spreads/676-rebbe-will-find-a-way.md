“We must write to the Rebbe,” they finally decided.

Because it was so urgent, my elter zeidy wrote a letter that same day. Could we sell Lubavitch House? he asked. And if yes, where should we go?

The next morning, after davening in the Chabad House, a real estate agent who had been helping them came over to my elter zeidy.

“Rabbi Feller,” he said, “you won’t believe this! A perfect building just became available. It has twenty bedrooms, a large dining room, and a huge kitchen. It’s only about ten minutes away from here. The owners have to sell it quickly, so you’d be able to buy it at a low price!”

Elter Zeidy was so excited! The shluchim hurried to see the building, and they all agreed: it was exactly what they needed.

The timing was incredible. Less than twenty- four hours after writing to the Rebbe, the perfect building had become available!

They sold Lubavitch House, and with that money, together with a generous donation, they were able to buy the new building.

From my elter zeidy’s story, I learned that when a Chossid has a question, he shouldn’t feel stuck. Through strengthening our hiskashrus and writing to the Rebbe, we can be sure that the Rebbe will find a way to answer.

Lubavitch House was full of memories, but it was getting too small. What should the shluchim do?

My elter zeidy, Rabbi Moshe Feller, is the head shliach of Minnesota.

In the early years of his shlichus, Chabad of Minnesota bought a big mansion that became known as Lubavitch House.

Hundreds of Shabbatons, weddings, brissim, and programs took place there. Bais Chana for women was there too, bringing thousands of students through its doors to learn Torah and grow in Yiddishkeit.

But almost thirty years later, the building needed a lot of repairs. And by then, Lubavitch House wasn’t big enough to host all the programs, anyway.

“What should we do?” the shluchim wondered. “Should we renovate Lubavitch House? Or should we find a larger building?”

It wasn’t an easy decision. Lubavitch House was very special to them because of the many mitzvos and memories that had happened there. But they also needed a bigger space.

On Erev Rosh Hashana תש”ס (1999), my elter zeidy came to Lubavitch House and saw a note by the door.

“I’m a contractor,” the note said, “and I love this mansion. I would like to buy it.”

When Elter Zeidy called him, the contractor offered a very large amount of money— almost double what the building was worth!

It now seemed like a good option to sell Lubavitch House. But if they sold it, where would they go? The shluchim searched for a new place, but nothing seemed like the right fit.
