An open miracle helped Tatty’s business partner believe in Hashem.

One day, Tatty’s business partner, who isn’t Jewish, arrived at the office very late. He slumped into his chair and put his head in his hands, looking very worried.

“What’s going on?” Tatty asked. “Is everything okay?”

The business partner sighed deeply. “It’s my mother-in-law,” he said. “She’s seriously ill. The doctors say that they can’t do much to help her.”

His eyes were sad and tired. Tatty could see how hard this was for him.

“Write a letter to the Rebbe,” he suggested. “He can bless her to feel better.”

“But I don’t believe in miracles,” the man retorted. “I don’t even believe that Hashem runs the world.” He was quiet for a moment and then shook his head. “You know what? If there’s a chance that it will help my mother-in-law get better, then I’ll do it.”

And so, he took Tatty’s advice and wrote the letter.

Two days later, anxious for an update, Tatty asked him how his mother-in-law was doing.

“Not very good,” Tatty’s partner replied. “The doctors have given up hope. They say there’s no chance that she’ll get better.”

“So then your wife should also write to the Rebbe!” Tatty told him. “After all, it’s her mother who is sick, and I’m sure she will pray extra hard for her recovery.”

The partner accepted Tatty’s suggestion, and asked his wife to write to the Rebbe.

Two days later, Tatty again asked how things were going.
