Mivtzoim—they feel like they’ve been around forever! Can you even imagine life without them? Yet, unbelievably, before the Rebbe introduced each of the mitzvah campaigns that we know so well, they simply didn’t exist.

Join us as we explore imaginary old diaries that could have belonged to kids just like you— kids who might have lived during the time when the Rebbe first announced many of these mivtzoim.

Starting from when he was just four years old, Ahrele\_who would probably be an Elter Zeidy by now\_wrote down these memories (with the help of his tatty), bringing us into the moments when Mivtza Matzah first began. ראש חודש ניסן, תשי"ד

“I’m so excited that it’s almost Pesach! ” I exclaimed to Tatty, who was helping me and Dovi wash the toys in the bathtub. “You know why Pesach is my favorite Yom Tov? It's because I love eating giant, crunchy, round matzah.”

“Every Yom Tov is your favorite!” insisted Dovi, who thinks that just because I’m four and he’s seven, he knows everything. “Purim was your favorite because you love eating little, tiny, triangle hamantashen, and Sukkos—”

“Oy, I didn’t even tell you what the Rebbe said in the sicha on Shabbos about matzah. Gevaldig!” interrupted Tatty just in time. “Listen up. In the olden days, the rav of every place would sell Shmura Matzah to the people in his shul, and that way everyone had proper, kosher, mehudar matzah for Pesach. Sadly, this minhag stopped. The Rebbe asked that it should start again—all rabbanim and leaders should send Shmura Matzah to their community members. If there is even one Yid who doesn’t have Shmura Matzah, we should send it to him!”

“Davka Shmura?” asked Mommy as she passed by, carrying a hamper full of stuffed animals to bring to the washing machine.

“Yes. This way, the Rebbe said, hundreds of thousands of Yidden will do the mitzvah properly! Matzah is called the “bread of emunah” and the “bread of healing.” Making sure all Yidden do this mitzvah b’hiddur will make it a healthy year in ruchniyus, which will make it a healthy year in gashmiyus!”

As any seven-year-old like me knows, there are four sons who come to the seder.

Well, anyways, that was until now. The Rebbe just told us that … wait for it … there is a fifth son! This son doesn’t even know it’s Pesach and doesn’t come to the seder at all!

Our task is to make sure to reach out to every Yid who doesn’t know about Pesach, and to make sure they celebrate the Yom Tov.

And that’s what giving out Shmura Matzah is all about! That’s why Yidden in Eretz Yisroel have been giving matzah to soldiers, government people, and not-yet-frum students.

And it’s not just happening in Eretz Yisroel. It’s all over the world!

P.S. I’m even big enough to write in my diary by myself now!

I want to fly to Eretz Yisroel to join in their new “Matzah For Every Student” campaign. Thousands of school kids went on trips to the matzah bakery in Kfar Chabad to experience making matzah, and even got to take a matzah home with them.

Man, that’s even better than a trip to the Yam Hamelach!

The way the Rebbe cares about every single Yid is unbelievable. The Rebbe said in a sicha yesterday that we can’t just be happy with giving out matzah to the people in our communities or to people that we know. The Rebbe said, “As long as there is one single Yid, man or woman, child or adult, in your surroundings— and the meaning of “your surroundings” is the entire world (!)— who does not have Shmura Matzah, or has Shmura Matzah but is unaware of its importance and message, there is no doubt that you must do something about it.” י"ב ניסן, תשל"ז

Remember when I traveled to Kentucky before Pesach when I was 24? My younger cousin Moish is doing something even

more extraordinary—he is going to help Yidden in prisons

The Rebbe said in a sicha yesterday that we can’t forget Yidden who are bedridden in hospitals and old age homes, and, of course, Yidden who are stuck in prisons. We have to help them celebrate Pesach, too. And that in this z’chus, the sick Yidden will get better faster, and the prisoners will get freed earlier! Amen!

Gotta run—I am heading to the mall near my house to give out Shmura Matzah, and then right back home to help my wife with Pesach prep!

My Yitzy is so excited! The Rebbe has launched a massive campaign to get the kids involved in Mivtza Pesach as well! This actually turned into a contest called “The Matzah Ball Contest.”

Involving the kids is so awesome! I wonder what “The Matzah Ball Contest” will lead to next!

(Oy, my toddler just woke up my baby in the middle of her nap! Gotta run!)
