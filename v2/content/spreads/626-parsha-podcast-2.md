Is that a boat? It kinda looks like a boat. Oooh! Those tiny animals are so cute!

Yossi, that’s pretty impressive! You made a really accurate teiva. I’m sure our guest this week will be super impressed. Right, Basya?

Thanks, Mimi. It’s just that Noach had the teiva to keep him safe from his big problem, but I don’t have anything to help with my big problem!

What’s your big problem? Maybe it’s something Noach can help you with?

Oh, yes! It’s hashgacha pratis that he’s our guest. He’ll for sure be able to help you.

Right on time kinderlach! I was just in the middle of building my teiva. Let me go inside so I can talk to you without distractions.

Thank you! We actually have a problem we wanted to discuss with you.

Well, yesterday I saw someone doing something silly, and my Yetzer Hara keeps saying that I should tell it to my friend Gitty, because it’s reeeally funny. I know I shouldn’t, because that would be lashon hara, but my Yetzer Hara won’t leave me alone. I wish I could just run into a boat and not hear him anymore!

I built us a small teiva … but I don’t really think it would stop the Yetzer Hara.

Noach, you’re able to go into the teiva and be safe from the mabul, which is your big problem, but our big problem is our Yetzer Hara. How do we get away from him?

Actually, Yossi was right on track. You see, “teiva” has two meanings: “a large boat” or “a word”. The Baal Shem Tov teaches us that Hashem gives every Yid a teiva: “ּתֵיבֹות הַּתֹורָה וְהַּתְפִילָה— the words of Torah and tefillah”! This is your personal ammunition to keep you safe from all of your problems, including that foolish Yetzer Hara!

But my siddur isn’t a boat. How do I go inside of words?

Well, I can imagine how that could be possible. You know what it’s like when you really get into your davening or learning? When you do it with a ton of chayus, it can feel as if you’re actually somewhere else. Like you’re surrounded by the kedusha and safe from all scary things in the world.

Oooh! I think that happens to me sometimes. When I get super-focused on davening or learning, it does seem like nothing else exists.

Exactly! You Levin kids catch on quick! I’ll just add one more thing: We read about the teiva in Chitas on Tuesday. Tuesday is the day of creation when Hashem said “ּכִי טֹוב—it is good” twice, because it was good for Hashem and good for the people.

What does that have to do with being safe in our davening teiva?

Well, we must make sure that our “going into the teiva” is not just good for Hashem up in Shamayim, but that it’s also good for people down here in this world. We have to help everyone else, too!

Oh, I get it now. We should invite others into our learning and davening. We can influence them to daven and learn with a lot of focus so that they are also safe inside of their own teiva.

And then we’ll fix the whole world and bring Moshiach!
