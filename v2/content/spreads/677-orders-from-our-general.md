<p class="lead">It’s easy to imagine someone who’s not free—a slave, a prisoner, or a person who’s controlled by someone/ something else.</p>

<span class="mc">But what does it look like for someone to be free?</span>

In this week’s parsha, Hashem tells Moshe Rabbeinu that when the Yidden leave Mitzrayim, they will come to Har Sinai to serve Him. This shows the real reason why the Yidden were freed: not just to stop being slaves to Paroh, but to receive the Torah and serve Hashem.

When we Yidden serve Hashem, that is when we are truly free.

There’s a hint to this in the way the Luchos were made. The Aseres Hadibros were not written with ink or paint. They were engraved into the actual stone. The Hebrew

By accepting the Luchos, which stand for the whole Torah, the Yidden became free—for real.

Choose the answer that shows real freedom:

Doing whatever I feel like, whenever I want.

Choosing to serve Hashem, even when it’s hard.

When you take the people out of Mitzrayim, you will serve Hashem on this mountain.

Sometimes, doing the right thing can feel hard. The Yetzer Hara tries to convince us that doing the wrong thing is easier or more fun. He wants us to think that freedom means we get to choose what we do or how we do it.

Want to know what true freedom looks like? Every time we do a mitzvah instead of an aveira, we show that we are not slaves to the Yetzer Hara (the little Paroh inside of us). We are truly free to do what we were created for: what Hashem wants. And for that, we have the Torah.

Which of the Aseres Hadibros reminds us that we were freed from Mitzrayim in order to serve Hashem?
