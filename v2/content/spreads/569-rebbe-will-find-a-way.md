woman from France who was becoming frum once went to the Rebbe for yechidus. Her parents had never given her a Jewish name, and she asked the Rebbe to choose a name for her. The Rebbe said: “Your French name is Jacqueline, which is similar to Yaakov. You should choose the name Yaakovah.”

The woman thanked the Rebbe, but she looked confused. “Don’t worry if you don’t know Hebrew,” the Rebbe told her. “I’ll write the name down so you won’t forget.” The Rebbe wrote “Yaakovah” on a small piece of paper and gave it to her.

“One minute,” Bubby exclaimed, jumping up in excitement. “Casey’s middle name is also Jacqualyn!”

Bubby began searching through boxes of old papers, hoping that she still had the one with Mommy’s Jewish name. After a while, she found the paper!

Right there were the words: ”יַעֲקֹבַה ּבַת רִבְקָה—Yaakovah bas Rivka”.

Stunned, Bubby called Mommy right away. “You won’t believe it!” she cried. “Your Jewish name is Yaakovah, not Kayla!”

Bubby and Mommy were amazed by the tremendous hashgacha pratis. Bubby had called at just the right moment, before Mommy was about to make Kayla her official Jewish name!

Mommy’s eyes filled with tears. The Rebbe had given her the gift of her real Jewish name, and she knew she would treasure it forever.

Mommy was about to make “Kayla” her official Jewish name. Suddenly, her phone began to ring…

For the first 25 years of Mommy’s life, she barely knew anything about Yiddishkeit. All of that changed in תשס”ד (2004), when her mother, our Bubby, began joining the Torah classes of Rabbi Shlomo Schwartz and his wife, Olivia.

Bubby shared what she learned with Mommy, who was amazed by the beauty and depth of Yiddishkeit. If this is what being Jewish is all about, she thought, I want to learn more!

That summer, Mommy went on a Birthright trip to Eretz Yisrael. The next year, she decided to leave her high-paying job in order to go back to Eretz Yisrael for seminary.

As she packed her suitcase, she turned to Bubby and said, “I want to use my Jewish name when I’m in Israel. Do you know what it is?”

“I’m afraid I don’t,” Bubby said sadly. “When you were a baby, we named you Casey

Jacqualyn. I wanted to give you a Jewish name, too, but the rabbi told me, ‘There’s no Jewish name for Casey. Your baby’s Jewish name will be … bas Rivka.’ I didn’t understand exactly what he said. He also gave me a paper with Hebrew letters, but I couldn’t read it, so I don’t know what it said.”

Mommy was stunned. Did she not have a Jewish name? She went to Rabbi Schwartz and asked him what to do.

“Don’t worry,” he said. “Just pick a name, see if you like it, and then make it your official Jewish name.”

Mommy chose the name “Kayla” because it was similar to her English name Casey. A few months later, she was ready to make Kayla her official Jewish name.

At the same time, Bubby was reading “To Know and to Care”, a book of stories about the Rebbe.
