It took several weeks for the Maharal and Reb Betzalel to make the journey from Prague to Worms. When they finally arrived at the house of Reb Eliyahu Baal Shem, they saw a home which looked, for the most part, quite ordinary, simple, and modest. It was made of stone, with narrow arched windows and a splintered wooden door—just like all the others on the street. Yet, it was easy to spot Reb Eliyahu’s house, due to the steady stream of people at the front door. Many of the visitors appeared to be crippled or ill. There was a young

man so frail he had to be carried in on a chair. An old woman hobbled in with her cane, screeching with pain each time she moved her left foot. A young mother came carrying a whimpering child in each arm.

A low archway separated between the front and back rooms. From their spot, the Maharal and Rav Betzalel were able to see a man— tall and broad-shouldered, with a silver-gray beard and a gentle smile—sitting in the back. From the whispers around them, they gathered that this man was Reb Eliyahu Baal Shem himself.
