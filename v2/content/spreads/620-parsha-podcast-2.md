chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

My class is raising money for needy children in Eretz Yisroel. Do any of you want to give some savings to tzedakah?

Yossi, now isn’t the time. We need to call our guest! Anyway, I’m saving that money for a new siddur.

But the poor children! Chaim, do you have any money from helping Eli Brown with his homework?

Sorry, Yossi. I already decided what I’m doing with all my money.

Mimi’s right, we shouldn’t keep Avner the Farmer waiting. Yossi, can you please make the call?

Okay, I’ll dial his number. But we’re going to talk about this again after the call.

Baruch Hashem, good! And how are you, Farmer Avner?

I am so grateful to Hashem that Hashem provided me with such parnassah that I always have the time and energy to discuss Torah!

Yes! Even then! The time right before Shavuos, is a very busy—and special—one for all of us farmers in Eretz Yisroel. And even then I have time to study Torah.

Great question! When our fruits begin to grow, we each tie a red string around the very first ones. Then, in the spring, we go around and collect these fruits.

We learn about that in this week's Parsha! When you go up to Yerushalayim for Shavous, you bring the first, nicest, and best of your crop to the Bais Hamikdash! A special gift for Hashem!

But, you probably worked so hard to grow them. Don’t you want to keep the best ones for yourselves?

Well, who does this earth belong to in the first place?

…Which makes these Hashem’s fruits! And I thank Him for sharing them with me by giving Him the first and the best. When we recognize that everything comes from Hashem, He showers us with incredible brachos.

Of course! Last year, my crop wasn’t that great. The pomegranates were the size of grapes, and the grapes were the size of grain. It was so hard for me to give away the few good ones. But I did. And Baruch Hashem, this year I’m able to bring Hashem even better fruits!

That sounds really difficult. How were you able to do that?

Like I said, all of the fruits I grow and everything that I own, really belongs to Hashem. Once you recognize that, no matter how challenging it may feel, the right thing to do is absolutely clear: we give the best to Him.

Wow. I also want to show that I know everything is from Hashem! I wish we were able to give bikkurim.

Actually, there are many ways that you can give Hashem bikkurim, like right when you wake up in the morning—

We’re giving our first thoughts and words to Hashem!
