The moment she landed, her phone rang. Someone else wanted to buy the house for much more money!

But it was too late. She had already signed to sell it to the first buyer.

That Friday, Tzivi went to the Ohel. She wrote a letter to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha that the sale should go smoothly. She didn’t even mention the higher offer. She just wanted the stress to finally be over.

An hour later, the first buyer called. “I won’t be ready on time,” he said. “Can we push off the sale?”

Tzivi was so excited! Because this buyer had broken the contract, she was free to sell it to someone else!

When Tzivi was about to give up, the Rebbe’s bracha turned everything around.

Our aunt Tzivi tossed and turned, unable to sleep. It felt like everything was going wrong, and she didn’t know what to do.

It had all started when she bought an old, run-down house. “I’ll fix it up and sell it for a profit!” she thought happily. But almost as soon as the work began, Tzivi saw a big orange paper stuck to the front door. In bold letters it said: “STOP ALL CONSTRUCTION until the city approves your plans.”

Her eyes grew wide. “But we just started!” she cried. Now, she didn't know if she'd ever be able to fix the house at all.

Tzivi worked hard to get the plans approved. She filled out a lot of papers and made many phone calls, but nothing changed. Then Covid started, and all the city offices closed. Months passed, and she still couldn’t do construction.

Finally, the city approved the building plans. But now, Tzivi faced another problem:

The workers were doing a sloppy job, and some of them wouldn’t even show up to work.

Meanwhile, bills kept piling up. Tzivi had borrowed money to buy the house, and now she had to borrow even more to pay the mortgage, the loans, and all the extra fees.

Finally, after two years of stress and heartache, Tzivi had enough. I can’t take it anymore, she thought. I’ll just sell the house the way it is. Even if I lose money, at least this nightmare will be over.

She found a buyer who agreed to buy the unfinished house. The price was low, and she would lose a lot of money, but she was so tired that she signed the contract. The sale was set for the last week of Tammuz, תשפ"ב (2022).

A few days before the sale was to take place, on Chof-Alef Tammuz, Tzivi flew to New York.
