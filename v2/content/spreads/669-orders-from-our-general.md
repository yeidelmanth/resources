<p class="lead">What would we do without light?</p>

Imagine we had to eat our meals in the dark, or couldn't see anything and would trip and fall every time we tried to walk. Light helps us in so many ways!

Every Erev Shabbos, Yiddishe women and girls bring light by lighting Shabbos candles. This light brings shalom into our homes, making Shabbos pleasant and comfortable. It also helps us serve Hashem: we can learn Torah, see which food is kosher for us to eat, and look inside a siddur to say the right brachos before and after eating.

But that's not all; Shabbos candles do something even more incredible! Like the candles of Sara and Rivka Imeinu, Shabbos licht light up our homes all week long.

We don't actually see them burning that long, but they bring ruchniyus light into our homes for the entire week. They help everything we do shine with the light of Yiddishkeit.

What’s the most special thing that Shabbos candles bring into the home?

Warmth, calm, and a cozy Shabbos feeling.

A light that guides the home all week long with Torah and mitzvos.

\[Sara Imeinu's\] Shabbos candles shone from one Erev Shabbos to the next.

<span class="mc">Every Yiddishe girl is the daughter of Sara, Rivka, Rochel, and Leah. As soon as she becomes Bas Mitzvah, she has the mission of bringing light into her home, together with her mother and sisters.</span>

How can she train for this great responsibility? By lighting Shabbos candles even when still under Bas Mitzvah!

In fact, our minhag is for a young girl to light candles before her mother does. She’s the first one to light up the home.

After all, this is the mission of every Yid: to light up our homes with Yiddishkeit, brightening the whole world.

Why do you think young Jewish girls are chosen to light before their mothers?

How does brightening our own homes light up the entire world?
