Now Tatty knew without a doubt that he had to get a trumpet—and he knew exactly which color to buy. As soon as that part of the Rebbe video was finished, he went online and ordered a brand new golden trumpet to play in honor of Hakhel.

Throughout the rest of that Hakhel year, Tatty happily played the trumpet for our family, friends, and school, as well as at many children's rallies. Until today, he continues

And do you know what makes this story extra special?

Tatty didn’t even write to the Rebbe about his dilemma, because he didn’t think it was important enough to include in his letter.

And yet, the Rebbe knew what was on Tatty’s mind. It was important to the Rebbe, because he felt how much it bothered Tatty. The Rebbe made sure to send him an answer.

As the leader of all the Yidden, the Rebbe is connected to each one of us in a very deep way. The Rebbe knows what’s going on in our lives and nothing is too small or unimportant. The Rebbe wants to help us with everything we ask for—and even with the things that we don’t ask for!

Tatty didn’t write about the trumpet in his letter. To his amazement, the Rebbe knew exactly what was on his mind

Tatty has always loved to play the trumpet. As life got busier, though, he didn’t have time to play for fun anymore. On rare occasions, he would play a few songs for us, but most of the time, his trumpet sat in a dark closet collecting dust.

In 5783, the year of Hakhel, some of Tatty’s friends remembered that he knows how to play the trumpet. “You should start playing again!” they told him. “After all, in the times of the Beis Hamikdash, the kohanim would play trumpets when Hakhel started!”

Tatty just sighed. He wished he could start playing again, but his old trumpet didn’t work properly, and he needed a new one. And when he looked online, there were so many different colors available. Which one should he choose?

Feeling overwhelmed by all the choices, Tatty decided not to get a trumpet. He tried forgetting about it, but in the back of his mind, he still wanted to start playing again.

The next time Tatty went to the Ohel, he wrote a pan about a lot of personal things.

But even though he was so torn about what to do about the trumpet, he didn’t write about it in his letter. After all, it was just a trumpet. He didn’t think it was important enough to ask the Rebbe about.

Tatty finished writing his letter and went to daven at the Ohel.

Afterward, he went to watch the Rebbe video that was playing nearby.

To his amazement, the Rebbe was describing a midrash that speaks about … trumpets! The Rebbe was saying that in the times of the Beis Hamikdash, every kohen would play a golden trumpet to announce that Hakhel was starting—and if a kohen didn’t blow a trumpet, people would think that he wasn’t a real kohen!

Wait a minute, Tatty realized. I’m a firstborn, and when Moshiach comes, the firstborns will
