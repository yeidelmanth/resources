chayolim just like you. Welcome to our podcast, where

Chayolim, in what way can this week’s lesson make a difference to you? What changes can you make in your own life?

But … don’t let it stop here! Conduct your own interviews at the Shabbos table. Choose someone

to be the podcast’s guest, and together explore how to live with the messages from the parsha.

I always wanted to meet a kohen who worked in the Bais Hamikdash. Parshas Tzav is the perfect time to do that! Please, everyone?

Oh, hi! Excuse me as I just clean up this blood and ashes from myself so I can take your call—

Aaagh! I mean, sorry for interrupting you, but why are you covered in blood and ashes?

That’s okay. I’m in the middle of my shift in the Beis Hamikdash. I was just bringing up some karbanos and burning things.

Huh? How can a karban be tamei? Karbanos have to be tahor, and the Torah calls them “קָדְׁשִים—holy,” the opposite of tamei!

Ah, yes, but if a karban touches something tamei, then the karban becomes tamei as well. It’s a very sad situation. If it happens, then we have a mitzvah to burn it.

But, but—why do you have to burn it? Maybe you could just nicely put it on the side, with a sign that says: “Don’t touch me! I’m tamei!”

Yes, yes, that is a very good idea. But we still burn it, because it’s a mitzvah to do so.

I think I know why. Is it because of the Yetzer Hara?

Oh, oh, I get it! Maybe someone will see the tamei karban, and the Yetzer Hara will try extra hard to get them to eat it. He’ll say, “Yum! Look at that piece of meat! You’ve been working hard all day, and your tummy is empty! Why don’t you eat it?”

Or what if someone doesn’t even know that it’s tamei? They might eat it by accident!

Ohhh, you burn it so that no one accidentally does an aveira?

You kids have real Torah minds! Good job on figuring that one out.

Yes, we burn kodshim that have become tamei because there is a tiny chance that someone might accidentally eat it or fall for their Yetzer Hara.

Well, one Yid doing something wrong is a big deal for Hashem!

And it should be for us, too. We should also do everything we can so that no Yiddishe kid does anything against what Hashem wants!

Well for one, you’re all doing a great job with this Parshafier Squad! I’m sure many kids out there are growing in their Torah and mitzvos because of it. So, chayolim, think about it: What can you do to protect yourself from the Yetzer Hara?

…and that’s another fabulous episode of the Parsha Podcast. Reb Kohen, thank you for joining us! We’ll be sure to think the question over. See you all chayolim next week!
