A few hours later, Tatty’s phone rang. It was Janet. “Rabbi, you won’t believe it,” she said. “For the first time in weeks, David actually smiled. He seems to be in much less pain today. David told me that right before I arrived, the surgeon told him that he’s showing improvement—and won’t need the dangerous surgery they had scheduled.” It was a miracle!

Tatty visited David in the hospital a few days later. After saying hello, David said, “My wife told me about the letter she wrote to the Rebbe and the mitzvos she decided to do. I made my own resolution too—I want to start eating more kosher food.”

Sure enough, David’s health continued to improve, along with his mitzvah observance.

Janet’s heart filled with fear. Was there anything she could do to help her husband?

Six years ago, on a hot day in Tishrei, Tatty heard a knock on our door.

It was Janet, a member of our community where we are on shlichus in Oro Valley, Arizona. She looked very worried. “You know my husband, David, was diagnosed with a terrible illness,” Janet began. “But what you might not know is how serious it is. The situation is getting worse. The doctor says that David is in critical condition.”

Janet’s forehead creased with worry, and tears filled her eyes. “Please, Rabbi,” she cried. “There must be something I can do to save my husband’s life!”

Tatty listened with compassion, wishing he could make everything better for Janet and her family. “We’ve all been praying for David,” he said, “and we’ll continue praying even more. But there’s

something else you can do: write a letter to the Lubavitcher Rebbe. Ask him for a blessing that David should have a complete recovery.”

Tatty then explained to Janet that a bracha is like rain. In order to receive it, you need a container to catch it—a mitzvah. “Why don’t you take on a mitzvah or two as a merit for David’s recovery?” he suggested. “You can start by fasting on Yom Kippur and lighting Shabbos candles every week.”

“That’s a great idea,” Janet said gratefully. “And you know what, I’ll also be more careful about keeping Shabbos.”

She began writing a letter to the Rebbe, asking for a bracha that David should be healed. Then, feeling a lot calmer, she went to visit him in the hospital.
