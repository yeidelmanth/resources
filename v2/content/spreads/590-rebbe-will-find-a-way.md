As Tatty left the tent to go into Ohel, he met an older shliach from Florida who was on his way out of the Ohel.

“Shalom aleichem,” the shliach greeted Tatty warmly. “How are you doing in Tallahassee?”

After Tatty described how hopeless he felt, the shliach asked, “Tell me, how much money do you need every month?”

“I will give you that amount of money each month,” announced the shliach.

“But—what do you mean?” Tatty stammered in shock. “Why are you doing this for me?”

The shliach shrugged his shoulders. “I’m not really sure. As I walked out of the Ohel, I was suddenly inspired to help a fellow shliach with their expenses. When I noticed you, a new shliach, I decided that you would be the one I would help.”

Tatty was blown away by the miracle that had just taken place. Before even walking into the Ohel, all his worries and questions— even those that he had not written—were washed away in a single instant! Before he even asked, he was answered.

The shliach continued helping Tatty and Mommy for a full year until they were able to manage on their own. Not only that, he even helped them buy a house!

Today, our family runs a vibrant shlichus with many successful pe’ulos for the local community and university students. Whenever we face a challenge, we know that the Rebbe is at our side, helping us succeed in our shlichus so we can bring Moshiach now!

Tatty and Mommy didn’t have money for their basic needs, let alone their shlichus activities. Would they be forced to leave their shlichus?

Tatty and Mommy moved on shlichus to Tallahassee around five years after Gimmel Tammuz. They came with much excitement, but hardly any money.

On the first day of looking for an apartment, they met a landlord who was thrilled to hear that they were moving to town. “My mother always wished that I would connect more to my Judaism,” he explained. “Now I’ll finally be able to learn what it means to be a Jew.”

This was clear Hashgacha Pratis, showing Tatty and Mommy that Tallahassee was the right place for their shlichus.

Their first event—a Purim party—was also very encouraging. Tatty and Mommy called all the Jewish-sounding names in the local phone book, and over thirty Yidden showed up! Their shlichus was off to a great start.

But as time went on, Tatty and Mommy had a big problem: they were running out of money.

“What should we do?” Mommy asked Tatty one day, a worried look in her eyes. “Without money, how will we pay our rent or buy food? How will we continue making events for the Yidden here?”

Tatty looked down at the floor sadly. “I don’t know. I tried raising money, but the local Yidden don’t have much to give. There must be something we can do…”

Tatty and Mommy tried thinking of all types of solutions, but nothing worked out. The money continued running out, until there was barely any left.

That year, on the night of Gimmel Tammuz, Tatty sat at the Ohel feeling very down.
