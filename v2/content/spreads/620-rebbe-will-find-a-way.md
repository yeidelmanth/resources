Not long after, Tatty traveled to New York for a simcha. He doesn’t go there often; maybe once or twice a year. While he was there, he made sure to visit the Ohel.

As he walked through the tent, he suddenly saw a familiar face. It was Motti!

Tatty’s eyes widened with surprise. He ran over to Motti, who looked just as shocked. "This is incredible!" Motti exclaimed. "I just wrote to the Rebbe about our learning, and here you are!"

After years of not seeing each other, but still learning together every week, they both ended up at the Ohel at exactly the same time!

Tatty and Motti knew that this wasn’t random—it was a sign that the Rebbe was proud and happy with their learning. It reminded them that even a small mitzvah

like learning for just ten minutes a week could make a real

With hashgacha pratis every step of the way, Tatty knew his hachlata was making a difference.

Tatty sat at the computer checking his emails, but he just couldn’t focus. His mind was filled with thoughts about the Yidden in Eretz Yisroel and the brave soldiers who risk their lives to protect them.

Ever since the war started on Shemini Atzeres, (October 7), Tatty wanted to help. But what could he do?

Just then, an email caught his eye: "Sign up to learn Torah with a chavrusa for just ten minutes a week as a z’chus for a soldier in Eretz Yisroel."

"I can do that!" Tatty exclaimed. Without hesitation, he signed up and eagerly waited to be matched with a chavrusa.

A few days later, Tatty received an email with the name of his new chavrusa. He had expected it

to be someone random, but to his surprise, it was Motti—an old friend whom he hadn’t seen in years! What hashgacha pratis! Out of all the people who signed up, they were paired together.

Tatty and Motti were given the name of a soldier, Amichai Moshe ben Basya Chana, to have in mind while they learned. They decided to learn Shaar Habitachon in English as a z’chus for Amichai Moshe’s safety. Week after week, they looked forward to their learning. It gave them so much chayus!

One day, they received an email thanking them for learning together for a whole year. Tatty felt so happy and proud that he had kept his hachlata for so long.
