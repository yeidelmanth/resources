Now that Moshe was back in Posen, Reb Yehuda Leib found his seventeen-year-old son a shidduch. The young woman’s name was Sara, the eldest daughter of Reb Shneur Zalman—a diamond merchant who was also a descendant of the Maharal. Alas, Sara had been orphaned at a young age, and so when she gave birth to their eldest child, a boy, they named him “Shneur Zalman”, after her late father. Moshe and Sara were soon blessed with a daughter as well, whom they called Freida.

Sara took over her father’s business, and was quite successful. Reb Moshe, too, became involved in business, traveling to countries around Europe for his dealings. Throughout it all, however, he

was sure to maintain his studies. Wherever Reb Moshe went, he’d visit the local libraries and archives in search of books to add to his growing library.

exactly one hundred years after his great- great-grandfather, the Maharal, graced the town of Posen with a year of his leadership.

In the year 5425 (1665), exciting news started to spread throughout Jewish communities across Europe: Moshiach was on his way! A man named Shabsai Tzvi had declared himself to be Moshiach ben Dovid. Jews began to sell their businesses and properties, and prepared to rush to Eretz Yisrael as soon as Shabsai Tzvi would announce it was time.

The Jewish people were so broken from years of suffering and persecution, that they so desperately wanted to believe Moshiach had indeed arrived. And so, they trusted Shabsai Tzvi’s words, despite a few warning signs that something was not quite right: Shabsai Tzvi did certain actions publicly that were against halacha: he said the Shem Hameforash2 out loud; he ate cheilev3; and he ate and drank on fast days. He excused it all by saying that though these things were forbidden by the revealed halacha, they were permitted according to the secrets of kabbalah. Some rabbanim sent out letters as soon as they noticed these things, warning people that this Shabsai Tzvi was not the real Moshiach. But unfortunately, many weren’t aware of—or didn’t pay attention to—these issues.

Shabsai Tzvi continued to travel around and spread word that he was Moshiach, until reaching the Middle East along with his assistant, Nassan of Gaza. When he arrived in Constantinople, in the year 5426 (1666), he was arrested by the Sultan for the crime of trying to overthrow the crown. The Sultan gave Shabsai Tzvi two choices: either convert to Islam, or be put to death for treason. Shabsai Tzvi chose to convert, rachmana litzlan. It was this action that proved once and for all that Shabsai Tzvi was not Moshiach.

The whole saga of Shabsai Tzvi had devastating effects for Jews all over Europe. It shattered countless hopes and dreams, and weakened the emunah of many. Some Jews, unfortunately, continued to believe in Shabsai Tzvi. And

the story of Shabsai Tzvi proved that teaching kabbalah was just too dangerous. If kabbalah had led to the Shabsai Tzvi tragedy, who knew what else it could lead to?
