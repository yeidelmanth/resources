Finally, early the next morning, he opened the Ohel app.

He wrote about his accident, and how the doctors said he needed emergency surgery. He ended with a request: Please give me a bracha that Hashem should help me heal.

A few hours later, a different surgeon came into the room. “Why did the other doctors say that you need surgery?” he asked Asaf. “I looked at your scan, and though your spleen was bleeding a few days ago, it’s not bleeding now at all!”

Asaf was shocked. “But last night, three doctors said I needed surgery! How could they all be wrong?”

The surgeon shrugged. “I don’t know. This morning, I checked the scan with another surgeon and a doctor, and we all agree that your spleen is fine! But just to be sure, I’ll schedule you for a checkup next week.”

The surgeon smiled. “That’s from your broken ribs. I’ll prescribe some medicine for you.”

That same day, Asaf returned home. At the following checkup, they confirmed that everything was healthy!

Two different teams of doctors had looked at the same scan. The first group saw that Asaf needed emergency surgery, while the second group saw that he was completely fine.

Asaf understood what had happened: The Rebbe’s bracha had helped in a miraculous way!

Sometimes, we face challeges that make us feel lost and afraid. But when we turn to the Rebbe, miracles can happen!

Lying in his hospital bed, Asaf typed a letter to the Rebbe. Then, the surgeon came in with surprising news.

Tatty has a friend named Asaf. When Asaf was a teenager, his father was invited to join a farbrengen with the Rebbe. Asaf went, too. Ever since then, he has stayed connected and writes to the Rebbe often, asking for brachos and guidance with the Ohel app.

He was driving through the streets of Alaska, where he lives, when suddenly—crash! Another car sped through the red light and slammed right into him.

Asaf was rushed to the hospital. He had six broken ribs and a cut on his spleen. (The spleen is a small organ inside the body that fights germs to keep us healthy.)

Asaf stayed in the hospital for four days. The doctors then told him, “You’re getting better. You can go home and rest.”

But a few days later, Asaf felt terrible pain in his back. It hurt so much that he rushed right back to the hospital. When the doctors took a new scan, the results didn’t look good.

“Your spleen is bleeding,” a surgeon told Asaf. “You need surgery right away. If we can’t stop the bleeding, we might have to remove your spleen altogether.”

Asaf was shocked. “Are you sure? When I left the hospital last time, they told me it was fine.”

The doctor explained, “The spleen is very delicate. Even a small movement could have opened the cut again. That’s why you’re in so much pain.”

Two more doctors looked at the scan and agreed. The surgery was scheduled for the next morning.

Asaf felt very worried. Then his phone rang— it was Tatty.

Tatty had heard about Asaf’s accident and called to see how he was doing. Asaf told him everything, and added that he was nervous about the surgery.

“Maybe write to the Rebbe and ask for a bracha,” Tatty suggested.
